  1. STEP 1 · START HERE

    Signals to System Intelligence: How Platform Teams Move from Observability to Intelligent Operations

    Murtuza Mukadam

    Why this stop

    0:08 Mukadam uses a 2 a.m. outage to illustrate how alerts, dashboards, and AI assessments can leave responders with plenty of tabs but no coordinated next step.

    More moments to jump to

    1:27 He contrasts the teams that own cost, performance, and security signals with the customer who experiences the service as a whole.

    2:25 He proposes connecting existing tools into shared context so teams can start by deciding what to do, rather than spending the first part of an incident collecting evidence.

    3:08 He warns that automation without enough context creates risk, and argues for giving people control over how far the automation can go.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  2. STEP 2 · NEXT

    OTel Blueprints: Prescriptive Patterns for Enterprise Observability

    Dan Gomez Blanco, Lukasz Ciukaj

    Why this stop

    8:27 The speakers describe a blueprint as a living document that makes scoped recommendations for a particular environment instead of listing every possible configuration.

    More moments to jump to

    1:28 The speakers say teams adopting OpenTelemetry in isolation can lose context between services and between applications and infrastructure.

    18:18 For a shared Kubernetes telemetry platform, the speakers suggest that platform teams own defaults and managed gateways while product teams own and extend their signals.

    20:07 For Kubernetes cluster observability, the speakers recommend native receivers, shared semantic conventions, and using the OpenTelemetry Operator for consistent collection.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  3. STEP 3 · NEXT

    Filter, Transform, Route: Mastering OTTL in the OpenTelemetry Collector

    Evan Bradley, Edmo Vamerlatti Costa, Jamie Danielson, Tyler Helmuth

    Why this stop

    30:16 The presenters use AI-related spans to show why processing order matters when some spans need a separate destination but the full trace also needs to be preserved.

    More moments to jump to

    4:14 The presenters explain why the Collector moved from component-specific conditions to a shared transformation language.

    12:53 The presenters compare three ways to debug a statement: inspecting emitted telemetry, tracing individual evaluations, and testing in the OTTL playground.

    37:01 The presenters use the OpenTelemetry Collector Builder to scaffold a custom Collector and explain where a custom transform function fits.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  4. STEP 4 · GO DEEPER

    Zero-Trust Telemetry: Stopping AI-Powered Poisoning Attacks on Observability Pipelines

    Chad M. Crowell, Diana Todea

    Why this stop

    13:37 The speakers describe using mutual TLS between OpenTelemetry Collectors and backends, and explain that verifying the sender does not verify that the data is true.

    More moments to jump to

    16:43 They propose checking the identity and scope of human and agent queries with fine-grained role-based access, then logging those decisions for audit.

    20:02 They recommend default-deny network policies for the telemetry namespace, with explicit allowances for the application-to-collector and collector-to-backend flows.

    22:42 They describe using Falco with Kubernetes audit events to detect suspicious activity, complementing the communication limits from network policy.

    All five moments and talk notes →
