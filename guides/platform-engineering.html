  1. STEP 1 · START HERE

    The Easiest Path Wins: Earning Platform Adoption with DDD and Product Thinking

    Fabrizio Lazzaretti

    Why this stop

    1:41 Lazzaretti says mandates push a platform to serve everybody and turn the goal into registering more users instead of checking whether they are satisfied.

    More moments to jump to

    3:23 For the why, he suggests a product canvas over a long business plan: the label pitch, goals, key metrics, a specific target user group (never everybody) and how the platform is funded.

    7:37 He brings in domain-driven design: domain storytelling with a business expert to agree a shared language, then event storming to find bounded contexts and where the software can be split.

    14:29 On the human side he follows Kotter's eight steps for change, including a guiding coalition with a management sponsor and short-term wins from a lighthouse project.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  2. STEP 2 · NEXT

    The CNCF Platform Engineering User Group & Platform Factors

    Colin Griffin, Avinash Sabat

    Why this stop

    13:27 Sabat distinguishes platform operators who own shared infrastructure and governance, specialist teams that produce capabilities, and consumers who request them through self-service.

    More moments to jump to

    8:30 Griffin says teams should use the maturity model to decide where investment makes sense rather than trying to reach its optimizing column in every area.

    17:13 Sabat says a self-service database should deliver a complete outcome, including storage and backups, with clear responsibilities and no need to contact the database team for every request.

    19:26 Sabat says a capability should reconcile repeated requests safely without drifting, and that both producers and consumers should be able to see how it is performing.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  3. STEP 3 · NEXT

    Making Environments Repeatable: From Tools to Platform Capabilities

    Saiyam Pathak

    Why this stop

    1:48 Saiyam says reusable environment automation has to encode ordered setup steps, pass one step's output into the next step, and prove readiness from the developer's perspective.

    More moments to jump to

    0:13 Saiyam says the simple request for an environment hides installation, service trust, configuration handoff, and readiness work that platform teams must complete first.

    3:09 Saiyam uses a CUDA GPU stack to argue that readiness requires scheduler visibility of the GPU and a successful smoke-test job, not only healthy operator components.

    4:21 Saiyam closes by asking platform teams what belongs in a reusable stack, what proves an environment ready, and which repeated requests the platform should handle for users.

    All five moments and talk notes →

  4. STEP 4 · GO DEEPER

    Platform as a Product in a 300-Year-Old Bank

    Abby Bangser, Joel King

    Why this stop

    11:13 Joel explains that Kratix provides the product API through Kubernetes CRDs, after which pipelines write validated desired state to Git for downstream reconciliation.

    More moments to jump to

    3:43 Joel says reuse failed when each application team copied and modified patterns, producing more than 135 variants, high cognitive load, configuration drift, and an eight-to-twelve-week path to a usable EKS cluster.

    8:34 Joel says NatWest adopted platform as a product by exposing curated products through an abstracted API, using Git as the source of truth, reconciliation loops, and fleet management rather than team-by-team manual change.

    19:55 Joel says the automated EKS product reduced provisioning from eight to twelve weeks to 35 minutes while including the AWS account, networking connectivity, guardrails, and self-service request path.
