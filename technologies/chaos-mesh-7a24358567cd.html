    CNCF Bingo: Play, Learn, Win!!!!!!

    Michael Rishi Forrester

    Michael Forrester runs a live bingo game he calls unofficial, in which the audience marks CNCF projects on a phone card as he reads three clues per project, from broad to narrow. He works through about fifteen projects, from Kubeflow to Knative, until three attendees win, and points to flashcards he wrote covering every CNCF project.

    12 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: You are new to the CNCF landscape and want a quick, game-style pass over what about fifteen well-known projects do.

    1. 0:13 The speaker explains the game: scan a QR code for a bingo card of CNCF projects with Kubernetes as the free middle square, and long-press a square to read what the project does.
    2. 2:17 He runs a practice round with three clues that lead to Kubeflow: a machine learning platform with notebooks and pipelines, started at Google, used for MLOps on Kubernetes.
    3. 4:15 He gives clues for Open Policy Agent: a general-purpose engine that answers allow or deny, run by Gatekeeper for Kubernetes admission, with rules written in Rego.
    4. 9:20 The first attendee gets bingo after the Harbor clues, which describe a private registry that scans images for vulnerabilities.
    5. 10:50 He says the same site has flashcards for every CNCF project, and that he had to look up about a third of the projects when writing them.

    The Most Boring Talk at KubeCon - And the One That Will Save Your Job

    Lev Andelman, Regina Voloshin, Cortney Nickerson, Julie Gunderson

    In this tutorial, Lev Andelman, Regina Voloshin, Cortney Nickerson and Julie Gunderson argue that internal developer platforms need disaster recovery planning like production systems, and introduce hands-on GitHub Codespaces labs that break and recover Backstage, Argo CD and Kyverno. The recording covers the introductions and closing advice on ownership, communication, change freezes and chaos engineering, while the lab time itself is not shown in detail.

    80 min slot · Advanced · Tutorial

    Observability and reliabilityPlatform engineering and developer experienceDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you run Backstage, Argo CD or Kyverno and have never tested recovering them.

    1. 4:07 Lev asks the room who has a portal, GitOps, policy as code and an RTO and RPO for their platform, then who has ever tested recovering it, and finds one person.
    2. 7:22 Lev says that when Backstage's Postgres database dies, some data can be repopulated from configuration but other data is lost for good, and asks what happens when version control, authentication or object storage is unavailable.
    3. 12:11 Regina calls creating Argo CD applications directly with the CLI or kubectl an anti-pattern, because losing the Argo CD cluster then loses the declarative source of truth.
    4. 15:29 Regina recommends ApplicationSets, Argo CD managing its own configuration, one bootstrap app, and regular disaster recovery drills on a non-production cluster.
    5. 21:40 Cortney says a Kyverno failure hurts because it sits on the path of every change, can fail in two directions, and rarely looks like a Kyverno problem.
