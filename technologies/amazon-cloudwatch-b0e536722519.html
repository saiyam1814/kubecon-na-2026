    1000 Clusters, 1 Smarter Brain: The Next Evolution of AIOps at Salesforce

    Anubhav Dhoot, Uttam Kumar

    The speakers say the accuracy of their incident troubleshooting agents fell as they added more data, and trace that to knowledge retrieval, evaluation, and agent design. They describe curated runbooks with hierarchical search, an LLM-as-judge pipeline calibrated with sampled on-call feedback, and a planner and critic design backed by a dependency knowledge graph, and report their own results.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Observability and reliabilityAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you are building incident triage agents for a large Kubernetes fleet and want one team's account of why more data made theirs worse.

    1. 4:28 The speaker says accuracy dropped to 25% as more data was added, and names three bottlenecks: knowledge management, evaluation, and agent design.
    2. 6:17 The speaker describes a similarity trap where long Slack threads full of repeated error codes outranked the runbook holding the fix, even with a lower weight.
    3. 12:25 The speaker says they use Cochran's formula to ask on-call engineers for feedback on a sample, citing 88 samples per 1,000 transactions, and use it to calibrate the automated evals.
    4. 16:56 The speaker describes the new design: a planner agent forms several hypotheses from the knowledge graph, isolated sub-agents investigate them in parallel, and a critic agent validates the result.
    5. 22:02 The speaker reports a 60% improvement in time to troubleshoot, close to 900 hours saved, and 60 to 80% accuracy on their eval pipeline.

    Achieving Cross-Cloud Portability with the Kubernetes Resource Orchestrator

    Michael McCune, Daniel Bruno, Sitesh Pattanaik, Bridget Kromhout, Jesse Butler

    In this SIG Cloud Provider session, the chairs explain why the SIG took in kro as a subproject, and Jesse Butler lays out the problem of ordering, reconciling and exposing cloud resources as platform APIs. Daniel Bruno and Sitesh Pattanaik then walk through an example generative AI service API built as a ResourceGraphDefinition and show recorded demos of it deployed to GKE, AKS and EKS clusters.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you build a platform across more than one cloud and want to see what a single kro-defined API looks like from both the platform team's and the product team's side.

    1. 2:41 Bridget Kromhout says Amazon, Google, Microsoft and others wanted a consistent way to work with resources, and recalls Jesse Butler suggesting they skip 18 months of each doing it their own way.
    2. 10:01 Jesse Butler describes kro as a vendor-neutral composition layer over any resource in the cluster, naming Crossplane, ACK, KCC, ASO or controllers you built yourself.
    3. 15:19 Daniel Bruno shows what the product team writes: nine lines of YAML naming the instance, the model, the replica count and whether monitoring is on, deployed to clusters on several clouds.
    4. 17:01 Daniel Bruno shows the platform team's side: a ResourceGraphDefinition with CEL expressions for defaults and includeWhen conditions, written without Go controllers, with cloud-specific details read from ConfigMaps.
    5. 27:41 Sitesh Pattanaik explains custom status conditions written in CEL, such as a model cache bound condition, and later says kro emits a duration metric for how long a condition has held.

    Platform as a Product in a 300-Year-Old Bank

    Abby Bangser, Joel King

    Abby frames platform engineering as a shift toward self-service with guardrails and connects it to the CNCF white papers, maturity model, and an emerging marketplace model. Joel describes how NatWest used product thinking, Kubernetes CRDs, GitOps reconciliation, and clear ownership boundaries to cut EKS cluster provisioning from eight to twelve weeks to 35 minutes.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want a regulated-enterprise case study connecting platform-as-product theory to Kubernetes and GitOps implementation plus measured outcomes.

    1. 3:43 Joel says reuse failed when each application team copied and modified patterns, producing more than 135 variants, high cognitive load, configuration drift, and an eight-to-twelve-week path to a usable EKS cluster.
    2. 8:34 Joel says NatWest adopted platform as a product by exposing curated products through an abstracted API, using Git as the source of truth, reconciliation loops, and fleet management rather than team-by-team manual change.
    3. 11:13 Joel explains that Kratix provides the product API through Kubernetes CRDs, after which pipelines write validated desired state to Git for downstream reconciliation.
    4. 19:55 Joel says the automated EKS product reduced provisioning from eight to twelve weeks to 35 minutes while including the AWS account, networking connectivity, guardrails, and self-service request path.
    5. 23:30 Abby closes by arguing that platform teams must optimize for outcomes over outputs, composing contributions into products that give users self-service access rather than merely automating infrastructure.
