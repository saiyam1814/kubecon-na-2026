    Scaling AI Model Distribution with Dragonfly: From HuggingFace to Production GPU Clusters

    Pavan Madduri

    Pavan Madduri, a Dragonfly contributor presenting alone because co-speaker Wenbo Qi could not travel, argues that pulling model weights of hundreds of gigabytes onto every GPU node at once is an infrastructure problem and explains how Dragonfly's peer-to-peer distribution and preheating address it. He then walks through the two pieces he says he contributed, the Hugging Face and ModelScope backends and the dragonfly-injector webhook, plus a cross-cloud pattern and the project roadmap.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Data, storage and stateAI inference and serving

    Watch this if: large model downloads are slowing your GPU nodes or raising your egress bill and you want to see how Dragonfly handles it.

    1. 4:24 Pavan Madduri describes the thundering herd he sees with model weights: a scale-out sends 100 GPU nodes to the same registry at once, the origin saturates and downloads time out and retry.
    2. 10:04 He cites a test of a 130 GB model on 200 nodes where only the seed peer pulled from the origin, which he puts at about 99% less egress than 26 TB, and adds that the number depends on the setup.
    3. 13:01 He shows the Hugging Face and ModelScope backends: dfget with an hf scheme makes the seed peer fetch the model from the hub once and other nodes get the pieces from peers.
    4. 16:31 He explains the dragonfly-injector: a mutating admission webhook adds the Dragonfly client and daemon socket to pods carrying an inject annotation, so teams change Helm values instead of application code.
    5. 23:06 He lists the roadmap he took from version 2.6: a Python SDK after the Rust and Go ones, RDMA-based distribution between peers, and bandwidth negotiation across parent peers.
