    Clueless About The Cost of AI Model Usage? OpenCost+llm-d = first of a kind inference cost tracking

    Alexander Meijer

    Alexander Meijer, presenting alone because Sima Nadler could not attend, describes an integration where OpenCost reads llm-d and vLLM token metrics and combines them with GPU and provider pricing to report inference costs such as cost per token. He demos the APIs and dashboard live against an IBM Research cluster, comparing allocation and usage costs to find idle models, and outlines a roadmap for deeper GPU metrics.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and servingObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you self-host LLMs on Kubernetes and want to know what each model and token actually costs.

    1. 1:46 Alexander Meijer says self-hosted models are generally cheaper than hosted ones, but that this only delays the cost problem.
    2. 6:40 He draws the line of responsibility: llm-d handles token counts, routing and scheduling, while OpenCost handles hardware metrics, pricing and cloud provider connections.
    3. 12:27 He explains the two cost bases: allocation, the max of request and usage, which he calls the production metric, and usage, which he calls the R&D metric.
    4. 16:41 His worked example divides a $15 deployment cost by 150 processed tokens to get $0.10 per token.
    5. 20:54 In the live demo, comparing $246 allocation cost with $22 usage cost shows one model was idle about 90 percent of the time, which he flags as a right-sizing target.
