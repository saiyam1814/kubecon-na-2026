    Code, Containers, and Clusters: The Next Chapter of Apple's Container Framework

    Kathryn Baldauf, Vince Prignano

    Kathryn Baldauf and Vince Prignano of Apple walk through Apple container, which runs each Linux container in its own lightweight VM on a Mac, plus container machines for persistent Linux dev environments. They announce container cluster for local multi-node Kubernetes clusters and demo building a controller and deploying it to a three-node cluster with Cilium.

    10 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Kubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you develop for Kubernetes on a Mac and want a local multi-node cluster that uses your locally built images without pushing them.

    1. 1:12 Vince Prignano says Apple bootstraps every cluster declaratively with Kubernetes Cluster API and ships a signed, continuously enforced bill of materials, and asks what it would take to bring that same bill of materials to the MacBook.
    2. 1:59 Kathryn Baldauf says Apple container, written in Swift for Apple silicon, runs each Linux container in its own lightweight VM, shares only the files you choose, and boots in under a second.
    3. 2:48 She introduces container machines: persistent Linux environments built from OCI images that share your Mac user and home folder, which she pitches as a good place for agent sessions.
    4. 4:06 She announces container cluster, which creates a real Kubernetes cluster bootstrapped by kubeadm, with each node as its own VM and a default CNI or one you bring.
    5. 6:53 Demo: Vince builds a controller image, creates a three-worker cluster with Cilium and the shared local image store, deploys with kubectl, then fixes a blocked app with a Cilium network policy.

    Data-Driven Platform Guardrails: What 16,500 Incidents Tell You to Automate First

    Henrique Santana, Henrique Dalssaso

    Henrique Santana and Henrique Dalssaso walk through the top root causes they found in more than 16,000 Kubernetes incidents, including IP exhaustion, auth failures and node provisioning problems. For each one they suggest platform guardrails to automate, and they explain how to run the same analysis on your own incident data.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Observability and reliabilityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want a list of common Kubernetes incident root causes and the guardrails these speakers suggest automating for each.

    1. 1:35 The speakers describe their dataset as more than 16,000 incidents across many Kubernetes clusters from January to mid-May, grouped with AI and then reviewed by hand. They say DNS did not make their top 10 root causes.
    2. 3:57 They say about a third of one top pattern was pods with no IP address available, from small or shared subnets, and suggest Cilium multi-pool IPAM and broader Karpenter node pools.
    3. 6:59 For auth failures they separate missing permissions, RBAC that was never bound to the pod, and policies on the target endpoint, and suggest Kyverno policies, projected service account tokens and cert-manager.
    4. 16:20 For node provisioning failures, which they put at almost 15% of incidents, they suggest setting kube-reserved and system-reserved, Cluster API machine health checks and Karpenter node auto repair.
    5. 20:59 To repeat the analysis on your own data, they suggest classifying by what actually failed rather than the symptom, ranking by frequency, and asking whether the platform, the app team or both can fix it.

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: You want a practical way to trace a pod startup networking failure from its sandbox to its first outbound packet.

    1. 6:26 Seetharaman describes when the runtime creates the pause container and network namespace after the kubelet receives a pod.
    2. 8:06 Seetharaman uses crictl to identify a running pod's sandbox and explains why the pause container is not listed like an application container.
    3. 13:13 Seetharaman distinguishes the loopback setup from the primary CNI plugin and identifies kindnet as the plugin used in her demo.
    4. 20:11 Seetharaman explains how kindnet uses host-local IP address management to allocate a pod address from a configured range.
    5. 24:09 Seetharaman clears the ARP cache and captures traffic during a ping to show the address resolution and packets leaving the pod.

    Here be DRAgons: Bringing native zero-copy networking to Pods with Cilium and netkit

    Daniel Borkmann, Aaron Campbell

    The speakers compare two Cilium network drivers for Kubernetes Dynamic Resource Allocation, one using SR-IOV and the other using netkit with queue leasing. They demonstrate how a pod can use a leased hardware receive queue and explain how zero-copy receive works with the kernel networking stack.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: you are evaluating hardware-accelerated pod networking and want to understand what Cilium queue leasing requires.

    1. 8:37 Borkmann explains how running BPF in the netkit device driver reduces the queue traversals seen with virtual Ethernet devices.
    2. 14:33 He introduces Cilium's two DRA driver options, SR-IOV and netkit queue leasing, and describes how the agent integrates with the node.
    3. 19:54 He explains how Linux queue leasing removes selected receive queues from the default set and binds them to a netkit device in a pod.
    4. 22:13 Campbell begins the demo by showing the operator's view of queue leasing and identifying the required hardware receive-queue capabilities.
    5. 30:22 He explains how packet headers stay in kernel memory while io_uring enables payload delivery directly to user space.

    Platform-First AI Agent Security: Zero-Trust Patterns for Kubernetes

    Carlos Santana

    Carlos Santana argues that a Kubernetes platform should treat agent-generated code as untrusted and provision isolation, credentials, and network policy separately. He describes using kro to compose those controls behind a platform API and shows captures of an Agent Sandbox warm pool assigning a ready pod.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you need to give teams a Kubernetes API for agents without giving those agents unrestricted access.

    1. 5:25 Santana describes SPIFFE and SPIRE as a way to identify an agent by URI and associate that identity with a credential across different environments.
    2. 12:14 Santana shows a kro ResourceGraphDefinition and explains how a declarative resource graph can give platform users a custom API without requiring the platform team to write a Go controller.
    3. 19:05 Santana compares gVisor, Kata Containers, and AWS Nitro Sandboxes as different isolation choices for running untrusted agent code.
    4. 21:27 Santana shows captures of a two-pod Agent Sandbox warm pool and says applying a claim assigned a ready pod instead of waiting for a fresh sandbox to start.
    5. 25:48 Santana describes a credential proxy that attaches a credential to an outbound request without handing the underlying secret to the agent, and gives an Agent Substrate example.

    Production AI on Kubernetes: Built to Scale with Oracle Cloud

    Lorenzo Biava, Jeff Hoffman

    Lorenzo Biava of LiveKit uses an earthquake scenario to show what real-time voice AI at scale demands and how LiveKit prepares its Kubernetes platform for data center and network failures. Jeff Hoffman of Oracle then describes how OKE and OCI cover reach, networking, compute and portable managed Kubernetes for workloads like LiveKit's.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI inference and servingAccelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run real-time voice or video AI on Kubernetes and want to hear how LiveKit plans for data center and network failures.

    1. 0:30 Lorenzo Biava walks through an earthquake scenario where AI agents answer every emergency call while drones and robots search, which he puts at well over 100,000 real-time sessions on a half-second response budget.
    2. 2:25 He says over a million developers build on LiveKit's open source framework, and its hosted platform runs on 50,000 cores across 30+ Kubernetes clusters, mostly on Oracle, moving 120 petabytes of data.
    3. 3:27 He describes how they prepare: pre-warmed agents, share-nothing sites so losing one reroutes traffic, a real-time overlay that routes around bad links, and calls that move off a dying server, with live agent migration in progress.
    4. 4:24 Jeff Hoffman says OKE runs in 50+ OCI regions across 28 countries with one cluster spec, plus flat-priced networking with your own IP ranges and shapes up to bare metal and GPUs.
    5. 5:10 He says OKE is conformant and open to custom node images for tools like BuildKit and Kata, and that one in five CNCF projects, including Kubernetes, Cilium and OpenTelemetry, run tests, builds or live environments on OCI.

    The Missing Layer of Platform Maturity: Kubernetes Networking

    Faseela Kundattil, Christophe Sauthier, Chad M. Crowell, Marino Wijay, James Spurin

    The panel explains why Kubernetes networking remains a maturity gap despite higher-level abstractions, using incidents and observability workflows to show why engineers still need mental models of the underlying stack. The panelists describe the hands-on CKNE exam as a vendor-flexible way to validate networking skills while arguing that AI should assist rather than replace engineering judgment.

    35 min slot · Any Level · Panel

    Networking and connectivityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: your platform team owns Kubernetes networking troubleshooting or is evaluating the CKNE curriculum.

    1. 1:41 The panel argues that Kubernetes networking is a missing layer of platform maturity because usable abstractions still require underlying troubleshooting knowledge when failures occur.
    2. 10:08 Chad uses a 504 ingress incident to argue for peeling back the network path locally, from pod logs and events through service endpoints to ingress routing and host-header behavior.
    3. 15:21 James argues that engineers should learn enough iptables, nftables, conntrack, and eBPF to understand failures and judge AI-suggested fixes rather than treating AI as the authority.
    4. 17:35 Chad argues that knowing where in the networking stack a problem lives matters more than loyalty to a toolset, with observability used to narrow environment and namespace before deep packet inspection.
    5. 23:31 Christophe says SMEs from varied projects designed the hands-on CKNE exam so candidates can answer with different tools rather than being forced into one stack.
