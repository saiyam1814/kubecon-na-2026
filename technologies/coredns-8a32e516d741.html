    1000 Clusters, 1 Smarter Brain: The Next Evolution of AIOps at Salesforce

    Anubhav Dhoot, Uttam Kumar

    The speakers say the accuracy of their incident troubleshooting agents fell as they added more data, and trace that to knowledge retrieval, evaluation, and agent design. They describe curated runbooks with hierarchical search, an LLM-as-judge pipeline calibrated with sampled on-call feedback, and a planner and critic design backed by a dependency knowledge graph, and report their own results.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Observability and reliabilityAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you are building incident triage agents for a large Kubernetes fleet and want one team's account of why more data made theirs worse.

    1. 4:28 The speaker says accuracy dropped to 25% as more data was added, and names three bottlenecks: knowledge management, evaluation, and agent design.
    2. 6:17 The speaker describes a similarity trap where long Slack threads full of repeated error codes outranked the runbook holding the fix, even with a lower weight.
    3. 12:25 The speaker says they use Cochran's formula to ask on-call engineers for feedback on a sample, citing 88 samples per 1,000 transactions, and use it to calibrate the automated evals.
    4. 16:56 The speaker describes the new design: a planner agent forms several hypotheses from the knowledge graph, isolated sub-agents investigate them in parallel, and a critic agent validates the result.
    5. 22:02 The speaker reports a 60% improvement in time to troubleshoot, close to 900 hours saved, and 60 to 80% accuracy on their eval pipeline.

    From Cloud Outages to AI Security Challenges: Building Resilient DNS Infrastructure at Scale

    John Belamaric, Yong Tang

    CoreDNS maintainers John Belamaric and Yong Tang point to recent cloud outages tied to DNS and describe the project's recent work, which they say is mostly about operating CoreDNS more robustly in production. Tang then describes being flooded with AI-generated vulnerability reports and both ask reporters to send fixes as pull requests on a private fork.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Networking and connectivityObservability and reliabilitySecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you depend on CoreDNS and want the maintainers' view of recent resilience work and of how AI-generated security reports are straining a small volunteer team.

    1. 1:44 Belamaric recalls a large AWS outage that he says turned out to be a simple DNS problem, and calls DNS simple in some ways but an extremely high-risk place in the network.
    2. 8:28 He says recent CoreDNS changes are mostly operational, such as load shedding, better timeouts and serving stale answers past their TTL when the upstream is down.
    3. 10:36 He describes the multi-socket change using the kernel's reuse-port option, which he says lets CoreDNS scale linearly with CPUs so clusters can scale it vertically instead of by node count.
    4. 13:29 Tang recounts receiving two batches of 50 vulnerability reports within a month, plus more than 20 emails, and says volunteer maintainers cannot keep up.
    5. 21:45 Belamaric asks people who scan CoreDNS with AI tools to also provide a fix as a pull request on a private fork, not a file attached to an email.

    How Anthropic Almost Melted GKE (And the PRs That Saved It)

    Maciek Różacki, Jian Cheung

    Maciek Różacki and Jian Cheung walk through six incidents from about two years of running very large GKE clusters for Anthropic, covering API server overload, a stuck StatefulSet controller, and a restart storm. For each one they describe what they saw, the short-term mitigation, and the upstream Kubernetes change they say followed.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Observability and reliabilityKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you run large or high-churn clusters and want concrete failure stories about list requests, watch caches, startup probes and staged recovery.

    1. 3:26 Różacki describes how API Priority and Fairness underestimated the cost of large pod lists, leading to API server replicas running out of memory in a loop, and names the watch cache and list pricing changes that followed.
    2. 7:20 Cheung describes a per-node add-on whose consistent reads fell through to a full storage scan when one replica's watch cache lagged, and says a one-line change to read from the cache resolved it that night.
    3. 13:28 Cheung explains how a 404 on a pod delete made the StatefulSet controller abort its whole sync during scale-down, and advises operator authors to treat not-found on delete as success.
    4. 16:52 Cheung walks through the restart loop in which the 210-second startup probe budget the kubelet allowed was shorter than the roughly 276 seconds the CNI agent needed to become ready at that cluster size.
    5. 19:01 The speakers describe recovering by blocking node traffic at the firewall so the control plane could warm up, then letting nodes back in one address range at a time.

    k8gb Is Growing up: Incubating Global Load Balancing for Kubernetes

    Fabrizio Sgura

    Fabrizio Sgura gives an update on k8gb, the Kubernetes Global Balancer, which he says has moved from CNCF Sandbox to Incubating. He recaps its DNS-based design with no central management cluster, lists what changed on the way to incubation, and describes uses for multi-region AI inference endpoints and digital sovereignty.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Lightning Talk

    Networking and connectivityObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you run services in several clusters or regions and want a DNS-based way to fail over between them without a central control plane.

    1. 0:31 Fabrizio Sgura describes k8gb as global load balancing across Kubernetes clusters and regions using Kubernetes-native resources, with no central management cluster and each cluster making its own decisions.
    2. 1:09 He lists the traffic strategies: round robin, weighted round robin, failover and GeoIP routing, and notes that DNS changes only where new connections go.
    3. 1:48 He says three components work together: CoreDNS answers DNS queries, ExternalDNS automates DNS delegation, and the k8gb controller coordinates the strategy, with k8gb staying out of the data path.
    4. 3:28 He says that since Sandbox the project expanded Gateway API support, introduced a vendor-neutral API with a migration path, added zone delegation, moved to an OCI Helm registry and signs container images with Cosign.
    5. 4:11 He describes putting an OpenAI-compatible inference endpoint behind k8gb for regional failover, and a pairing with NVIDIA Dynamo where k8gb picks the healthy region and Dynamo picks the local worker.

    Managing Network Security in Multi-Tenant Environments with Istio and Cilium

    Michael Bolot, Zaira Shaikh

    Michael Bolot and Zaira Shaikh of GEICO describe replacing a mix of cloud firewalls, on-premises firewalls and in-cluster policies with one API that configures Istio and Cilium across clusters on a flat, routable pod IP space. They explain how SPIRE identities and Istio authorization policies decide which applications may talk, while a small set of Cilium policies blocks what bypasses the sidecar, and they close with tips for writing Cilium policies safely.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Security, identity and policyNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: you run Cilium and Istio together and want to hear how one platform team splits network policy duties between them.

    1. 1:44 Bolot lists the problems with the legacy setup: no unified definition of the policy affecting a device, manual configuration, inconsistent enforcement and a complex network topology.
    2. 7:03 Shaikh describes the new system: pods are restricted by Istio resources and network policies written by a data plane agent on every workload cluster, configured from a central API server, with Cilium's BGP features advertising all pod IPs.
    3. 15:03 Bolot says SPIRE handles authentication by issuing workloads a certificate after attestation, and Istio authorization policies state what a given SPIFFE ID may reach, with service entries and workload entries connecting clusters.
    4. 19:55 Bolot gives three reasons they do not use Cilium for everything: they want authorization based on SPIFFE IDs, user-chosen labels would exhaust Cilium's identity map, and Cilium's layer 7 Envoy would duplicate the one Istio already runs.
    5. 25:29 Shaikh shares tips for Cilium policies, including using audit mode and Hubble, minding the default enforcement mode, and never cutting the operator or etcd off from the API server.

    SIG Network Intro and Updates

    Surya Seetharaman, Guilherme Cassolato, Austin Abro, Bowei Du

    Four SIG Network contributors review what the SIG owns and what changed in 2026 across Kubernetes core and its subprojects, including kube-proxy's planned move to nftables as the default mode, Gateway API releases, Cluster Network Policy reaching v1beta1 and the status of smaller subprojects. They also report findings from the SIG's first structured survey of its 22 subprojects, and Austin Abro describes how he used AI to make his first contribution.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Networking and connectivityKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you want a year's worth of Kubernetes networking changes in one session, or you run kube-proxy and need to plan for nftables.

    1. 3:40 Bowei Du mentions a new proposal for network-isolated pods with no networking by default, which he links to agent sandbox use cases, and lists deprecations of external IPs, kube-proxy IPVS mode and the Endpoints API.
    2. 4:45 Austin Abro explains the opt-in TCP userspace proxy that lets nftables mode serve NodePorts on localhost, and advises setting the kube-proxy backend explicitly before the default changes to nftables in 1.40.
    3. 9:57 Surya Seetharaman announces the v1beta1 release of Cluster Network Policy, which unifies the former admin and baseline CRDs, and notes the work began in March 2020.
    4. 16:11 Guilherme Cassolato says the SIG ran its first structured survey of its 22 subprojects and learned that AI is driving many agendas to the point of overlap, some projects have only one or two maintainers, and maintainers want faster API review.
    5. 20:47 Abro recounts using AI to learn kube-proxy internals for his localhost NodePort contribution, and says the maintainers' insistence on not breaking existing users kept the feature opt-in.
