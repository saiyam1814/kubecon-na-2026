    Explore TAG Workloads Foundation: Core Runtime, Batch Scheduling, and Moar

    Stephen Rust, Yuan Tang, Marlow Warnicke, Rajas Kakodkar, Paco Xu

    Five leads of the CNCF TAG Workloads Foundation explain what a technical advisory group does, what this one covers, and how its batch subproject and white paper initiatives run. They then walk through graduated, incubating and sandbox projects they place in the TAG's scope and describe how newcomers can join meetings, reviews and initiatives.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoptionKubernetes internals and runtimesAccelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you maintain or use a workload, runtime or scheduling project and want to know what the TAG does and how to take part.

    1. 2:10 Stephen Rust describes a TAG as a technical advisory group that supports CNCF projects and says it is a way to scale advice across many projects with few people.
    2. 5:13 Rust says initiatives are time-bound efforts with a deliverable such as a white paper, while subprojects carry longer-term work, and that the TAG helps projects with their maturity reviews.
    3. 10:23 Marlow Warnicke describes the batch subproject's scheduling capabilities white paper, which she says aims to compare schedulers under different workload mixes, and asks for contributors.
    4. 14:15 Paco Xu lists graduated projects he places in the TAG's scope and says Karmada and Kubeflow graduated this year and KubeVirt graduated recently.
    5. 19:07 Rajas Kakodkar says no single role or expertise is needed to get involved, and suggests joining meetings, sharing pain points, demoing a project or reviewing white papers.

    Kubernetes Anonymous: Confessions of Kubernetes Cargo Cultists

    Taylor Thomas, Brooks Townsend

    The speakers, who say they have worked both on Kubernetes-heavy platforms and on a wasmCloud platform that avoided Kubernetes, argue that both extremes are traps. They list red flags for using Kubernetes where it fits poorly, describe what each extreme cost them, and suggest that CNCF projects be compatible with Kubernetes without depending on it.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You are deciding how much of your platform or project should be built on Kubernetes APIs and want the tradeoffs from people who tried both extremes.

    1. 1:43 The speakers poll the room on red and yellow flags, such as using Kubernetes as the database for config objects, making every service a CRD and mapping an API permission model onto Kubernetes RBAC.
    2. 10:58 Taylor Thomas says avoiding Kubernetes on principle for their wasmCloud platform was their own yellow flag, and that it cost them adoption and integrations with the rest of the ecosystem.
    3. 15:27 Describing Kargo, Thomas says Kubernetes became its database, API and auth model, and that most of its high and critical CVEs this year came from contorting the access model to fit Kubernetes RBAC.
    4. 17:29 He says Kargo 2.0 moves promotion and artifact tracking to a real database while Kubernetes stays as the execution substrate that runs pods.
    5. 20:21 Thomas asks people to stop putting everything in a CRD and offers test questions, including whether the work depends on a Kubernetes feature and whether it would be valuable outside Kubernetes.

    Sandboxing on Kubernetes with WebAssembly: A Hands-on Tutorial

    Eric Gregory, Bailey Hayes

    Eric Gregory and Bailey Hayes use wasmCloud and Kubernetes to show how WebAssembly components get explicit permissions for the resources they need. In the workshop, they start with an agent that has no tools and add capabilities one at a time, then show a separate scaling benchmark.

    80 min slot · Beginner · Tutorial

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: You want to see a WebAssembly sandbox on Kubernetes and how to grant an agent tool only the access it needs.

    1. 6:18 The speakers explain the deny-by-default model and show where the wasmCloud operator schedules a workload onto a host pod.
    2. 23:14 The speakers introduce the workshop's scripted model and explain why it makes the effect of sandbox permissions easier to distinguish from model behavior.
    3. 37:40 The speakers build an optional Rust component and inspect the WebAssembly interfaces it imports for HTTP and filesystem access.
    4. 56:36 The speakers demonstrate a narrow GitHub integration for triaging pull requests and explain how the host plugin keeps the sign-in token out of the workload.
    5. 1:03:56 The speakers switch from the single-instance workshop to a recorded density test and explain the resource constraints they encountered while scaling on Kubernetes.
