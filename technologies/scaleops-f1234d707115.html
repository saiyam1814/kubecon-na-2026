    Autonomous Orchestration for AI Infrastructure

    Yodar Shafrir

    Yodar Shafrir of ScaleOps argues that AI agents waste resources because they idle and then spike, and says they fail far more often than traditional microservices. He pitches ScaleOps's autonomous resource orchestration and a new capability he says lends agents' idle resources to other workloads.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationAccelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run agent workloads on Kubernetes and want a vendor's view of why they waste resources and crash.

    1. 0:15 Yodar Shafrir lists four problems he sees in AI infrastructure in the agent era: high compute cost, high token cost, agent reliability, and scarce, expensive GPUs.
    2. 0:46 He says agents sit idle, then spike CPU when the LLM tells them what to run, so resources sized for the peak are wasted most of the time and memory is held the whole time.
    3. 1:39 He says ScaleOps sees agents fail 80% more often than traditional microservices, and that a crash means rerunning the whole step and paying for its tokens again.
    4. 4:04 He pitches ScaleOps as autonomous resource orchestration for every workload and claims it cuts infrastructure cost by 70 to 80%.
    5. 5:09 He announces a new ScaleOps capability that he says makes agents 90% more cost-efficient by lending their idle CPU and freed memory to other workloads.
