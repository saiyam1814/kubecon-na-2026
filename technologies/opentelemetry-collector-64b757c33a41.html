    Hope Is Not a Rollout Strategy: Balancing (AI) Quality, Cost, and Risk Through Experimentation

    Todd Baert

    Baert presents feature flags and telemetry as a way to manage AI rollouts without redeploying the application. He uses a calculator demo to show how he evaluates guardrails and model settings for different groups of users.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and servingDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you want to test changes to AI models or guardrails on a small group before rolling them out to everyone.

    1. 7:26 Baert shows an application asking OpenFeature for a model name with a fallback and context, while leaving targeting decisions outside the application code.
    2. 8:43 He shows how the OpenFeature Operator represents flags as Kubernetes custom resources so their values can change without an application redeploy.
    3. 9:54 He introduces an LLM-powered calculator so he can compare generated answers against a known correct result.
    4. 12:27 He enables a guardrail through a flag and uses the dashboard to watch what changes after the rollout.
    5. 18:54 He demonstrates assigning a zero-temperature variant to a quarter of trial users while the rest stay on the old setting.

    OTel Blueprints: Prescriptive Patterns for Enterprise Observability

    Dan Gomez Blanco, Lukasz Ciukaj

    Dan Gomez Blanco and Lukasz Ciukaj say adopting OpenTelemetry team by team can leave traces, metrics, logs, and profiles difficult to correlate. They introduce OpenTelemetry Blueprints as maintained guidance and walk through patterns for virtual machines, shared telemetry platforms, and Kubernetes clusters.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Observability and reliability

    Watch this if: You need OpenTelemetry defaults that teams can share without forcing every service to use an identical configuration.

    1. 1:28 The speakers say teams adopting OpenTelemetry in isolation can lose context between services and between applications and infrastructure.
    2. 8:27 The speakers describe a blueprint as a living document that makes scoped recommendations for a particular environment instead of listing every possible configuration.
    3. 16:48 For environments outside Kubernetes, the speakers recommend managing agents centrally, adding a collector gateway layer, and standardizing resource attributes.
    4. 18:18 For a shared Kubernetes telemetry platform, the speakers suggest that platform teams own defaults and managed gateways while product teams own and extend their signals.
    5. 20:07 For Kubernetes cluster observability, the speakers recommend native receivers, shared semantic conventions, and using the OpenTelemetry Operator for consistent collection.

    Sample Smarter, Not Harder (Or Not At All!)

    Reese Lee, Juliano Costa

    Reese Lee and Juliano Costa say sampling at the backend doesn't remove the overhead of generating telemetry in the application. Using the OpenTelemetry Demo, they compare disabling redundant instrumentation with probabilistic, policy-based tail, and adaptive sampling.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Observability and reliability

    Watch this if: You want to cut tracing overhead and keep visibility into slow, failing, or low-traffic requests.

    1. 8:58 The speakers show how they disabled overlapping HTTP instrumentation in the Node.js frontend so it did not generate spans they did not need.
    2. 11:49 The speakers set the collector to sample ten percent probabilistically and compare spans received by the collector with spans sent to Jaeger.
    3. 15:43 The speakers explain that tail sampling sends complete traces to the collector so it can apply policies before forwarding or dropping them.
    4. 16:49 The speakers show tail-sampling rules that keep errors and requests above a latency threshold rather than relying on a fixed fraction for all traces.
    5. 21:41 The speakers describe how adaptive sampling responds to changes in load so a rare checkout flow is more likely to remain visible during a traffic spike.
