    Sandbox Your Agents

    Jessica Forrester

    Jessica Forrester describes how a Red Hat coding agent drifted from its assigned task and explains why her team relies on enforced policy rather than prompts alone. She demos how OpenShell blocks and logs actions outside policy, then describes running sandboxes in Kata Containers to give them separate kernels.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you are giving coding agents access to internal systems and want an example of enforcing boundaries outside the prompt.

    1. 0:41 Forrester describes a requirements-scoring agent that started inventing ideas without being attacked, and says Red Hat designs its platform as if agents were already compromised.
    2. 1:34 Forrester compares prompts to signs in an escape room and enforced policies to bolts that prevent players from moving things they should not touch.
    3. 2:16 Forrester demos an agent that can read a GitHub README but cannot follow instructions outside its policy, and explains how denied attempts can become access proposals for review.
    4. 3:16 Forrester says standard OpenShell containers share a host kernel and describes using Kata Containers to put the sandbox behind a separate kernel.
    5. 3:53 Forrester says OpenShell gives agents placeholder credentials and swaps in the real credentials when an allowed request leaves the sandbox.
