    SIG Autoscaling Project Updates

    Jack Francis, Jason Deal, Heba Elayoty, Kuba Tużnik

    Jack Francis, Jason Deal, Heba Elayoty and Kuba Tużnik give updates on Kubernetes SIG Autoscaling projects, including Karpenter's DRA support, balanced consolidation and node repair, the capacity buffer and capacity quota APIs, and the split of Cluster Autoscaler into a core plus separate provider projects. Elayoty then explains what workload-aware scheduling needs from autoscalers, and Tużnik presents scheduler-driven autoscaling, a proposed redesign in which the scheduler requests nodes from the autoscaler.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you run Karpenter or Cluster Autoscaler and want the latest feature updates plus the proposed redesign of how autoscalers work with the scheduler.

    1. 3:28 Jason Deal says Karpenter added DRA support in its scheduling simulation in v1.14, covering structured parameters, consumable capacity, partitionable devices and prioritized alternatives, with provider implementations in the AWS provider and EKS Auto Mode.
    2. 6:11 He introduces balanced consolidation, which weighs the share of pods disrupted against the share of cost saved and only acts when the score passes a threshold.
    3. 10:08 Jack Francis says Cluster Autoscaler is being split into a core repository and separate provider projects, and that the SIG will keep supporting releases in the older all-in-one model for a transition period.
    4. 19:50 Kuba Tużnik describes the split brain problem: the autoscaler has to predict what the scheduler will decide, and when its predictions are off it makes wrong scaling decisions.
    5. 24:15 He outlines the proposed design in which the autoscaler publishes potential capacity and the scheduler issues node claims, and estimates it at more than ten KEPs over one to two years.

    Two Drivers, One Topology: GPU:NIC Rail Alignment for RDMA VMs with KubeVirt DRA

    Jian Li, Alay Patel, Kazuki Sato

    Kazuki Sato of NTT DOCOMO BUSINESS, Jian Li of SK Telecom and Alay Patel describe how a multi-site GPU platform on KubeVirt moved from a manual workaround to Dynamic Resource Allocation claims that pair each GPU with an RDMA NIC under the same PCIe root. They show a recorded benchmark comparing aligned and misaligned VMs, explain the Multus change they are proposing upstream, and outline what KubeVirt's DRA support covers today and what is planned.

    15 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: you run GPU virtual machines with RDMA networking on KubeVirt and need each GPU paired with the right NIC without pinning devices by hand.

    1. 2:07 Sato describes the earlier workaround: filling a node with placeholder pods that each hold one GPU or NIC and deleting only the ones holding the wanted pair, which had to be redone VM by VM after every node or VM restart.
    2. 5:10 Li explains that the GPU driver and the SR-IOV NIC driver both publish the standard PCI root attribute, so one resource claim with a GPU request, a VF request and a match constraint lets the scheduler allocate them as a pair.
    3. 7:15 He says upstream KubeVirt network DRA could not consume a VF wired through a Multus network, so they added a feature gate that takes the VF from the resource claim and leaves the existing CNI chain unchanged.
    4. 10:21 In the recorded demo Sato compares two four-GPU VMs: he reports about 200 Gbps of RDMA bandwidth on the misaligned VM and 391 Gbps on the aligned one, with placement as the only difference.
    5. 12:33 Li says the next gap is that CPUs and huge pages are still placed by the kubelet without topology hints from DRA devices, so a VM can get its GPU and NIC on one socket and its memory on another.

    Why Are You Still Wasting Whole GPUs? Hardware-Level Sharing, Scheduled with DRA

    Engin Diri

    Engin Diri of Pulumi recounts helping a European customer build a self-hosted inference platform, moving from whole GPUs and time slicing to NVIDIA MIG slices that pods request through Dynamic Resource Allocation. He shows a recorded demo of MIG profiles, resource slices and claims on an AWS node serving a model behind an inference gateway, and lists open questions such as quotas, models larger than a node and start-up ordering.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run inference on your own GPUs and want a practical walk through MIG slices requested with Dynamic Resource Allocation.

    1. 8:32 Diri says time slicing gives no memory or fault isolation between workloads, so the choice was between wasting GPUs and risking a noisy neighbor.
    2. 11:24 Diri says Dynamic Resource Allocation, which he dates to Kubernetes 1.34, lets a request describe device attributes instead of an integer count.
    3. 22:16 In the recorded demo, Diri lists the 1G, 2G and 4G MIG profiles on the node and the resource slices the DRA driver published.
    4. 24:28 Diri lists open day-two questions he is evaluating with the customer, including Kueue for MIG quota and which workload runs first.
    5. 28:06 Diri advises starting the DRA driver only after MIG is configured, because he ran into race conditions between the two.
