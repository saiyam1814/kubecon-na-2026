    Containerd: Project Update and Deep Dive

    Wei Fu, Samuel Karp

    Maintainers Samuel Karp and Wei Fu describe containerd's move to a four-month release cadence aligned with Kubernetes, a yearly long-term support release, and the features they shipped in 2.4 and propose for 2.5. Karp then explains how a surge of AI-assisted security reports in 2026 changed the project's triage and patching process.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you run containerd under Kubernetes and want to plan upgrades around the new release and LTS schedule, or you maintain a project facing a flood of AI-generated security reports.

    1. 1:16 Karp says containerd moved to a four-month release cadence timed with Kubernetes so that features depending on the container runtime arrive in both projects at about the same time.
    2. 2:33 Karp describes a long-term support release that comes out once a year and is supported for two years, giving a year of overlap between LTS releases.
    3. 14:37 Fu explains that direct unpack for overlayfs removed the implicit flush on unmount, so a crash could leave a committed snapshot without its data, and says a single EROFS layer file is easier to fsync.
    4. 18:43 Fu presents a proposed 2.5 feature, a sandboxed runc shim that handles the sandbox API, with the goal of removing the pause container that he calls a waste of resources for most pods.
    5. 21:51 Karp reports 156 incoming security reports in 2026 against 36 from 2020 to 2025, with 14 CVEs issued this year, and attributes the rise to LLM-assisted bug hunting.

    etcd from 3.7 to 3.8 and Beyond

    Siyuan Zhang, Marek Siarkowicz, Ivan Valdes Castillo, Nont Thanonchai W, Jeffrey Ying

    The SIG etcd maintainers give project updates, describe the RangeStream feature and dependency cleanup in etcd 3.7, and outline performance work planned for 3.8 such as non-blocking defragmentation, an index rewrite and compression. They also report on the etcd-operator's move away from StatefulSets and take audience questions about operator maturity and compression.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesData, storage and stateObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you run etcd or large Kubernetes control planes and want to hear from the maintainers what 3.7 changed and what 3.8 is aiming for.

    1. 2:34 Ivan Valdes Castillo says the project aims to support at least the latest three minor versions and gives the current patch levels of 3.5, 3.6 and 3.7.
    2. 6:28 Jeffrey Ying explains that RangeStream splits a very large range request into chunks sized by bytes instead of key count, which he says keeps etcd memory use more consistent.
    3. 9:06 Ying says RangeStream with concurrent decoding gave about two to three times the throughput for watch cache initialization, and that both are on by default in Kubernetes 1.37 and etcd 3.7.
    4. 15:33 Marek Siarkowicz says non-blocking defragmentation has landed for 3.8 and does most of its work in the background instead of locking the database for tens of seconds.
    5. 18:10 Nont says the etcd-operator stopped using StatefulSets and now manages pods directly, because StatefulSets got in the way of recovering from partial and full cluster failures.

    Hidden Pod Startup Latency: Container Image Pull

    Omer Oz

    Omer Oz describes how LinkedIn investigated slow pod startups on its bare-metal fleet and traced most of the time to unpacking container image layers, not downloading them. He walks through the kubelet and containerd settings his team changed, the results he reports, and further options such as zstd compression, the EROFS snapshotter and peer-to-peer caching.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: large container images slow your pod startups and you want a mental model of the pull path plus settings to try.

    1. 9:12 Oz describes the trigger: Flink apps shipping about 28 GB of images against a 20-minute pod startup SLO, which he says caused a series of incidents at LinkedIn.
    2. 14:46 Oz walks through an image pull timeline and says the download finishes early while most time goes to unpacking, which containerd does one layer at a time by default.
    3. 15:26 Oz explains LinkedIn's fix of raising the kubelet's parallel image pulls to three and lowering containerd's download concurrency, then shows the latency results he measured.
    4. 21:46 Oz presents the EROFS snapshotter for containerd, which he says skips eager unpacking and cut unpack time to under 0.4 seconds in his tests.
    5. 23:57 Oz describes a serialization point he found in containerd that capped total download throughput, and points to his pull request removing it.
