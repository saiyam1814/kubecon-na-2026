    Human Scale, Agent Scale

    Jonathan Bryce

    Jonathan Bryce argues that agents put new pressure on systems built around human browsing habits, citing Wikimedia as an example. He says cloud-native design principles can help existing services handle agent traffic, not just help teams run models.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want examples of how agent traffic can change demand on the systems people already use.

    1. 1:38 Bryce argues that systems designed for human speed are already under stress as they begin to operate at agent speed.
    2. 2:23 Bryce compares the shift to smartphones turning mobile networks from mostly voice and SMS into data networks, and predicts agents will change a wider range of systems.
    3. 3:13 Citing Anthropic's internal observations, Bryce says a single goal given to an agent can generate many requests, unlike a simple human chat exchange.
    4. 5:16 Bryce cites Wikimedia as an example, saying agents follow different browsing patterns from humans and place disproportionate load on resource-intensive requests.
    5. 6:26 Bryce argues that cloud-native principles such as resilience, scalability, and observability still apply, and frames AI as a new workload rather than the endpoint for the ecosystem.
