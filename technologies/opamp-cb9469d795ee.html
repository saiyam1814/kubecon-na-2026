    OTel Blueprints: Prescriptive Patterns for Enterprise Observability

    Dan Gomez Blanco, Lukasz Ciukaj

    Dan Gomez Blanco and Lukasz Ciukaj say adopting OpenTelemetry team by team can leave traces, metrics, logs, and profiles difficult to correlate. They introduce OpenTelemetry Blueprints as maintained guidance and walk through patterns for virtual machines, shared telemetry platforms, and Kubernetes clusters.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Observability and reliability

    Watch this if: You need OpenTelemetry defaults that teams can share without forcing every service to use an identical configuration.

    1. 1:28 The speakers say teams adopting OpenTelemetry in isolation can lose context between services and between applications and infrastructure.
    2. 8:27 The speakers describe a blueprint as a living document that makes scoped recommendations for a particular environment instead of listing every possible configuration.
    3. 16:48 For environments outside Kubernetes, the speakers recommend managing agents centrally, adding a collector gateway layer, and standardizing resource attributes.
    4. 18:18 For a shared Kubernetes telemetry platform, the speakers suggest that platform teams own defaults and managed gateways while product teams own and extend their signals.
    5. 20:07 For Kubernetes cluster observability, the speakers recommend native receivers, shared semantic conventions, and using the OpenTelemetry Operator for consistent collection.
