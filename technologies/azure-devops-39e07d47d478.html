  • Platform Engineering Day

    Platform Engineering's Missing Chapter: Services Organizations

    Atulpriya Sharma

    Atulpriya argues that platform engineering models built for product companies do not directly fit consultancies and services organizations. He presents delivery capital and a capability layer as ways to reuse organizational learning across client engagements while measuring ramp time, consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you build or operate platforms in a consultancy, systems integrator, or client-facing delivery organization.

    1. 2:59 Atulpriya argues that platform engineering's familiar vocabulary was not built around the operating model of consulting firms and services organizations.
    2. 9:03 Atulpriya defines delivery capital as the learning a services organization accumulates across engagements, and says it is the asset the organization truly owns.
    3. 11:04 Atulpriya says the capability layer joins reusable organizational knowledge with an internal consultant IDP of sandboxes and demo clusters so teams can apply prior learning.
    4. 12:57 Atulpriya maps the approach across cloud, IaC, CI/CD, observability, and monitoring, with artifacts such as decision records, checklists, templates, baselines, and named alert owners.
    5. 23:29 Atulpriya argues that ownership, rotation, and billable-work identity block adoption in services firms, and says time-to-ramp, delivery consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time are the measures to watch.

    Two Namespaces, One Mesh: Zero-Downtime Releases for an Always-On Aviation Platform

    Rahul Raj Valia Veetil

    The speaker describes how his team at Etihad Airways releases a three-tier airline operations platform without downtime by running the live and candidate versions in two namespaces behind one Istio mesh. He says a header routes only testers to the candidate, and after approval users get a two-minute notice before traffic switches and the old version is drained.

    Length not listed · Level not listed · Type not listed

    Delivery, GitOps and release safetyNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: You need blue-green releases across front end, back end and database for an application that cannot take downtime.

    1. 0:46 The speaker says the operations platform must always be on, and that a release has to keep a static front end, a Kubernetes back end and a database compatible.
    2. 1:57 He describes two namespaces, one holding the live version and one the candidate, with an Istio ingress deciding where traffic goes.
    3. 3:54 He says Istio marks the new version as a candidate reachable only by testers, and after promotion the team monitors for two to three minutes and can roll back.
    4. 5:06 He shows that users get a pop-up with a two-minute timer to save their work before being moved to the new version.
    5. 6:22 He says a header passed through the front end, back end and database channels is what sends testers to the preview version, and he then plays a recorded demo of the pipeline.
