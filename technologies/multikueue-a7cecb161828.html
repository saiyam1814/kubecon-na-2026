    Many Clusters, One GPU Pool: Locality-Aware Scheduling and Data Delivery With Kueue and Dragonfly

    Yiqing Wang, Adam Buran

    Yiqing Wang and Adam Buran describe how Roblox shares GPU capacity across clusters using Kueue while leaving each cluster to check its own capacity. They explain how a locality-aware dispatcher chooses clusters and how Dragonfly caches training data and checkpoints near the jobs that use them.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingData, storage and state

    Watch this if: You operate GPU clusters in more than one location and want to share capacity without repeatedly pulling the same datasets across regions.

    1. 6:55 The speakers describe two levels of MultiKueue admission: the hub checks team quota, then the candidate clusters check node capacity and the first to admit the job runs it with KubeRay.
    2. 8:15 The speakers explain how the dispatcher first filters for requirements such as GPU type and full-job fit, then prefers the data region for a limited time before considering other regions.
    3. 9:19 The speakers explain how Roblox separates pool-wide guaranteed quota from cluster-level borrowing and uses preemption to reclaim borrowed GPUs when a guaranteed job arrives.
    4. 14:51 The speakers describe Dragonfly looking for file pieces in the local cache, other peers, and seed peers before falling back to Amazon S3.
    5. 15:55 The speakers report that half of the data served came from node-local cache and another 43 percent from peers in the same cluster, leaving 7 percent served from Amazon S3.
