    PKI Done Easy with Pod Certificates and Cluster Trust Bundles

    Taahir Ahmed, Stanislav Láznička

    Taahir Ahmed and Stanislav Láznička explain two Kubernetes features they say are stable in 1.37: Pod Certificates, where the kubelet generates a key and obtains and rotates a certificate for a pod, and Cluster Trust Bundles for distributing root certificates. They demo Ahmed's TinyCert signer issuing SPIFFE certificates to a client and a server, and outline built-in signers they hope to add upstream.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Security, identity and policyKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you still copy CA ConfigMaps into every namespace or hand-roll certificate requests for pods, and want the API-level view of the replacement.

    1. 5:44 Láznička describes the Pod Certificates flow: the kubelet generates the private key, publishes a PodCertificateRequest, a signer issues the certificate, and the kubelet writes both into the pod, so he says the key never leaves the node.
    2. 8:20 He says certificate lifetimes are capped at 24 hours for kubernetes.io signers and 91 days for all others, and that the kubelet also handles rotation.
    3. 10:26 Ahmed says Kubernetes has no built-in signer for these yet, so you bring your own, and introduces TinyCert, a small signer he wrote that he suggests for learning the feature set.
    4. 16:05 In the demo he applies a client and a server pod that each mount a SPIFFE certificate and trust bundle, shows the PodCertificateRequest objects, and reads logs of each side authenticating the other's SPIFFE ID.
    5. 22:48 Láznička outlines a planned built-in signer for service serving certificates, which he says would cover server TLS for webhooks and aggregated APIs inside a single cluster.
