    Rook: Intro and Deep Dive with Ceph Storage

    Parth Arora, Blaine Gardner

    Parth Arora introduces Rook as the operator that runs and manages Ceph storage on Kubernetes and describes recent erasure coding work in Ceph. Blaine Gardner then covers recent Rook features and explains a CephX authentication CVE and the key rotation Rook offers in response.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Panel

    Data, storage and stateSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you run or are evaluating Rook and Ceph and want the maintainers' view of erasure coding, recent features and the CephX key rotation.

    1. 8:37 Arora explains erasure coding with a 4+2 example, saying 100 TB of data takes 150 TB instead of the 300 TB used by three replicas, with some cost in performance.
    2. 9:38 He describes Ceph's direct reads feature, where a client reads straight from an OSD, and says erasure coding with large blocks can now be faster than three replicas.
    3. 11:53 Gardner says Rook now has client IO rate limiting based on cgroup v2 io.max, which can be added to an existing PVC with a VolumeAttributesClass.
    4. 13:58 Gardner says the team is experimenting with an NVMe over Fabrics gateway that exposes RBD block devices outside the cluster over NVMe/TCP, and gives SmartNICs feeding GPUs as a reason.
    5. 18:24 Gardner explains the CephX CVE as service tickets having no integrity check, which he says let a client with any key work towards an admin key, and says Rook can rotate most keys automatically after the update.
