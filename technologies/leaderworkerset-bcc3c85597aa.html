    DisaggregatedSet Meets Multi-EPP: Coordinating llm-d at Scale in production

    Mathis Felardos, Lucas Fernandes Martins

    Mathis Felardos presents DisaggregatedSet, which Mistral AI contributed to the LeaderWorkerSet project to deploy and roll out prefill and decode roles together. Lucas Fernandes Martins presents multi-EPP for llm-d, where several router replicas share in-flight load state through a pluggable cross-replica syncer, with Mistral's Redis implementation as the example.

    20 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: You run disaggregated prefill and decode serving and want safer rollouts or a horizontally scaled llm-d router.

    1. 1:18 Mathis Felardos explains disaggregated serving, splitting compute-bound prefill from memory-bound decode, and why it is hard to operate during rollouts.
    2. 3:10 He describes DisaggregatedSet, contributed into LeaderWorkerSet, with declarative prefill and decode roles and coordinated rolling updates.
    3. 5:48 He describes cross-revision gating, where llm-d shifts requests gradually to a new revision so incompatible prefill and decode pods do not pair up.
    4. 9:07 Lucas Fernandes Martins shows why naive multiple EPP replicas diverge, and introduces a cross-replica syncer interface with set, get and aggregate.
    5. 14:38 He shows benchmarks where one EPP fails after around 700 requests per second while three EPPs syncing through Redis keep serving.

    One Workload to Rule Them All: Workload Aware Scheduling for Distributed AI Training on Kubernetes

    Andrey Velichkevich

    Andrey Velichkevich explains why scheduling distributed training one pod at a time can deadlock jobs and how the Kubernetes Workload and PodGroup APIs model them as a unit. He shows how Kubeflow Trainer exposes those capabilities through training runtimes and compares three scheduling policies on a local cluster.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: You run distributed training on Kubernetes and want to understand when pod groups and topology constraints matter.

    1. 0:48 Velichkevich illustrates how two training jobs can deadlock when the scheduler gives each job some GPUs but neither gets enough to start all its ranks.
    2. 4:44 Velichkevich distinguishes the Workload template from runtime PodGroups and shows how a PodGroup can express a minimum pod count and topology constraints.
    3. 7:10 Velichkevich introduces composite PodGroups for workloads with a hierarchy, where a parent can require a minimum number of child groups before scheduling proceeds.
    4. 10:12 Velichkevich explains the Kubeflow Trainer split: platform admins define runtime blueprints, and scientists use a Python SDK to submit jobs without managing Kubernetes deployment details.
    5. 15:17 In a local kind demo, Velichkevich sets up a comparison of default pod-by-pod placement, gang scheduling, and rack-constrained placement for a PyTorch job.

    Volcano for the Agentic AI Era: Unified Scheduling Across Training, Inference, and Agents

    Ricardo Aravena, Rainie Li

    Rainie Li explains how Volcano aims to schedule training, inference and agent workloads on one Kubernetes cluster, using a batch scheduler and an agent scheduler that share nodes through a sharding controller, plus multi-level gang scheduling, network topology hypernodes and gang-aware preemption. Ricardo Aravena then covers how workload controllers such as Kthena, KubeRay and Grove map onto Volcano primitives, the project's benchmarking work and the roadmap.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingAI agents and automationAI inference and serving

    Watch this if: you schedule AI training, inference or agent workloads on shared GPU clusters and want to know what Volcano offers for gang and topology-aware placement.

    1. 3:02 Rainie Li says Volcano uses two schedulers, a batch scheduler for coordinated workloads and an agent scheduler for latency-sensitive pods, with a sharding controller assigning candidate nodes to each and moving them as demand changes.
    2. 6:49 She introduces multi-level gang scheduling, in which a pod group contains subgroups with their own completeness requirements, such as a tensor-parallel group or a prefill or decode replica.
    3. 9:56 She explains gang-aware preemption, which treats victims as groups and simulates placement first, so no pod is evicted if the whole incoming gang cannot fit.
    4. 15:12 Ricardo Aravena says KubeRay support is merged upstream and listed for Volcano 1.16, and walks through how its head and worker groups translate into Volcano subgroups.
    5. 18:35 He describes the scheduling benchmark framework shipped with Volcano 1.15, which reports scheduling latency and binding throughput on kind, KWOK or an existing cluster, and notes it says nothing about GPU job speed.

    WG-Batch Updates: What’s New and What Is Next?

    Amy Chen, Yuki Iwai, Marcin Wielgus

    Marcin Wielgus, Amy Chen and Yuki Iwai give an update on the Kubernetes Batch Working Group and its projects. They cover new Kueue features such as concurrent admission, configurable preemption, topology-aware spreading, the dynamic quota orchestrator and partial scale-up, then Job, JobSet and workload-aware scheduling updates.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run AI training or batch jobs on Kubernetes and want to know what changed in Kueue, Job and JobSet.

    1. 3:46 Wielgus says that since last November Kueue shipped 54 releases, while PRs, issues and contributors each more than doubled.
    2. 4:45 Wielgus explains concurrent admission, where virtual copies of a workload keep trying for cheaper reserved capacity and move the job there once it frees up.
    3. 12:36 Wielgus introduces topology-aware spreading for inference, which caps the share of serving capacity placed in one rack, for example at 45%.
    4. 14:27 Chen describes the dynamic quota orchestrator, which discovers real capacity from node pools or MultiKueue worker clusters and adjusts nominal quota automatically.
    5. 28:54 Iwai explains that Kueue is adopting a shared scheduler library from kube-scheduler so its node feasibility checks match what kube-scheduler will accept.

    Why Are You Still Wasting Whole GPUs? Hardware-Level Sharing, Scheduled with DRA

    Engin Diri

    Engin Diri of Pulumi recounts helping a European customer build a self-hosted inference platform, moving from whole GPUs and time slicing to NVIDIA MIG slices that pods request through Dynamic Resource Allocation. He shows a recorded demo of MIG profiles, resource slices and claims on an AWS node serving a model behind an inference gateway, and lists open questions such as quotas, models larger than a node and start-up ordering.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run inference on your own GPUs and want a practical walk through MIG slices requested with Dynamic Resource Allocation.

    1. 8:32 Diri says time slicing gives no memory or fault isolation between workloads, so the choice was between wasting GPUs and risking a noisy neighbor.
    2. 11:24 Diri says Dynamic Resource Allocation, which he dates to Kubernetes 1.34, lets a request describe device attributes instead of an integer count.
    3. 22:16 In the recorded demo, Diri lists the 1G, 2G and 4G MIG profiles on the node and the resource slices the DRA driver published.
    4. 24:28 Diri lists open day-two questions he is evaluating with the customer, including Kueue for MIG quota and which workload runs first.
    5. 28:06 Diri advises starting the DRA driver only after MIG is configured, because he ran into race conditions between the two.
