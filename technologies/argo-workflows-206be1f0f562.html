    1000 Clusters, 1 Smarter Brain: The Next Evolution of AIOps at Salesforce

    Anubhav Dhoot, Uttam Kumar

    The speakers say the accuracy of their incident troubleshooting agents fell as they added more data, and trace that to knowledge retrieval, evaluation, and agent design. They describe curated runbooks with hierarchical search, an LLM-as-judge pipeline calibrated with sampled on-call feedback, and a planner and critic design backed by a dependency knowledge graph, and report their own results.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Observability and reliabilityAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you are building incident triage agents for a large Kubernetes fleet and want one team's account of why more data made theirs worse.

    1. 4:28 The speaker says accuracy dropped to 25% as more data was added, and names three bottlenecks: knowledge management, evaluation, and agent design.
    2. 6:17 The speaker describes a similarity trap where long Slack threads full of repeated error codes outranked the runbook holding the fix, even with a lower weight.
    3. 12:25 The speaker says they use Cochran's formula to ask on-call engineers for feedback on a sample, citing 88 samples per 1,000 transactions, and use it to calibrate the automated evals.
    4. 16:56 The speaker describes the new design: a planner agent forms several hypotheses from the knowledge graph, isolated sub-agents investigate them in parallel, and a critic agent validates the result.
    5. 22:02 The speaker reports a 60% improvement in time to troubleshoot, close to 900 hours saved, and 60 to 80% accuracy on their eval pipeline.

    A Decade of AI on Kubernetes: Kubeflow’s Journey to CNCF Graduation

    Valentina Rodriguez Sosa, Alexander Perlman, Christian Heusel, Shivay Lamba, David Aronchick

    Kubeflow co-founder David Aronchick recounts how the project started, and contributors describe its components, governance and what they say CNCF graduation required. The speakers then outline planned work, including Kubeflow Pipelines 3.0 changes such as removing the ML Metadata service, a unified Python SDK, trainer and notebook features, an MCP server and experimental Helm charts for the community distribution.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you run or contribute to Kubeflow and want the origin story, what graduation involved, and the maintainers' stated plans for Pipelines 3.0, the SDK and agent support.

    1. 2:30 David Aronchick says the first Kubeflow release was deliberately small, with only a Jupyter notebook, a TensorFlow CRD and what later became KServe, and that pipelines came about six months later.
    2. 12:22 Christian Heusel says Kubeflow reached CNCF graduated status in July 2026, and that graduation required maintainers from at least two organizations per sub-project, independent adopters and a security and governance review.
    3. 20:56 Alexander Perlman says Kubeflow Pipelines 3.0 will consolidate the per-step driver pods into one pod per run, roughly halving the pod count, using Argo Workflows executor plugins.
    4. 22:13 He says 3.0 removes the ML Metadata microservice and database and moves execution tracking, artifacts, lineage and caching into the Pipelines API server.
    5. 29:24 Shivay Lamba describes an experimental Kubeflow MCP server that exposes trainer jobs to agents while applying the user's own Kubernetes permissions so the agent stays within bounds.

    Michael Rishi Forrester

    Michael Forrester runs a live bingo game he calls unofficial, in which the audience marks CNCF projects on a phone card as he reads three clues per project, from broad to narrow. He works through about fifteen projects, from Kubeflow to Knative, until three attendees win, and points to flashcards he wrote covering every CNCF project.

    12 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: You are new to the CNCF landscape and want a quick, game-style pass over what about fifteen well-known projects do.

    1. 0:13 The speaker explains the game: scan a QR code for a bingo card of CNCF projects with Kubernetes as the free middle square, and long-press a square to read what the project does.
    2. 2:17 He runs a practice round with three clues that lead to Kubeflow: a machine learning platform with notebooks and pipelines, started at Google, used for MLOps on Kubernetes.

    5. 10:50 He says the same site has flashcards for every CNCF project, and that he had to look up about a third of the projects when writing them.

    From Consumers to Contributors: How Growing Open Source Members Transformed Our ML Platform Team

    Tolga Tuncoglu, Herik Webb

    The speakers, who lead ML training platforms at Capital One, describe how their teams went from maintaining internal forks of Kubeflow Pipelines to fixing problems upstream and holding maintainer roles in the project. They share the three changes they made, the contribution numbers they attribute to the shift, and examples from Argo Workflows, OAuth2 Proxy and Notebook Intelligence.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Community and open source adoptionPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: You run a platform built on open source projects you only consume and want to hear how one enterprise team made the case for contributing upstream.

    1. 3:58 Tolga Tuncoglu says that after Kubeflow Pipelines moved to v2 their tenants were not ready, so the team kept internal forks and struggled to keep them in sync with upstream.
    2. 5:32 He describes a high-severity protobuf CVE as the turning point: they fixed the upstream CI for the legacy version, shipped a release there and stopped maintaining the fork.
    3. 7:46 Tuncoglu lists the three things they changed: time for upstream work in sprints, asking whether a fix can go upstream first, and recognizing upstream contributions.
    4. 12:15 He says their share of human-merged pull requests in the project went from 5% to 48%, and that 71% of pull requests to release branches come from Capital One.
    5. 16:50 Herik Webb says they chose Netflix's Notebook Intelligence to add AI tooling to their Jupyter platform, talked to its maintainers and contributed 242 pull requests over six months.

    Millions of Events, One Right Order: Financial Data Processing at Intuit

    Yashash Lokesh, Sri Harsha Yayi

    The speakers describe using Numaflow to process streams of Intuit financial events on Kubernetes. They walk through a simple transaction pipeline, a multi-stage analytics pipeline, and an ordering example for customer account events.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Data, storage and state

    Watch this if: You are building a streaming pipeline where financial events for the same account must stay in order.

    1. 8:32 The speakers explain why Intuit wanted a common streaming platform: language lock-in, scaling through downstream bottlenecks, and preserving the order of financial events.
    2. 12:26 The speakers show a live transaction pipeline that consumes events from Kafka using a single Numaflow vertex.
    3. 15:13 The speakers walk through a financial analytics pipeline that filters at the source, pre-aggregates records, and routes results to different sinks.
    4. 16:36 The speakers use customer account changes to explain Numaflow's ordering option and why related events must stay in sequence throughout the pipeline.
    5. 18:23 The speakers explain how the same event pipeline can support model inference or agents by scaling its components independently.

    Translating Mesh Identity into MCP Impersonation: Governing AI Agent Identities with Istio

    Juan David Arguello Plata

    The speaker describes an agent platform on Istio ambient in which agents and tools get workload identities and each user's token travels with a request, so third-party tools are called on behalf of that user. He argues that without mesh authorization policies the prompt is the only guardrail, and that the mesh, not the model, should be the security boundary.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policyAI agents and automationNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: You are building an agent platform and need tool calls to carry the real user's identity, not a shared service account.

    1. 0:18 The speaker names prompt injection, user impersonation and secret leakage as the three red flags of the agent identity problem.
    2. 0:57 He says that without workload identities in the mesh, a triage agent can reach every other agent and every tool.
    3. 2:09 He says third-party tools reached through service accounts log only the service account, so the audit trail does not show which user an agent acted for.
    4. 2:48 He describes the platform: Istio ambient with peer authentication and authorization policies, kagent and kmcp managing agents and tools with SPIFFE identities, and agentgateway checking user tokens.
    5. 4:52 He walks through the flow in which a token is minted at login, an Argo workflow sends the event and token to agentgateway, and the chosen agent calls the tool through an MCP server, REST API or CLI.
