    CNCF Platform Engineering Technical Community Group Update

    Chris Plank, Colin Griffin, Atulpriya Sharma, Katie Greenley

    The community-group leaders describe the CNCF Platform Engineering Technical Community Group as an open space for practitioners, vendors, and end users, and point viewers to its meetings, white paper, maturity model, self-assessment, and capability-factors work. They invite feedback and participation through birds-of-a-feather sessions, morning platform coffees, the community booth and lightning talks, a conference guide, and a post-KubeCon wrap-up.

    10 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Community and open source adoptionPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want the community group's meeting rhythm, current initiatives, and in-person KubeCon activities in one short update.

    1. 0:11 The presenters describe the community group as an open place for practitioners, vendors, and end users to share knowledge, explore practices, and collaborate on cloud-native platforms.
    2. 1:09 The presenters describe biweekly Tuesday meetings that rotate between open hallway-track discussion and community calls with updates and a guest speaker.
    3. 3:11 Colin introduces the platform capability factors initiative as work on organizational and contractual models for delivering and contributing platform capabilities, with feedback requested on the review draft.
    4. 5:42 Atul presents the conference guide as a single page for Platform Engineering Day talks and platform-related sessions across the rest of KubeCon.
    5. 6:41 Atul says the community booth will host platform discussions and a series of ten-minute lightning talks about what participants are learning and building.

    The CNCF Platform Engineering User Group & Platform Factors

    Colin Griffin, Avinash Sabat

    Colin Griffin describes how the CNCF Platform Engineering User Group develops resources and recommends using its maturity model to decide where to invest rather than to maximize every dimension. Avinash Sabat presents draft Platform Capability Factors and uses a self-service database to explain what a capability should deliver and who should own it.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Birds of a Feather

    Community and open source adoptionPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you are defining what a self-service platform capability should deliver and who will own it.

    1. 5:20 Griffin says the user group brings contributors together to write resources such as the platforms white paper, the maturity model, and its self-assessment.
    2. 8:30 Griffin says teams should use the maturity model to decide where investment makes sense rather than trying to reach its optimizing column in every area.
    3. 13:27 Sabat distinguishes platform operators who own shared infrastructure and governance, specialist teams that produce capabilities, and consumers who request them through self-service.
    4. 17:13 Sabat says a self-service database should deliver a complete outcome, including storage and backups, with clear responsibilities and no need to contact the database team for every request.
    5. 19:26 Sabat says a capability should reconcile repeated requests safely without drifting, and that both producers and consumers should be able to see how it is performing.
