    10 Years of Minikube and the Future of Millions of Users

    Medya Ghazizadeh

    The speaker, who says he led minikube for seven years, looks back on the project's ten years, including its testing infrastructure, benchmarking tools, and growth in downloads. He describes the project's reduced staffing since 2025, his own burnout and departure from Google, and what he hopes the next maintainers will do.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoptionPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you use minikube or maintain an open source project and want a longtime maintainer's account of how it was built and what it needs next.

    1. 5:04 The speaker says minikube lets users choose the container runtime and whether to run in a container or a VM, and that its VM drivers allow AI workloads on a MacBook's GPU.
    2. 14:42 The speaker says the project ended up with 300 integration tests across 21 environment combinations, and credits that testing with catching Kubernetes regressions.
    3. 19:21 The speaker says a benchmark showed minikube's slow start came from lacking a preload mechanism, not from the driver, and that with preloads a VM-driver cluster starts in 13 seconds.
    4. 22:05 The speaker says that in 2025 he absorbed the work of four or five people, and that he rejected advice to let the project break in order to attract funding.
    5. 24:33 The speaker says he can no longer work on minikube full-time and hopes the next maintainers grow the bench, end reliance on any single company, and gain representation in Kubernetes decision-making.

    Autoscaling Reality Check: The Hands-On HTTP Optimization Challenge

    David Pech

    David Pech runs a hands-on tutorial in which attendees benchmark HTTP services in NGINX, Go, PHP and Java with k6 and watch how autoscaling reacts, first with the CPU-based Horizontal Pod Autoscaler and then with Knative scaling on concurrent requests. He walks through each round on a Grafana dashboard and describes the tuning he tried, including cases where he says his own improvement failed.

    80 min slot · Any Level · Tutorial

    Accelerators and workload schedulingObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you run HTTP services behind HPA or Knative and want to see, round by round, where autoscaling lags behind real traffic.

    1. 10:11 The speaker lays out the lab: Argo CD, Istio, Knative and a Prometheus stack on a local k3d cluster or GitHub Codespaces, with k6 load tests run against services in several flavors.
    2. 29:15 In the NGINX round scaled on CPU, the speaker shows on the dashboard that pods stay at the maximum after the load falls and that latencies remain, and asks the room why.
    3. 36:23 The speaker explains Knative's panic window: when demand jumps well above the estimate the autoscaler enters panic mode and does not scale down during it.
    4. 1:03:46 For the Java round the speaker says slow startup limits how fast scaling can react, names GraalVM as one option, and shows a tuning attempt that he says failed completely.
    5. 1:06:13 In the scale-to-zero round the speaker points out that a workload at zero has no metrics, which he says makes alerting on it hard.

    K3s Lightning Update

    Orlin Vasilev

    Orlin Vasilev, who introduces himself as the K3s community manager, gives a short introduction to K3s and an update on its latest releases. He covers a fix for a systemd restart loop, Gateway API CRDs shipped out of the box, a move of the project's images to GitHub Container Registry, and community growth numbers.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Lightning Talk

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you use K3s and want the release changes that may need action, especially the image registry move.

    1. 0:25 Orlin Vasilev describes K3s as a Kubernetes distribution in a single binary under 100 MB that boots in seconds and can be installed with one curl command.
    2. 1:14 He highlights a fix in the release he names as 1.36.6 for a systemd restart loop that he says could occur on slow networks.
    3. 2:03 He says K3s now includes the Gateway API CRDs out of the box, so users can create gateways and HTTP routes without installing them manually.
    4. 2:17 He announces that K3s images are moving from the Rancher organization on Docker Hub to GitHub Container Registry, and says users with air-gapped setups, mirrors or private registries need to act and should read the blog post.
    5. 3:16 He reports that since the last KubeCon the project gained 1,600 GitHub stars, 238 forks and 44 contributors, for a total of 1,339 contributors.
