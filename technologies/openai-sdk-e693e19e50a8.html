    Secure Your Agents in 5 Minutes: Durable Execution with Cadence

    Diana Zawadzki

    Diana Zawadzki of the Cadence team at Uber presents Cadence as a way to make long-running AI agent tasks durable, so that a crashed worker resumes from saved progress instead of starting over. She plays a recorded run of an on-call agent workflow that she says is deployed at Uber, with injected failures, and outlines the steps for wrapping an agent in Cadence activities and workflows.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Lightning Talk

    AI agents and automationObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you run long, multi-step AI agent tasks and want to see how a workflow engine keeps their progress across failures.

    1. 0:48 Diana Zawadzki describes Cadence as open source software built at Uber that tracks a job through its steps, so in her words a crash is a pause and not a reset from scratch.
    2. 1:15 She explains the model: the workflow tracks what happens next, activities are the model or tool calls, and if a worker stops another worker can pick up where it left off.
    3. 1:38 She lists what she says Cadence gives agents: recovery from crashes and API rate limits, waiting for human review, rewinding to the last viable step, and child workflows for child agents.
    4. 2:46 She introduces a case study, an on-call agent workflow she says is deployed live at Uber, that traces logs, drafts a report, waits for human review and saves the report.
    5. 3:44 She plays a recorded run in which a rate limit error is retried, a human signal asks for a deeper draft, and the workers are killed before the run resumes from saved progress and completes.
