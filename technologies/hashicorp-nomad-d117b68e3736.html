    Kubernetes Anonymous: Confessions of Kubernetes Cargo Cultists

    Taylor Thomas, Brooks Townsend

    The speakers, who say they have worked both on Kubernetes-heavy platforms and on a wasmCloud platform that avoided Kubernetes, argue that both extremes are traps. They list red flags for using Kubernetes where it fits poorly, describe what each extreme cost them, and suggest that CNCF projects be compatible with Kubernetes without depending on it.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You are deciding how much of your platform or project should be built on Kubernetes APIs and want the tradeoffs from people who tried both extremes.

    1. 1:43 The speakers poll the room on red and yellow flags, such as using Kubernetes as the database for config objects, making every service a CRD and mapping an API permission model onto Kubernetes RBAC.
    2. 10:58 Taylor Thomas says avoiding Kubernetes on principle for their wasmCloud platform was their own yellow flag, and that it cost them adoption and integrations with the rest of the ecosystem.
    3. 15:27 Describing Kargo, Thomas says Kubernetes became its database, API and auth model, and that most of its high and critical CVEs this year came from contorting the access model to fit Kubernetes RBAC.
    4. 17:29 He says Kargo 2.0 moves promotion and artifact tracking to a real database while Kubernetes stays as the execution substrate that runs pods.
    5. 20:21 Thomas asks people to stop putting everything in a CRD and offers test questions, including whether the work depends on a Kubernetes feature and whether it would be valuable outside Kubernetes.
