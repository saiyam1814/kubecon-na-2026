    Reduce vLLM Pod Cold Starts with KServe Kernel Caching

    Jooho lee, Vishwa Hiren Gandhi

    The speakers explain how GPU kernel compilation can slow vLLM startup even when model weights are already cached. They describe KServe's approach for sharing signed kernel caches across pods and report what changed in their benchmark.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: Your vLLM replicas scale slowly even after you have cached the model weights.

    1. 4:33 The speakers break down a baseline vLLM startup of about 250 seconds and say the compilation step accounts for roughly 35 seconds, excluding image pulls and model downloads.
    2. 5:38 The speakers explain why a new replica can compile kernels again even after another pod has compiled them: the new pod does not inherit the previous pod's local cache.
    3. 10:31 The speakers propose packaging compiled kernels as a signed OCI artifact in a registry so another node can verify the signer before making the cache available.
    4. 13:52 The speakers distinguish a workload fingerprint that identifies where a cache came from from a compatibility fingerprint that checks whether compilation settings match.
    5. 18:24 In one benchmark, the speakers report that reusing the cache cut startup from 160 to 110 seconds and compilation from about 35 seconds to just over one second.
