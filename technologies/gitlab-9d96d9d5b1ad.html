    From Clever to Correct: Lessons from Rebuilding SeatGeek's Platform AI Agent

    Colin O'Dell

    Colin O'Dell describes why SeatGeek replaced its specialist-heavy platform agent, Rufus, with a generalist agent called Sandy that can carry a task from investigation through a code change. He explains how a per-session sandbox keeps full logs out of the agent's context and how the team uses reports from Slack or Jira to improve the agent itself.

    10 min slot · Intermediate · Lightning Talk

    AI agents and automationPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: You are deciding what to build yourself when an internal platform agent needs to diagnose problems and make changes.

    1. 1:23 O'Dell explains how Rufus routed requests to four specialists that could not easily hand work off. An Argo CD specialist could diagnose a deployment failure but could not pass the fix to the coding agent.
    2. 4:10 He describes rebuilding around one OpenCode harness and a per-session sandbox, where Sandy can store full logs on disk and pull only the relevant lines into its context.
    3. 6:43 He explains why the team reused OpenCode and Daytona for the harness and sandboxes, leaving more time for platform-specific routines and skills.
    4. 7:05 He describes how the team uses Sandy to fix Sandy: a report from Slack or Jira can start a sandboxed fix and merge request. He also describes a tool that posts a short report when the agent gets stuck.
    5. 8:56 He argues for treating the internal agent as a product that keeps evolving, after putting Rufus into maintenance mode made later improvements harder to adopt.

    Is Platform Engineering and the Platform Maturity Model Still Relevant in the Agentic AI Era?

    Chris Plank, Corey McGalliard

    Chris and Corey introduce the CNCF Platform Engineering Maturity Model as a map rather than a pass-or-fail assessment and describe community updates such as plainer language, translations, a self-assessment tool, and worked examples. They argue that agentic AI makes platform maturity more relevant because proofs of concept still need production ownership, guardrails, customer feedback, and redesign for machine-speed scale.

    25 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI agents and automationPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want a practitioner walkthrough of applying the CNCF Platform Engineering Maturity Model to AI-era platforms.

    1. 5:15 The speakers describe the maturity model's five aspects of investment, adoption, interfaces, operations, and measurement across four levels, framing it as a map rather than a pass-or-fail assessment.
    2. 7:51 Chris uses the investment aspect to argue that many AI rollouts remain provisional projects focused on training and adoption numbers without considering day-two operation or product ownership.
    3. 11:05 The speakers say the updated model includes a community-contributed self-assessment tool whose questions can reveal concrete gaps in customer feedback and platform practice.
    4. 13:20 The speakers argue that AI proofs of concept encounter the maturity question when teams ask how they will run in production, who will own them, and how day-two maintenance will work.
    5. 20:29 The speakers argue that AI multiplies existing good and bad platform behavior, while systems designed for human-scale interaction must be redesigned for agents operating continuously and at much higher concurrency.

    No RAG, No Regrets: Letting an LLM Drive 30 EKS Clusters in Production

    Trevor Linton

    Trevor Linton describes how Mastercard built an agent harness to handle repeatable infrastructure work without retrieval-augmented generation. He walks through a cert-manager upgrade and explains how scoped tools, result filtering, token limits, and human approvals fit into the workflow.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    AI agents and automationPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: You want to automate routine Kubernetes maintenance while keeping approvals and audit trails visible.

    1. 7:30 Linton says his team deploys its Node.js agent harness as a Kubernetes application and uses PostgreSQL to resume interrupted sessions.
    2. 10:16 Linton shows a cert-manager upgrade run that reads release notes, checks compatibility, proposes changes, and leaves merge and production change approvals to people.
    3. 11:39 Linton opens an audit trace that exposes individual tool calls and lets reviewers inspect the data given to the model.
    4. 16:46 Linton describes projections that filter or aggregate tool results before they enter the model context, using a SQL-like interface.
    5. 18:50 Linton explains how each task gets a hard token budget and iteration cap, with the remaining allowance available to the agent on each loop.

    Platform as a Product in a 300-Year-Old Bank

    Abby Bangser, Joel King

    Abby frames platform engineering as a shift toward self-service with guardrails and connects it to the CNCF white papers, maturity model, and an emerging marketplace model. Joel describes how NatWest used product thinking, Kubernetes CRDs, GitOps reconciliation, and clear ownership boundaries to cut EKS cluster provisioning from eight to twelve weeks to 35 minutes.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want a regulated-enterprise case study connecting platform-as-product theory to Kubernetes and GitOps implementation plus measured outcomes.

    1. 3:43 Joel says reuse failed when each application team copied and modified patterns, producing more than 135 variants, high cognitive load, configuration drift, and an eight-to-twelve-week path to a usable EKS cluster.
    2. 8:34 Joel says NatWest adopted platform as a product by exposing curated products through an abstracted API, using Git as the source of truth, reconciliation loops, and fleet management rather than team-by-team manual change.
    3. 11:13 Joel explains that Kratix provides the product API through Kubernetes CRDs, after which pipelines write validated desired state to Git for downstream reconciliation.
    4. 19:55 Joel says the automated EKS product reduced provisioning from eight to twelve weeks to 35 minutes while including the AWS account, networking connectivity, guardrails, and self-service request path.
    5. 23:30 Abby closes by arguing that platform teams must optimize for outcomes over outputs, composing contributions into products that give users self-service access rather than merely automating infrastructure.

    Platform Engineering's Missing Chapter: Services Organizations

    Atulpriya Sharma

    Atulpriya argues that platform engineering models built for product companies do not directly fit consultancies and services organizations. He presents delivery capital and a capability layer as ways to reuse organizational learning across client engagements while measuring ramp time, consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you build or operate platforms in a consultancy, systems integrator, or client-facing delivery organization.

    1. 2:59 Atulpriya argues that platform engineering's familiar vocabulary was not built around the operating model of consulting firms and services organizations.
    2. 9:03 Atulpriya defines delivery capital as the learning a services organization accumulates across engagements, and says it is the asset the organization truly owns.
    3. 11:04 Atulpriya says the capability layer joins reusable organizational knowledge with an internal consultant IDP of sandboxes and demo clusters so teams can apply prior learning.
    4. 12:57 Atulpriya maps the approach across cloud, IaC, CI/CD, observability, and monitoring, with artifacts such as decision records, checklists, templates, baselines, and named alert owners.
    5. 23:29 Atulpriya argues that ownership, rotation, and billable-work identity block adoption in services firms, and says time-to-ramp, delivery consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time are the measures to watch.
