    Adyen Joins CNCF: Staying Independent with Open Source

    Juliano Martinez

    Juliano Martinez announces that Adyen has joined CNCF as a Platinum End User member and explains why the payments company runs its whole platform in house on open source to avoid vendor lock-in. He asks companies to contribute reference architectures to the End User TAB and introduces kubectl-rexec, Adyen's open source tool for auditable Kubernetes sessions.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you run Kubernetes in a regulated industry and need every session on your clusters to be auditable.

    1. 0:00 Juliano Martinez announces that Adyen has joined CNCF as a Platinum End User member, and says open source runs underneath almost everything Adyen builds.
    2. 1:56 He says Adyen processed over 1.39 trillion euros last year across billions of transactions and runs on more than 100 Kubernetes clusters.
    3. 2:36 He says Adyen runs its whole platform in house in its own data centers, and relies on open standards and open source so it is never bound to a vendor's decisions.
    4. 4:03 He says he now sits on the CNCF End User Technical Advisory Board and asks companies of any size to contribute reference architectures.
    5. 4:36 He introduces kubectl-rexec, Adyen's open source tool that captures every session on Kubernetes, like a tee log, so access stays auditable in a regulated environment.
