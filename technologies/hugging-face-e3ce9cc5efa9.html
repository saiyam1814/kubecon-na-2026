    Compliance Drift on Kubernetes: Building Proof Infrastructure for AI Agents in Regulated Industries

    Saideep Navakoti

    Saideep Navakoti presents ACRA, a Kubernetes proof workflow intended to detect changes in AI-agent behavior and make the resulting investigations auditable. He shows how the demo displays investigation tickets and describes how auditors could query evidence drawn from traces.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: You need to show auditors what an AI agent was approved to do and what it actually did.

    1. 7:55 Navakoti explains that Kyverno checks pod admission and OpenTelemetry records traces, but argues that neither alone tells an operator whether an agent's behavior has drifted.
    2. 9:32 He introduces ACRA's API server, controller, and gate, then explains how its contract evaluator checks agent behavior against a declared baseline.
    3. 12:21 He shows a demo UI that calls the API to display investigation tickets, after describing how a drift event produces evidence from collected traces.
    4. 20:04 He frames drift as a reason to re-approve an agent rather than silently fix it, and walks through how a custom Kubernetes controller reports the agent's state.
    5. 21:32 He describes evidence an auditor can query for a specific task or incident, including a telemetry graph rather than a manually maintained spreadsheet.

    Free Tokens for Everyone

    William Morgan

    William Morgan says running open-weight models on Kubernetes shifts inference costs from buying tokens to operating GPU capacity. He presents Kinference as a layer for defining tenant policy, tracking usage, and routing requests to local or external backends.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: You run a Kubernetes platform and need to share inference capacity across teams without giving up visibility or control.

    1. 12:01 Morgan explains that choosing an open-weight model variant depends on available memory, context length, and expected concurrency, not just the model family.
    2. 14:39 Morgan frames shared GPU inference as a platform problem: teams want self-service and different levels of service while operators must keep finite capacity reliable.
    3. 18:12 Morgan describes putting a traffic enforcement gateway in front of model servers to handle multiple models and tenants with explicit policy.
    4. 21:01 Morgan says the gateway can export Prometheus-compatible metrics for existing dashboards and break out performance by model and tenant.
    5. 23:47 Morgan walks through policies that can choose a model, reject a request, or send it to an external provider using prompt, GPU, and tenant signals.

    From Pod to Actuator: Physical AI and the Future of Kubernetes

    Abhilash Somasamudramath

    Abhilash Somasamudramath introduces Quack, a robot duck he says learned to walk on a Kubernetes cluster. He explains why robotics needs separate computers for simulation, training, and on-device inference, and how NVIDIA has used Kubernetes to connect them.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you want to see a robot on stage and hear how Kubernetes could connect simulation, training, and on-device inference.

    1. 0:54 Somasamudramath introduces Quack, a robot duck from Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics, says it learned to walk on a Kubernetes cluster, and tries to get it to walk on stage.
    2. 2:00 Somasamudramath describes a robotics model that turns sensor inputs and instructions into action tokens, such as joint or steering vectors, rather than language tokens.
    3. 3:28 Somasamudramath breaks physical AI into three computers: one for simulating scenes, one for training models, and one on the robot for inference at the point of action.
    4. 4:37 Somasamudramath outlines three waves of physical AI compute: learning from captured real-world data, training in simulation, and using deployed fleets to feed further improvements.
    5. 6:06 Somasamudramath says NVIDIA contributed to KAI Scheduler and open-source Run:ai to help allocate GPUs and gang-schedule workloads across heterogeneous clusters.

    Groundhog Pod: Scaling AI Inference with Kata Pod Snapshots

    David Justice, Roaa Sakr

    David Justice and Roaa Sakr describe a prototype that snapshots an initialized Kata pod VM along with its GPU state. They report a faster restore than cold start in their demo and discuss where hardware compatibility can limit reuse of a snapshot.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and servingKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You are comparing pre-warmed GPU pods with restoring initialized workloads when inference demand spikes.

    1. 6:52 The speakers say a VM snapshot may fail to restore on a different CPU microarchitecture and describe using virtual CPU profiles to define a compatible feature set.
    2. 8:47 The speakers explain that CUDA checkpointing moves GPU state into the running application inside the VM, so the subsequent VM snapshot can capture that state too.
    3. 13:00 In the inference demo, the speakers terminate the original pod and show another pod restoring from its snapshot and returning a response.
    4. 14:15 For the demo on an NVIDIA Tesla T4, the speakers report about 34 seconds for the cold start and 11 seconds from a snapshot, which skipped the software and model loading steps.
    5. 20:02 In a second demo, the speakers plot the training loss and show it continuing from a restored snapshot after the training pod is interrupted.

    Minting Your Own Tokens: The Server Side of the Token Economy

    Suraj Deshmukh, Nilekh Chaudhari

    Suraj Deshmukh and Nilekh Chaudhari walk through vLLM deployments on Kubernetes, from a single GPU to a model split across two nodes. They compare throughput and first-token latency and explain how GPU memory and interconnects influence the deployment choices.

    80 min slot · Beginner · Tutorial

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: You want to deploy a model with vLLM and understand what changes when it no longer fits on one GPU.

    1. 15:18 The presenters inspect the running single-GPU deployment and show where the GPU request is specified in the vLLM configuration.
    2. 22:07 The presenters explain the difference between prefill, which processes the prompt and produces the first token, and decode, which generates the rest of the reply.
    3. 37:59 The presenters raise benchmark concurrency from one request to four and discuss why higher aggregate token throughput need not mean a shorter wait for each user.
    4. 53:21 The presenters animate how tensor-parallel workers exchange partial results at each layer and discuss why GPU interconnect speed matters.
    5. 1:07:01 The presenters introduce LeaderWorkerSet's leader and worker pattern before showing how they configure pipeline parallelism across two nodes.

    Securing the Agentic Future: Distributing LLM Weights and Sandboxed AI Agents via Harbor

    Chenyu Zhang, Yan Wang

    Chenyu Zhang and Yan Wang present Harbor as a shared registry for model weights and sandboxed agent images, with different checks for the two supply chains. They demonstrate importing a model from Hugging Face and mounting it in Kubernetes, then discuss declaring an agent's requested permissions alongside its image.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you want to distribute model weights through Harbor and review the permissions a sandboxed agent requests.

    1. 6:45 The speaker explains that ModelPack packages a model as an OCI artifact so a compliant registry can store it without tying distribution to a serving stack.
    2. 12:32 The speaker distinguishes model scanning from ordinary CVE scanning and describes signing model artifacts with Cosign as a separate supply-chain check.
    3. 17:33 The speaker shows a Harbor sync policy for a Hugging Face model, previews the source file list, and inspects the model card and license after the sync.
    4. 19:43 The speaker shows a Kubernetes deployment pointing at Harbor and says Kubernetes mounts the synced model files into the pod.
    5. 22:14 The speaker describes the Docker Sandbox Kit Spec as a declaration attached to the agent image that lists the network destinations, credentials, and paths the agent asks to use.
