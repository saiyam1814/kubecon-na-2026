    Open Policy Agent. (OPA) Intro & Deep Dive

    Sebastian Spaink, Johan Fylling

    Sebastian Spaink and Johan Fylling introduce Open Policy Agent as a general-purpose policy engine and Rego as its declarative policy language. They then cover this year's Rego changes, new Java and Swift evaluators built on OPA's intermediate representation, runtime improvements, and updates from Gatekeeper, opa-java-wasm and Conftest.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you write Rego policies and want to know about the new and, or and not semantics and the coming Rego v2.

    1. 0:53 Spaink describes the problem OPA targets: policy scattered across enforcement points owned by different teams, so nobody can easily answer what the policy is.
    2. 8:42 Fylling shows the new or keyword for inline disjunction, while still recommending helper rules to keep large logical expressions maintainable.
    3. 12:33 Fylling explains the pitfall where a not expression over an undefined input value stopped the whole rule, and how the compiler now wraps negations in closures.
    4. 17:14 Fylling says a Rego v2 import enables the new syntax per module, ahead of an OPA 2.0 release the team plans for next year.
    5. 20:06 Fylling presents Java and Swift evaluators that run OPA's intermediate representation inside applications as libraries, with bundle management and decision logging.

  • Platform Engineering Day

    Platform Engineering's Missing Chapter: Services Organizations

    Atulpriya Sharma

    Atulpriya argues that platform engineering models built for product companies do not directly fit consultancies and services organizations. He presents delivery capital and a capability layer as ways to reuse organizational learning across client engagements while measuring ramp time, consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you build or operate platforms in a consultancy, systems integrator, or client-facing delivery organization.

    1. 2:59 Atulpriya argues that platform engineering's familiar vocabulary was not built around the operating model of consulting firms and services organizations.
    2. 9:03 Atulpriya defines delivery capital as the learning a services organization accumulates across engagements, and says it is the asset the organization truly owns.
    3. 11:04 Atulpriya says the capability layer joins reusable organizational knowledge with an internal consultant IDP of sandboxes and demo clusters so teams can apply prior learning.
    4. 12:57 Atulpriya maps the approach across cloud, IaC, CI/CD, observability, and monitoring, with artifacts such as decision records, checklists, templates, baselines, and named alert owners.
    5. 23:29 Atulpriya argues that ownership, rotation, and billable-work identity block adoption in services firms, and says time-to-ramp, delivery consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time are the measures to watch.

    RBAC as a Platform Capability: Applying Platform Engineering to Kubernetes Access

    Sean Corcoran, Erin Walter

    Sean Corcoran and Erin Walter describe how PlayStation replaced a centralized namespace and access workflow with separate Kubernetes custom resources. They explain how a hub controller reconciles those resources across clusters and how the team transfers ownership from its old scheduled jobs.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: You manage Kubernetes access across many clusters and want application teams to own access requests separately from namespace configuration.

    1. 6:08 The speakers describe how chained pull requests repeated validations and then waited for a scheduled job to apply each change.
    2. 7:25 The speakers explain why they moved from one scheduled job per cluster to a controller in a hub cluster that applies changes downstream.
    3. 9:07 The speakers distinguish the resource that declares namespace configuration from the resource that declares who should have access and what permissions they receive.
    4. 11:38 The speakers walk through how the controller resolves a namespace reference and creates the corresponding role binding in the target cluster.
    5. 19:07 The speakers describe dry-run reconciliation that writes the generated manifest to resource status so they can inspect it before applying it downstream.

    Welcome to the Vulnpocalypse!

    Liz Rice, Adrian Mouat, Hannah Foxwell, Andrew Martin

    The panelists say AI-assisted discovery is surfacing more vulnerabilities than security teams can triage using severity labels alone. They discuss using platform context, deployment inventories, and emergency patch paths to decide what to fix first.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Panel

    Security, identity and policy

    Watch this if: You need to prioritize patches when vulnerability reports outnumber the issues your team can fix immediately.

    1. 1:40 The panel says AI-assisted vulnerability discovery has increased the volume of reports while teams have less enriched CVE data to help triage them.
    2. 6:41 A panelist recommends offering security controls as platform capabilities and teaching engineering teams how to use them.
    3. 10:00 The panel discusses keeping a bill of materials and an inventory of what's installed where, so teams can locate affected dependencies when a vulnerability is disclosed.
    4. 18:08 A panelist says teams can keep their normal update process while reserving a faster route for emergencies, depending on their risk tolerance.
    5. 24:55 A panelist says a medium-severity issue in a critical environment can matter more than a higher-severity one with little exposure, and explains why application reachability matters.
