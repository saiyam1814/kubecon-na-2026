    From Pod to Actuator: Physical AI and the Future of Kubernetes

    Abhilash Somasamudramath

    Abhilash Somasamudramath introduces Quack, a robot duck he says learned to walk on a Kubernetes cluster. He explains why robotics needs separate computers for simulation, training, and on-device inference, and how NVIDIA has used Kubernetes to connect them.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you want to see a robot on stage and hear how Kubernetes could connect simulation, training, and on-device inference.

    1. 0:54 Somasamudramath introduces Quack, a robot duck from Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics, says it learned to walk on a Kubernetes cluster, and tries to get it to walk on stage.
    2. 2:00 Somasamudramath describes a robotics model that turns sensor inputs and instructions into action tokens, such as joint or steering vectors, rather than language tokens.
    3. 3:28 Somasamudramath breaks physical AI into three computers: one for simulating scenes, one for training models, and one on the robot for inference at the point of action.
    4. 4:37 Somasamudramath outlines three waves of physical AI compute: learning from captured real-world data, training in simulation, and using deployed fleets to feed further improvements.
    5. 6:06 Somasamudramath says NVIDIA contributed to KAI Scheduler and open-source Run:ai to help allocate GPUs and gang-schedule workloads across heterogeneous clusters.
