    10 Years, 10 Lessons

    Ram Iyengar

    Ram Iyengar of the Linux Foundation shares ten lessons from a decade around platform engineering, covering abstractions, definitions, metrics, costs, feedback and ownership. He argues that platform users are engineers who want to see inside the abstraction, and closes by saying developer experience is turning into agent experience.

    10 min slot · Any Level · Lightning Talk

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you want an 11-minute list of one practitioner's platform lessons on abstractions, metrics, costs, feedback and ownership.

    1. 3:30 Iyengar says platform users are engineers who want to look inside an abstraction, and that handing one over to be used without examination creates friction with developer teams.
    2. 4:45 He says it is hard to agree on one definition of the platform, that it is more than a handful of tools put together, and that microservices got misread as letting every team pick any language.
    3. 7:01 On measurement, he asks whether time to first hello world or lead time for changes is the right metric, and whether 100% uptime counts as success if a database takes two days to get.
    4. 9:09 He calls feedback loops complaint boxes and suggests building a community instead: inner sourcing and growing platform advocates one team at a time.
    5. 10:15 He says developer experience has become agent experience, and closes with a bonus lesson 11: keep learning.

    Open Policy Agent. (OPA) Intro & Deep Dive

    Sebastian Spaink, Johan Fylling

    Sebastian Spaink and Johan Fylling introduce Open Policy Agent as a general-purpose policy engine and Rego as its declarative policy language. They then cover this year's Rego changes, new Java and Swift evaluators built on OPA's intermediate representation, runtime improvements, and updates from Gatekeeper, opa-java-wasm and Conftest.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you write Rego policies and want to know about the new and, or and not semantics and the coming Rego v2.

    1. 0:53 Spaink describes the problem OPA targets: policy scattered across enforcement points owned by different teams, so nobody can easily answer what the policy is.
    2. 8:42 Fylling shows the new or keyword for inline disjunction, while still recommending helper rules to keep large logical expressions maintainable.
    3. 12:33 Fylling explains the pitfall where a not expression over an undefined input value stopped the whole rule, and how the compiler now wraps negations in closures.
    4. 17:14 Fylling says a Rego v2 import enables the new syntax per module, ahead of an OPA 2.0 release the team plans for next year.
    5. 20:06 Fylling presents Java and Swift evaluators that run OPA's intermediate representation inside applications as libraries, with bundle management and decision logging.

    The Identity Crisis: Who is the AI Agent in Your Cluster?

    Abdel Sghiouar

    Abdel Sghiouar argues that agents sharing a Kubernetes service account are hard to distinguish when something goes wrong. He proposes short-lived SPIFFE identities, gateways for authorization, and sandboxing, with humans setting policy instead of approving every action.

    10 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you need to separate agent identity, authorization, and runtime isolation when designing a Kubernetes platform.

    1. 1:23 Sghiouar says it is hard to predict which tools or endpoints an agent will call, and argues that a shared Kubernetes service account makes it hard to trace an incident to a particular replica.
    2. 2:40 Sghiouar distinguishes agents acting for a human from agents triggered autonomously, and argues the latter need a unique, short-lived identity tied to their task.
    3. 3:29 Sghiouar proposes SPIFFE and SPIRE for giving agents short-lived identities at runtime, potentially scoped to a replica or session.
    4. 4:15 Sghiouar says identity does not answer authorization and discusses using a gateway to evaluate what an agent wants to do before it accesses an external resource.
    5. 8:09 Sghiouar urges platform engineers to move from human approval of each action to human-defined governance that sets boundaries for what agents can do.
