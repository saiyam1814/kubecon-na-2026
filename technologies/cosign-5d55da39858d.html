    Public Sector User Group BoF: Roadmap, White Papers, Our Group

    William Crum, Daniel Moch

    William Crum and Daniel Moch describe how the CNCF Public Sector User Group brings shared problems to the wider cloud-native community. They review the group's secure software delivery paper and ask for input on a planned paper about delivering software into disconnected environments.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Birds of a Feather

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you deliver cloud-native software into air-gapped environments and want to compare approaches with public-sector peers.

    1. 2:33 The speakers describe the user group as a way for people running cloud-native systems in production to identify common problems and bring them to CNCF advisory bodies.
    2. 6:31 The speakers say their first white paper covers trusted software delivery and how producers can package, sign, and attest software for public-sector consumers.
    3. 8:01 The speakers describe comparing how Hoppr and Droppr, Zarf, the Open Component Model, and Hauler approach software delivery into disconnected environments.
    4. 9:24 The speakers say the next white paper aims to give teams a shared vocabulary for air-gapped deployments, including the work of mirroring registries and sideloading images.
    5. 16:59 In the discussion, the speakers suggest starting in the user group and bringing the question to the Technical Advisory Board if it draws wider interest.

    Reduce vLLM Pod Cold Starts with KServe Kernel Caching

    Jooho lee, Vishwa Hiren Gandhi

    The speakers explain how GPU kernel compilation can slow vLLM startup even when model weights are already cached. They describe KServe's approach for sharing signed kernel caches across pods and report what changed in their benchmark.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: Your vLLM replicas scale slowly even after you have cached the model weights.

    1. 4:33 The speakers break down a baseline vLLM startup of about 250 seconds and say the compilation step accounts for roughly 35 seconds, excluding image pulls and model downloads.
    2. 5:38 The speakers explain why a new replica can compile kernels again even after another pod has compiled them: the new pod does not inherit the previous pod's local cache.
    3. 10:31 The speakers propose packaging compiled kernels as a signed OCI artifact in a registry so another node can verify the signer before making the cache available.
    4. 13:52 The speakers distinguish a workload fingerprint that identifies where a cache came from from a compatibility fingerprint that checks whether compilation settings match.
    5. 18:24 In one benchmark, the speakers report that reusing the cache cut startup from 160 to 110 seconds and compilation from about 35 seconds to just over one second.

    Securing the Agentic Future: Distributing LLM Weights and Sandboxed AI Agents via Harbor

    Chenyu Zhang, Yan Wang

    Chenyu Zhang and Yan Wang present Harbor as a shared registry for model weights and sandboxed agent images, with different checks for the two supply chains. They demonstrate importing a model from Hugging Face and mounting it in Kubernetes, then discuss declaring an agent's requested permissions alongside its image.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you want to distribute model weights through Harbor and review the permissions a sandboxed agent requests.

    1. 6:45 The speaker explains that ModelPack packages a model as an OCI artifact so a compliant registry can store it without tying distribution to a serving stack.
    2. 12:32 The speaker distinguishes model scanning from ordinary CVE scanning and describes signing model artifacts with Cosign as a separate supply-chain check.
    3. 17:33 The speaker shows a Harbor sync policy for a Hugging Face model, previews the source file list, and inspects the model card and license after the sync.
    4. 19:43 The speaker shows a Kubernetes deployment pointing at Harbor and says Kubernetes mounts the synced model files into the pod.
    5. 22:14 The speaker describes the Docker Sandbox Kit Spec as a declaration attached to the agent image that lists the network destinations, credentials, and paths the agent asks to use.

    Your CI's Mistaken Identity

    Andrew McNamara, Maia Iyer

    Andrew McNamara and Maia Iyer say a namespace and service account can tell you where a CI task runs without telling you what it should be allowed to do. They describe pairing per-task workload identities with admission-time policy and using those identities to authorize access and sign attestations.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Delivery, GitOps and release safetySecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: You want to avoid giving every task in a Kubernetes CI pipeline the same privileges.

    1. 4:30 The speakers explain that SPIRE can use more than namespace and service account when assigning workload identities, so CI pods in the same namespace can have different roles.
    2. 9:19 The speakers explain that a Tekton bundle's digest changes when its task definition changes, and show where Sigstore signatures and attestations can be stored.
    3. 14:14 The speakers describe using Kyverno admission policy to require extra verification before a task can get a production identity.
    4. 16:57 In the live demo, the speakers show that an unsigned task from an untrusted catalog was blocked before a pod was scheduled.
    5. 22:21 The speakers explain how task-scoped identities let a verification policy check not just for an attestation, but for the specific CI task expected to produce it.
