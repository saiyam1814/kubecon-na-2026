    One Workload to Rule Them All: Workload Aware Scheduling for Distributed AI Training on Kubernetes

    Andrey Velichkevich

    Andrey Velichkevich explains why scheduling distributed training one pod at a time can deadlock jobs and how the Kubernetes Workload and PodGroup APIs model them as a unit. He shows how Kubeflow Trainer exposes those capabilities through training runtimes and compares three scheduling policies on a local cluster.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: You run distributed training on Kubernetes and want to understand when pod groups and topology constraints matter.

    1. 0:48 Velichkevich illustrates how two training jobs can deadlock when the scheduler gives each job some GPUs but neither gets enough to start all its ranks.
    2. 4:44 Velichkevich distinguishes the Workload template from runtime PodGroups and shows how a PodGroup can express a minimum pod count and topology constraints.
    3. 7:10 Velichkevich introduces composite PodGroups for workloads with a hierarchy, where a parent can require a minimum number of child groups before scheduling proceeds.
    4. 10:12 Velichkevich explains the Kubeflow Trainer split: platform admins define runtime blueprints, and scientists use a Python SDK to submit jobs without managing Kubernetes deployment details.
    5. 15:17 In a local kind demo, Velichkevich sets up a comparison of default pod-by-pod placement, gang scheduling, and rack-constrained placement for a PyTorch job.

    WG-Batch Updates: What’s New and What Is Next?

    Amy Chen, Yuki Iwai, Marcin Wielgus

    Marcin Wielgus, Amy Chen and Yuki Iwai give an update on the Kubernetes Batch Working Group and its projects. They cover new Kueue features such as concurrent admission, configurable preemption, topology-aware spreading, the dynamic quota orchestrator and partial scale-up, then Job, JobSet and workload-aware scheduling updates.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run AI training or batch jobs on Kubernetes and want to know what changed in Kueue, Job and JobSet.

    1. 3:46 Wielgus says that since last November Kueue shipped 54 releases, while PRs, issues and contributors each more than doubled.
    2. 4:45 Wielgus explains concurrent admission, where virtual copies of a workload keep trying for cheaper reserved capacity and move the job there once it frees up.
    3. 12:36 Wielgus introduces topology-aware spreading for inference, which caps the share of serving capacity placed in one rack, for example at 45%.
    4. 14:27 Chen describes the dynamic quota orchestrator, which discovers real capacity from node pools or MultiKueue worker clusters and adjusts nominal quota automatically.
    5. 28:54 Iwai explains that Kueue is adopting a shared scheduler library from kube-scheduler so its node feasibility checks match what kube-scheduler will accept.

    WG-Workload Aware Scheduling: An Introduction

    Kevin Hannon, Andrey Velichkevich, Heba Elayoty, Matt Matejczyk

    The speakers explain why the Kubernetes working group is moving from scheduling individual pods to scheduling whole workloads. They describe the APIs, controller integrations, and roadmap for bringing placement, preemption, and resource sharing together.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you maintain a workload controller or want to understand the Kubernetes workload scheduling roadmap.

    1. 2:44 The speakers frame the new APIs around gang scheduling, topology constraints, and preemption at the workload level.
    2. 4:00 They distinguish a PodGroup, which groups pods with shared scheduling requirements, from a CompositePodGroup, which groups other groups.
    3. 8:45 They describe integrations with out-of-tree workload controllers such as JobSet and LeaderWorkerSet.
    4. 20:27 They describe a read-only scheduler library that lets controllers ask what would happen if a workload were placed.
    5. 26:13 They outline a goal of graduating the core APIs and scheduling capabilities in Kubernetes 1.39, with performance and hardening work still ahead.
