    Managing Data at Scale: Best Practices and Evolution of SIG-Apps

    Maciej Szulik, Janet Kuo

    SIG Apps leads Maciej Szulik and Janet Kuo review recent and in-progress changes to the core Kubernetes workload APIs, including Job failure policy, workload-aware (gang) scheduling in the Job and Deployment controllers, and StatefulSet rollout options, noting which may miss the 1.38 code freeze. Kuo then covers the SIG's subprojects for batch, AI inference and agents, among them JobSet, LeaderWorkerSet, Agent Sandbox and an MCP lifecycle operator, plus a new workload conformance working group.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesData, storage and stateAccelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run Jobs, StatefulSets or AI workloads on Kubernetes and want to know what SIG Apps is changing and which subprojects it has added.

    1. 4:09 Maciej Szulik explains gang scheduling, where a group of pods is scheduled together rather than one at a time, and says the Deployment controller is the second controller getting workload-aware scheduling after the Job controller.
    2. 6:46 He describes a long-standing StatefulSet complaint, that a broken rollout is not repaired automatically and pods must be fixed by hand, and the recreate update strategy introduced to partly address it.
    3. 13:31 Janet Kuo presents Agent Sandbox, the subproject she leads for isolated, stateful singleton workloads such as AI agents and code execution, and says it reached a 1.0 release with about 180 contributors.
    4. 17:46 She introduces a new subproject for maintenance windows, because a PodDisruptionBudget only counts pods and has no notion of when disruption is acceptable, and mentions a workload conformance working group.
    5. 25:33 Asked how upstream gang scheduling relates to Kueue and KAI Scheduler, Szulik says the aim is for those projects to reuse the new primitives, and that the API must be enabled explicitly until it reaches v1.

    One Workload to Rule Them All: Workload Aware Scheduling for Distributed AI Training on Kubernetes

    Andrey Velichkevich

    Andrey Velichkevich explains why scheduling distributed training one pod at a time can deadlock jobs and how the Kubernetes Workload and PodGroup APIs model them as a unit. He shows how Kubeflow Trainer exposes those capabilities through training runtimes and compares three scheduling policies on a local cluster.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: You run distributed training on Kubernetes and want to understand when pod groups and topology constraints matter.

    1. 0:48 Velichkevich illustrates how two training jobs can deadlock when the scheduler gives each job some GPUs but neither gets enough to start all its ranks.
    2. 4:44 Velichkevich distinguishes the Workload template from runtime PodGroups and shows how a PodGroup can express a minimum pod count and topology constraints.
    3. 7:10 Velichkevich introduces composite PodGroups for workloads with a hierarchy, where a parent can require a minimum number of child groups before scheduling proceeds.
    4. 10:12 Velichkevich explains the Kubeflow Trainer split: platform admins define runtime blueprints, and scientists use a Python SDK to submit jobs without managing Kubernetes deployment details.
    5. 15:17 In a local kind demo, Velichkevich sets up a comparison of default pod-by-pod placement, gang scheduling, and rack-constrained placement for a PyTorch job.

    OptimizationJob: Hyperparameter Optimization at Scale with Kubeflow Trainer

    Aniket Shaha, Ricardo Aravena

    The speakers present OptimizationJob, a new Kubeflow Trainer API that they say runs hyperparameter tuning with each trial as a TrainJob and no second system to install. They explain how it grew out of Katib, play a recorded demo, and list the limits of the first alpha phase and the roadmap.

    20 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: You tune hyperparameters on Kubernetes and want to know what the new OptimizationJob API in Kubeflow Trainer does today and what it leaves out.

    1. 3:13 The speaker says Katib works but means two installs, two APIs and metrics that live outside the job.
    2. 3:51 The speaker says the change that made this possible was putting training metrics in the TrainJob status, so tuning can live inside Trainer.
    3. 12:13 The speaker says trials are just TrainJobs, so they reuse runtimes, JobSet and gang scheduling, and each trial is queued by Kueue like any other.
    4. 13:04 The speaker says phase one is small on purpose, with random search, one objective, alpha feature gates that are off by default, and YAML only.
    5. 14:17 The speaker lays out the roadmap in three buckets, which are smarter search, more resiliency and a Python SDK.

    SIG Instrumentation Introduction and Deep Dive

    David Ashpole, Catherine Fang, Stephen Lang

    The three maintainers describe what Kubernetes SIG Instrumentation owns, from the metrics, logging and tracing frameworks to subprojects such as kube-state-metrics and the newly adopted kubernetes-mixin. They walk through the past year's changes, including structured z-pages, Resource State Metrics and native histograms, then list next year's plans and the subprojects where they say they need contributors.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Observability and reliabilityKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you run Kubernetes monitoring and want the maintainers' view of what changed in metrics, logs and traces and where the SIG wants help.

    1. 4:10 Catherine Fang presents the statusz and flagz z-pages, which she says expose a component's binary version and effective flags on its serving port and are gaining structured output formats this year.
    2. 8:02 Catherine introduces Resource State Metrics, a new component that she says uses CEL-style expressions to read any field of a custom resource without writing Go code in kube-state-metrics.
    3. 9:00 Stephen Lang says kubernetes-mixin is new to SIG Instrumentation and describes work to cut alert fatigue and to tie mixin releases to Kubernetes versions with a scrape allow list and a version support matrix.
    4. 10:27 David Ashpole says Prometheus native histograms are now beta and on by default in Kubernetes, claims they cost about ten times less than classic histograms, and notes users must enable them in Prometheus and update queries.
    5. 18:52 David says Prometheus Adapter has no maintainers, so the SIG is formally deprecating it and encouraging users to migrate to KEDA.

    Signed, Sealed, Denied: Attestation-Driven Admission with Kyverno + CEL at AT&T

    Chad Swenson, Sagar Kundral

    Chad Swenson of AT&T argues that a signature alone is not a deployment approval and describes a platform design where GitHub Actions emits Sigstore-backed attestations and Kyverno's ImageValidatingPolicy checks them against each environment's rules at admission. Sagar Kundral of Nirmata demos the policies on a live cluster and presents Kyverno 1.19 and 1.20 changes, benchmark numbers and advice on exceptions, failure behavior, evidence retention and rollout.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Security, identity and policyDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you sign images already and want to see how attestations can drive per-environment admission decisions that you can later explain to an auditor.

    1. 3:38 Chad says a valid signature and provenance are useful but neither is a deployment approval, so the platform must still ask whether trusted claims meet the destination's requirements.
    2. 5:15 Chad gives an example where the same image digest is admitted in development on provenance alone but denied in production because it lacks a signed SBOM attestation.
    3. 13:51 Chad says verifying only against GitHub's private Sigstore instance broke down for upstream and vendor images with other trust roots, and credits a fix released in Kyverno 1.19 after he raised it with the lead maintainer.
    4. 17:28 Sagar runs a scripted demo on a live cluster showing verification with a signer workflow identity, an unattested image blocked, a production denial for missing code reviews, a policy exception in dev, and an org ID pin.
    5. 24:38 Sagar presents benchmark numbers from a kind cluster where he says p50 admission latency fell from 990 ms to 96 ms and registry calls from 14 to 3 in Kyverno 1.20.

    WG-Batch Updates: What’s New and What Is Next?

    Amy Chen, Yuki Iwai, Marcin Wielgus

    Marcin Wielgus, Amy Chen and Yuki Iwai give an update on the Kubernetes Batch Working Group and its projects. They cover new Kueue features such as concurrent admission, configurable preemption, topology-aware spreading, the dynamic quota orchestrator and partial scale-up, then Job, JobSet and workload-aware scheduling updates.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run AI training or batch jobs on Kubernetes and want to know what changed in Kueue, Job and JobSet.

    1. 3:46 Wielgus says that since last November Kueue shipped 54 releases, while PRs, issues and contributors each more than doubled.
    2. 4:45 Wielgus explains concurrent admission, where virtual copies of a workload keep trying for cheaper reserved capacity and move the job there once it frees up.
    3. 12:36 Wielgus introduces topology-aware spreading for inference, which caps the share of serving capacity placed in one rack, for example at 45%.
    4. 14:27 Chen describes the dynamic quota orchestrator, which discovers real capacity from node pools or MultiKueue worker clusters and adjusts nominal quota automatically.
    5. 28:54 Iwai explains that Kueue is adopting a shared scheduler library from kube-scheduler so its node feasibility checks match what kube-scheduler will accept.

    WG-Workload Aware Scheduling: An Introduction

    Kevin Hannon, Andrey Velichkevich, Heba Elayoty, Matt Matejczyk

    The speakers explain why the Kubernetes working group is moving from scheduling individual pods to scheduling whole workloads. They describe the APIs, controller integrations, and roadmap for bringing placement, preemption, and resource sharing together.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you maintain a workload controller or want to understand the Kubernetes workload scheduling roadmap.

    1. 2:44 The speakers frame the new APIs around gang scheduling, topology constraints, and preemption at the workload level.
    2. 4:00 They distinguish a PodGroup, which groups pods with shared scheduling requirements, from a CompositePodGroup, which groups other groups.
    3. 8:45 They describe integrations with out-of-tree workload controllers such as JobSet and LeaderWorkerSet.
    4. 20:27 They describe a read-only scheduler library that lets controllers ask what would happen if a workload were placed.
    5. 26:13 They outline a goal of graduating the core APIs and scheduling capabilities in Kubernetes 1.39, with performance and hardening work still ahead.
