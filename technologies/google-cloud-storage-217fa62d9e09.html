    Frameworkless ML Serving: Scaling to Thousands of Models with KEDA, Pub/Sub, and Native K8s

    Robin Deaconu, Neb Jovanovic

    The presenters explain why multi-minute drug-discovery jobs led their team away from a general-purpose inference framework. They describe how stateless workers, durable queues, and native Kubernetes scheduling serve interactive and batch requests on the same fleet.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and serving

    Watch this if: interactive inference and long-running batch jobs need to share the same pool of accelerators.

    1. 6:01 The presenters explain why multi-minute accelerator jobs made durable requests and backlog-based scaling more useful to them than in-flight concurrency.
    2. 9:12 They describe how a stateless worker backed by an asynchronous queue became the core building block of the new platform.
    3. 16:29 They explain how KEDA wakes a deployment from zero replicas and passes queue signals to the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler for further scaling.
    4. 18:46 They walk through high-priority requests preempting batch workers and describe how queue leases let interrupted work become available again.
    5. 27:52 They describe dual-writing to Redis and Bigtable to verify parity before cutting over with a configuration flag they could flip back.

    Kubernetes Data Protection WG Intro & Deep Dive

    Xing Yang, Dave Smith-Uchida

    Xing Yang and Dave Smith-Uchida explain why the Kubernetes Data Protection Working Group formed and walk through the backup and restore building blocks it drives, including COSI, snapshot topology, changed block tracking and volume group snapshots. Smith-Uchida then presents two white papers in progress, on data protection best practices and on recovering from ransomware attacks.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Data, storage and stateKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you back up stateful workloads on Kubernetes or are planning how to recover from a ransomware attack.

    1. 0:53 Yang says GitOps can recreate manifests, but secrets and data in persistent volumes are not in Git, which is why the group builds Kubernetes-native data protection blocks.
    2. 5:47 Yang highlights COSI v1alpha2 support for one bucket access covering multiple buckets with read-write, write-only and read-only modes.
    3. 8:40 Yang explains changed block tracking, which lets backup vendors copy only the blocks that changed between two snapshots through a new CSI snapshot metadata service.
    4. 14:08 Smith-Uchida describes ransomware as an adversarial problem where the control plane and backups may be compromised and must be verified before restoring.
    5. 15:52 Smith-Uchida recommends immutable backups, such as S3 compliance mode, and moving backup tools from static API keys to short-lived tokens obtained via OIDC.

    Pods Are Not Enough for AI Agents, Meet Agent-substrate

    Christian Posta

    Christian Posta says long-lived agent sessions need an identity and state that can outlast any one Kubernetes pod. He demonstrates how Agent Substrate suspends and resumes actors on shared workers and controls their access to outside services.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI agents and automationKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You need to run agents that pause and resume without keeping one Kubernetes pod per session.

    1. 8:16 Posta says an actor's identity and policy should be independent of the pod so the actor can keep its state when it isn't running.
    2. 11:06 Posta explains that an actor's identity persists for the actor's lifetime and that network calls from the sandbox require policy approval.
    3. 18:22 Posta suspends one actor in the demo, freeing enough capacity in the same worker for another actor to run.
    4. 22:22 Posta explains how agentgateway can check egress policy and attach a credential outside the sandbox instead of handing the secret to the actor.
    5. 28:47 Posta distinguishes Agent Substrate's actor runtime from kagent, which can use conversation and user context to decide when to suspend or resume an actor.

    Untrusted Code on Secure Runtimes: Agent Sandboxes on Kubernetes

    Mofi Rahman

    Mofi Rahman leads a hands-on lab on GKE, with four Google colleagues helping attendees in the room, and explains that it uses the newly open-sourced Agent Substrate where the abstract promised Agent Sandbox. Attendees create workers, actor templates and actors, suspend and resume them with their state, allow a model call through an egress policy and run 20 actors on two workers, and he says internal benchmarks reached more than 100 million actors.

    80 min slot · Beginner · Tutorial

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policyKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you want to follow a full lab on suspending and resuming agent workloads on Kubernetes and see how actors differ from one pod per sandbox.

    1. 21:39 Rahman explains why the lab switched projects: Agent Sandbox maps a sandbox to a pod with warm pools, while Agent Substrate adds actors, many per pod, and he suggests Sandbox for agents that run for days and Substrate for short, bursty sessions.
    2. 25:22 He lists Substrate's parts: an API server, Postgres in place of etcd, which he says could not scale to the 100 million actor goal, egress control, and a router that proxies requests so actors do not each need an IP address.
    3. 36:15 He suspends an actor, sends it a request and gets an answer anyway, explaining that the router holds the request while the actor resumes from its snapshot mid-state, unlike Cloud Run, Lambda or Knative starting from zero.
    4. 49:14 A model call from the actor fails because egress is denied by default, and it succeeds after he creates an egress gateway and a policy that allows the one model URL.
    5. 57:09 A third actor times out on two full workers, and he stresses that Substrate does not decide when to suspend: the platform team defines idle, here three seconds without a request, after which 20 actors share two workers.
