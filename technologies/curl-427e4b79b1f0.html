    Four Resources, One Object: Surviving Gateway API After Ingress NGINX

    Sitesh Pattanaik

    Pattanaik demonstrates how replacing Ingress NGINX annotations with separate Gateway API and Envoy Gateway objects can leave route policies out of sync. He shows how his example uses a resource graph to manage those objects together and compares that approach with other ways to manage their lifecycle.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Networking and connectivityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you are migrating from Ingress NGINX and need a way to keep routes and their policies from drifting apart.

    1. 6:19 Pattanaik identifies the four resources in his example: an HTTPRoute plus security, backend traffic, and client traffic policies.
    2. 10:43 He explains that a policy's target reference does not tell the route about the policy or require it to share the route's lifetime.
    3. 20:47 He describes the platform team owning a ResourceGraphDefinition while an application team supplies inputs such as the hostname.
    4. 27:41 He shows that the graph manages the route and its authorization policy together when he switches to a replacement resource.
    5. 29:13 He compares Helm, kro, Crossplane, and a custom operator, asking which component will manage the full resource graph as a unit.

    Hidden Pod Startup Latency: Container Image Pull

    Omer Oz

    Omer Oz describes how LinkedIn investigated slow pod startups on its bare-metal fleet and traced most of the time to unpacking container image layers, not downloading them. He walks through the kubelet and containerd settings his team changed, the results he reports, and further options such as zstd compression, the EROFS snapshotter and peer-to-peer caching.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: large container images slow your pod startups and you want a mental model of the pull path plus settings to try.

    1. 9:12 Oz describes the trigger: Flink apps shipping about 28 GB of images against a 20-minute pod startup SLO, which he says caused a series of incidents at LinkedIn.
    2. 14:46 Oz walks through an image pull timeline and says the download finishes early while most time goes to unpacking, which containerd does one layer at a time by default.
    3. 15:26 Oz explains LinkedIn's fix of raising the kubelet's parallel image pulls to three and lowering containerd's download concurrency, then shows the latency results he measured.
    4. 21:46 Oz presents the EROFS snapshotter for containerd, which he says skips eager unpacking and cut unpack time to under 0.4 seconds in his tests.
    5. 23:57 Oz describes a serialization point he found in containerd that capped total download throughput, and points to his pull request removing it.

    Mythos vs Maintainers: Drowning in the Vulnerability Tsunami

    Fabian Kammel, John Kjell

    Fabian Kammel, speaking as a security researcher, and John Kjell, speaking as a maintainer of in-toto's Witness, walk through finding, reporting, triaging, fixing and following up on a vulnerability at a time when they say AI models let anyone file reports that are hard to tell from real ones. They give recommendations for each side in three levels, from steps to adopt today to actions meant to help the wider ecosystem, and ask for empathy toward maintainers, whom they call the bottleneck.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Security, identity and policyCommunity and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you maintain an open source project or report vulnerabilities to one and want both sides' concrete steps for handling AI-assisted security reports.

    1. 3:45 Kjell says AI-generated reports were easy to spot and close a year ago, but that newer models let novices submit reports that look real and take a lot of effort to verify, and that curl and bug bounty programs have pulled back as a result.
    2. 8:25 Kammel says the first thing he does for AI-assisted research is build a sandbox, preferring micro VMs over sandboxes that share the host kernel, and that researchers should understand and reproduce a model's finding before reporting it.
    3. 10:40 Kjell advises maintainers to turn on GitHub's private vulnerability reporting and publish a security policy with a response time frame, report expectations, supported versions, scope and a threat model in the repository.
    4. 14:34 Kammel lists what a researcher's report should do: follow the project's security policy, check for duplicates, cut the model's text down, and propose a severity score, affected versions and a weakness type.
    5. 24:53 Kjell shows that a GitHub temporary private fork has no Actions tab, and says the Witness maintainers keep their own detached fork so that fixes can run through CI before release.

    2. 12:14 Santana shows a kro ResourceGraphDefinition and explains how a declarative resource graph can give platform users a custom API without requiring the platform team to write a Go controller.

    Why Are You Still Wasting Whole GPUs? Hardware-Level Sharing, Scheduled with DRA

    Engin Diri

    Engin Diri of Pulumi recounts helping a European customer build a self-hosted inference platform, moving from whole GPUs and time slicing to NVIDIA MIG slices that pods request through Dynamic Resource Allocation. He shows a recorded demo of MIG profiles, resource slices and claims on an AWS node serving a model behind an inference gateway, and lists open questions such as quotas, models larger than a node and start-up ordering.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run inference on your own GPUs and want a practical walk through MIG slices requested with Dynamic Resource Allocation.

    1. 8:32 Diri says time slicing gives no memory or fault isolation between workloads, so the choice was between wasting GPUs and risking a noisy neighbor.
    2. 11:24 Diri says Dynamic Resource Allocation, which he dates to Kubernetes 1.34, lets a request describe device attributes instead of an integer count.
    3. 22:16 In the recorded demo, Diri lists the 1G, 2G and 4G MIG profiles on the node and the resource slices the DRA driver published.
    4. 24:28 Diri lists open day-two questions he is evaluating with the customer, including Kueue for MIG quota and which workload runs first.
    5. 28:06 Diri advises starting the DRA driver only after MIG is configured, because he ran into race conditions between the two.
