    Explore TAG Workloads Foundation: Core Runtime, Batch Scheduling, and Moar

    Stephen Rust, Yuan Tang, Marlow Warnicke, Rajas Kakodkar, Paco Xu

    Five leads of the CNCF TAG Workloads Foundation explain what a technical advisory group does, what this one covers, and how its batch subproject and white paper initiatives run. They then walk through graduated, incubating and sandbox projects they place in the TAG's scope and describe how newcomers can join meetings, reviews and initiatives.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoptionKubernetes internals and runtimesAccelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you maintain or use a workload, runtime or scheduling project and want to know what the TAG does and how to take part.

    1. 2:10 Stephen Rust describes a TAG as a technical advisory group that supports CNCF projects and says it is a way to scale advice across many projects with few people.
    2. 5:13 Rust says initiatives are time-bound efforts with a deliverable such as a white paper, while subprojects carry longer-term work, and that the TAG helps projects with their maturity reviews.
    3. 10:23 Marlow Warnicke describes the batch subproject's scheduling capabilities white paper, which she says aims to compare schedulers under different workload mixes, and asks for contributors.
    4. 14:15 Paco Xu lists graduated projects he places in the TAG's scope and says Karmada and Kubeflow graduated this year and KubeVirt graduated recently.
    5. 19:07 Rajas Kakodkar says no single role or expertise is needed to get involved, and suggests joining meetings, sharing pain points, demoing a project or reviewing white papers.

    From Citadel to Contract: Migrating a Monolith with 500+ Services to Kubernetes

    Holly Mooneyham

    Holly Mooneyham describes how Cisco Meraki started with Kubernetes outposts and then designed a platform contract for migrating parts of its monolith. She explains how the team moved workloads incrementally across two platforms and what she learned about ownership, early adopters, and retiring the old system.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Kubernetes internals and runtimesPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you are moving a mature monolith to Kubernetes and need to decide what the platform owns.

    1. 5:33 Mooneyham says Meraki initially left the Citadel monolith alone and put early services that did not need to sit close to the databases in separate Kubernetes outposts.
    2. 9:01 Mooneyham defines a platform contract as agreed promises, interfaces, and ownership boundaries, including who takes responsibility when a team leaves the preferred path.
    3. 12:58 Mooneyham shows the Elastic Shard deployment flow and explains why even a shared Helm chart did not settle where teams should draw the ownership boundary.
    4. 17:20 Mooneyham says the deployment system lets teams move workloads between the legacy and Kubernetes platforms by service, by shard, or by both, using capacity needs and service-owner intent.
    5. 22:35 Mooneyham says retiring the old platform is part of finishing the migration, not a task to leave indefinitely after the new platform goes live.

    When Infrastructure Breaks in Production: How EarnIn Brought Testing to the Full CNCF Stack

    Priya Namasivayam

    Priya Namasivayam of EarnIn describes a cert-manager upgrade that looked healthy but broke Linkerd certificates and customer logins, and says the team responded by running infrastructure tests as Kubernetes resources with Testkube, triggered by Flux reconciliation. A co-presenter from Testkube runs a live demo on a kind cluster in which an operator upgrade passes health checks but fails a test workflow.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Delivery, GitOps and release safetyObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you upgrade cluster add-ons through GitOps and want to catch breakage that pod health checks do not show.

    1. 3:48 The speaker explains the incident: she says cert-manager 1.18 began rotating private keys on renewal instead of extending them, which broke certificates for Linkerd while everything looked green.
    2. 5:26 The speaker describes the fix as a Kubernetes-native testing setup built on Testkube, where tests and triggers are CRDs and a Git commit that starts Flux reconciliation also starts the tests.
    3. 10:35 The co-presenter starts the live demo by bringing up a kind cluster with Flux, CloudNativePG, cert-manager and a sample to-do application.
    4. 12:42 The co-presenter shows a Testkube test workflow for cert-manager that checks certificates are still valid after an upgrade.
    5. 20:49 After the simulated upgrade the test workflow fails, and the co-presenter explains that the operator is running but a breaking change stops the ServiceMonitor from working.
