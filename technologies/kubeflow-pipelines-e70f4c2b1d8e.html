    A Decade of AI on Kubernetes: Kubeflow’s Journey to CNCF Graduation

    Valentina Rodriguez Sosa, Alexander Perlman, Christian Heusel, Shivay Lamba, David Aronchick

    Kubeflow co-founder David Aronchick recounts how the project started, and contributors describe its components, governance and what they say CNCF graduation required. The speakers then outline planned work, including Kubeflow Pipelines 3.0 changes such as removing the ML Metadata service, a unified Python SDK, trainer and notebook features, an MCP server and experimental Helm charts for the community distribution.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you run or contribute to Kubeflow and want the origin story, what graduation involved, and the maintainers' stated plans for Pipelines 3.0, the SDK and agent support.

    1. 2:30 David Aronchick says the first Kubeflow release was deliberately small, with only a Jupyter notebook, a TensorFlow CRD and what later became KServe, and that pipelines came about six months later.
    2. 12:22 Christian Heusel says Kubeflow reached CNCF graduated status in July 2026, and that graduation required maintainers from at least two organizations per sub-project, independent adopters and a security and governance review.
    3. 20:56 Alexander Perlman says Kubeflow Pipelines 3.0 will consolidate the per-step driver pods into one pod per run, roughly halving the pod count, using Argo Workflows executor plugins.
    4. 22:13 He says 3.0 removes the ML Metadata microservice and database and moves execution tracking, artifacts, lineage and caching into the Pipelines API server.
    5. 29:24 Shivay Lamba describes an experimental Kubeflow MCP server that exposes trainer jobs to agents while applying the user's own Kubernetes permissions so the agent stays within bounds.

    Building Reproducible AgentOps Pipelines with Kubeflow and MLflow

    Alyssa Goins, Ana Biazetti

    The speakers say multi-turn agent loops drive up inference cost and propose a repeatable model compression pipeline as one answer. They demo a Kubeflow Pipelines workflow that serves a model with KServe, benchmarks it, compresses it and benchmarks it again, with the runs tracked in MLflow.

    13 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    AI agents and automationAI inference and servingDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: You want a worked example of a Kubeflow Pipelines run that compresses a model and compares benchmarks before and after.

    1. 1:04 The speaker proposes a continuous optimization flow that compresses models with pipelines, which she says cuts hardware footprint and latency.
    2. 4:25 The speaker lists the pipeline steps, which are ingesting the base model, a baseline evaluation, compression, deployment with KServe and a final validation.
    3. 6:36 The speaker shows the compression step, which she says uses GPTQ quantization with a W8A8 scheme and outputs a metrics artifact and the compressed model.
    4. 9:27 The speaker says the 2.17 release of Kubeflow Pipelines added an MLflow plugin that uploads pipeline runs to MLflow automatically.
    5. 11:13 The speaker reports that the compressed model had three times faster time to first token, double the request throughput and 37% of the memory footprint.

    From Consumers to Contributors: How Growing Open Source Members Transformed Our ML Platform Team

    Tolga Tuncoglu, Herik Webb

    The speakers, who lead ML training platforms at Capital One, describe how their teams went from maintaining internal forks of Kubeflow Pipelines to fixing problems upstream and holding maintainer roles in the project. They share the three changes they made, the contribution numbers they attribute to the shift, and examples from Argo Workflows, OAuth2 Proxy and Notebook Intelligence.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Community and open source adoptionPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: You run a platform built on open source projects you only consume and want to hear how one enterprise team made the case for contributing upstream.

    1. 3:58 Tolga Tuncoglu says that after Kubeflow Pipelines moved to v2 their tenants were not ready, so the team kept internal forks and struggled to keep them in sync with upstream.
    2. 5:32 He describes a high-severity protobuf CVE as the turning point: they fixed the upstream CI for the legacy version, shipped a release there and stopped maintaining the fork.
    3. 7:46 Tuncoglu lists the three things they changed: time for upstream work in sprints, asking whether a fix can go upstream first, and recognizing upstream contributions.
    4. 12:15 He says their share of human-merged pull requests in the project went from 5% to 48%, and that 71% of pull requests to release branches come from Capital One.
    5. 16:50 Herik Webb says they chose Netflix's Notebook Intelligence to add AI tooling to their Jupyter platform, talked to its maintainers and contributed 242 pull requests over six months.

    Many Clusters, One GPU Pool: Locality-Aware Scheduling and Data Delivery With Kueue and Dragonfly

    Yiqing Wang, Adam Buran

    Yiqing Wang and Adam Buran describe how Roblox shares GPU capacity across clusters using Kueue while leaving each cluster to check its own capacity. They explain how a locality-aware dispatcher chooses clusters and how Dragonfly caches training data and checkpoints near the jobs that use them.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingData, storage and state

    Watch this if: You operate GPU clusters in more than one location and want to share capacity without repeatedly pulling the same datasets across regions.

    1. 6:55 The speakers describe two levels of MultiKueue admission: the hub checks team quota, then the candidate clusters check node capacity and the first to admit the job runs it with KubeRay.
    2. 8:15 The speakers explain how the dispatcher first filters for requirements such as GPU type and full-job fit, then prefers the data region for a limited time before considering other regions.
    3. 9:19 The speakers explain how Roblox separates pool-wide guaranteed quota from cluster-level borrowing and uses preemption to reclaim borrowed GPUs when a guaranteed job arrives.
    4. 14:51 The speakers describe Dragonfly looking for file pieces in the local cache, other peers, and seed peers before falling back to Amazon S3.
    5. 15:55 The speakers report that half of the data served came from node-local cache and another 43 percent from peers in the same cluster, leaving 7 percent served from Amazon S3.

    Zero to Maintainer in 45 Days: An AI-Assisted Migration of a CNCF Project's Legacy Frontend

    Jeff Spahr

    The speaker, who says he is not a frontend developer, describes how he used AI coding agents to migrate the aging Kubeflow Pipelines frontend off deprecated dependencies, starting from a wish to remove vulnerabilities. He walks through the checks he used to earn maintainers' trust, from adding tests before each change to a pixel-by-pixel visual comparison, and how he became a frontend approver after 45 days and later a maintainer.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Community and open source adoptionAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: You have a daunting migration in an open source project's backlog and want to see how one contributor used AI agents on it while keeping maintainers' trust.

    1. 2:50 The speaker says the dependency problem had stayed unsolved because it meant replacing deprecated dependencies such as Create React App and the whole UI testing framework, not just bumping versions.
    2. 6:38 His approach for each change was to check test coverage with his AI agent, add the missing tests first and only then make the change; along the way he found the frontend end-to-end tests had been disabled for years.
    3. 8:50 The speaker shows a comparison tool he built with his AI agent that compares main and the pull request pixel by pixel and circles visual regressions, such as a contrast problem that passing tests had missed.
    4. 10:11 He opens the largest pull request, which adds about 124,000 lines and removes about 145,000, and says the maintainers merged it after the visual comparison showed no regressions.
    5. 21:25 The speaker's takeaway is to retry problems that looked too hard a year ago, and he says unexciting migration work on the foundations is a perfect use case for AI.
