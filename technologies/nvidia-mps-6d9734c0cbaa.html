    Beyond One Model per GPU: Agent-Driven Placement for Maximum GPU Utilization on Kubernetes

    Weiqi Sun, Ratnopam Chakrabarti, Ajay Vohra, Vivek Kolasani, Felipe Garcia

    The speakers from AWS and NVIDIA present a pipeline in which an agent, a constraint solver and real GPU measurements search for ways to place several LLMs on shared GPUs with CUDA MPS while each model still meets its latency targets. They walk through the plan, profile, search and export stages, the hand-off to KAI Scheduler through Helm, and results they measured on L40S and H100 nodes.

    80 min slot · Advanced · Tutorial

    Accelerators and workload schedulingAI inference and servingAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you serve many LLMs on Kubernetes and want a worked method for packing them onto fewer GPUs without breaking latency targets.

    1. 4:16 A speaker sets the running example: 15 models, two L40S machines with 16 GPUs, and the question of the fewest GPUs that still meet each model's service level objective.
    2. 20:19 A speaker says CUDA MPS gives no performance isolation, that the limits are caps and not guarantees, and that this is why every placement is benchmarked.
    3. 24:23 A speaker introduces the solution as a Claude skill split into plugins and commands that runs plan, profile and search stages, with a CP solver proposing placements for the agent to test.
    4. 43:24 A speaker explains that KAI Scheduler does not read the certified export directly, so a deployment adapter turns it into Helm values with one release per model.
    5. 1:02:25 A speaker gives five takeaways from the H100 experiments, including a measured co-location latency cost of about 1.3 to 2.3 times and a preference for MPS over time slicing.

    Why Are You Still Wasting Whole GPUs? Hardware-Level Sharing, Scheduled with DRA

    Engin Diri

    Engin Diri of Pulumi recounts helping a European customer build a self-hosted inference platform, moving from whole GPUs and time slicing to NVIDIA MIG slices that pods request through Dynamic Resource Allocation. He shows a recorded demo of MIG profiles, resource slices and claims on an AWS node serving a model behind an inference gateway, and lists open questions such as quotas, models larger than a node and start-up ordering.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run inference on your own GPUs and want a practical walk through MIG slices requested with Dynamic Resource Allocation.

    1. 8:32 Diri says time slicing gives no memory or fault isolation between workloads, so the choice was between wasting GPUs and risking a noisy neighbor.
    2. 11:24 Diri says Dynamic Resource Allocation, which he dates to Kubernetes 1.34, lets a request describe device attributes instead of an integer count.
    3. 22:16 In the recorded demo, Diri lists the 1G, 2G and 4G MIG profiles on the node and the resource slices the DRA driver published.
    4. 24:28 Diri lists open day-two questions he is evaluating with the customer, including Kueue for MIG quota and which workload runs first.
    5. 28:06 Diri advises starting the DRA driver only after MIG is configured, because he ran into race conditions between the two.
