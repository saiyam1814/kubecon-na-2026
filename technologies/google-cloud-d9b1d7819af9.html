  • Connectivity

    From Load Balancer Sprawl to Multicluster Service Discovery

    Gaurav Nanda, Vincent Cheng

    Gaurav Nanda and Vincent Cheng describe how Databricks moved cross-cluster service calls from per-service cloud load balancers and a shared proxy to direct pod-to-pod routing. They say Cilium Cluster Mesh provides the connectivity while their own xDS-based endpoint discovery service, EDS, shares pod endpoints between clusters in a region by subscribing to its peers.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Networking and connectivity

    Watch this if: cross-cluster calls in your fleet go through a load balancer per service and you want to see a route to direct pod routing without adopting a full service mesh.

    1. 3:39 Nanda says per-service load balancers multiplied configuration and access rules until cloud quotas became a constraint, and that the shared proxy built next added an extra hop, a shared fleet to manage and a shared failure risk.
    2. 6:16 He explains what direct pod reachability gives their RPC clients inside one cluster: per-request load balancing, soft zone affinity and adaptive routing, driven by pod metadata from EDS over xDS.
    3. 16:06 Cheng says Cilium global services met the connectivity goals but balance per connection like Kubernetes services and did not fit their client libraries, so they could not match in-cluster behavior.
    4. 18:11 He describes a second attempt where EDS read the Cluster Mesh etcd, and says a stale or empty etcd replica could then turn a partial connectivity failure into a wider discovery failure.
    5. 24:29 He says the final design, EDS instances subscribing to each other with incremental xDS updates, showed a P99 of at most 20 ms and a P99.9 of at most 200 ms in a test with 5 million endpoints, 100 clusters and 500 changes per second.

    From Vibe to Live: Building for the Real World

    David Biel, Trey Caliva

    David Biel, who says he has no engineering background, and cloud security engineer Trey Caliva tell how David vibe-coded his family dental cabinet company's website and back-office tools and got them to production on managed services. Trey argues that the advice he gave in person should live in the AI's context as skills, hooks and MCP server permissions, so that platform teams can guide the many non-engineers he says are already building this way.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceAI agents and automationDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: non-engineers in your company are shipping AI-built apps and you want ideas for guiding them.

    1. 2:22 David says he got the work done with a $200 Claude subscription, compared with the $5,000 to $10,000 his family was quoted for a website and brand about ten years earlier.
    2. 5:28 Trey says David's app runs on Firebase Hosting, Cloud Functions, Firestore and Firebase Auth, chosen so there are no servers to patch or clusters to run.
    3. 7:49 Trey lists what first-time builders may miss, such as open database rules, unauthenticated endpoints and pasting an API key into the chat.
    4. 10:16 Trey proposes putting guardrails in the AI's context through skills, hooks and MCP server access, with skills moving from ad hoc to a shared repo to a governed catalog.
    5. 13:47 Trey says skills advise and hooks enforce, showing a simplified hook that runs a script on each prompt to catch pasted secrets, plus deny rules that block deploying from a laptop.

    OpenFeature Update from the Maintainers

    Jonathan Norris, Todd Baert

    Jonathan Norris and Todd Baert report on OpenFeature's growth in providers and SDK downloads, including vendors that now use OpenFeature as their native SDK. They cover new Dart and C++ SDKs, spec 0.9.0, flagd and OFREP improvements, agent skills for OpenFeature, and governance changes.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Delivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you use feature flags and want to know what changed in OpenFeature, flagd and OFREP this year.

    1. 2:55 Norris says OpenFeature now has almost 300 providers across 51 vendors and, by his estimate, well over 100 million SDK downloads per quarter.
    2. 3:58 Baert describes vendors such as Datadog, Cloudflare, Google Cloud, Vercel and Dynatrace choosing OpenFeature as their native SDK.
    3. 6:41 Baert lists flagd changes, including fractional bucketing on any context type, disabled flags no longer counted as errors, and later a FIPS 140 mode.
    4. 10:08 Norris explains that OFREP can now send a server-sent events URL so clients refetch flags in real time when configuration changes.
    5. 12:12 Norris presents OpenFeature agent skills, saying evaluations improved Haiku from 59% to 89% and Sonnet from 79% to 94%.

  • Platform Engineering Day

    Platform Engineering's Missing Chapter: Services Organizations

    Atulpriya Sharma

    Atulpriya argues that platform engineering models built for product companies do not directly fit consultancies and services organizations. He presents delivery capital and a capability layer as ways to reuse organizational learning across client engagements while measuring ramp time, consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time.

    25 min slot · Intermediate · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you build or operate platforms in a consultancy, systems integrator, or client-facing delivery organization.

    1. 2:59 Atulpriya argues that platform engineering's familiar vocabulary was not built around the operating model of consulting firms and services organizations.
    2. 9:03 Atulpriya defines delivery capital as the learning a services organization accumulates across engagements, and says it is the asset the organization truly owns.
    3. 11:04 Atulpriya says the capability layer joins reusable organizational knowledge with an internal consultant IDP of sandboxes and demo clusters so teams can apply prior learning.
    4. 12:57 Atulpriya maps the approach across cloud, IaC, CI/CD, observability, and monitoring, with artifacts such as decision records, checklists, templates, baselines, and named alert owners.
    5. 23:29 Atulpriya argues that ownership, rotation, and billable-work identity block adoption in services firms, and says time-to-ramp, delivery consistency, proposal strength, and delivery time are the measures to watch.

    The Operator Calls Are Coming from Inside the Namespace

    Justin O'Leary

    Justin O'Leary says he took over cloud environments from ordinary namespace access by asking cloud operators to act with their own credentials, and demos what he describes as the same confused-deputy flaw in Config Connector, Azure Service Operator, AWS Controllers for Kubernetes and Crossplane. He recounts how each vendor or project responded to his reports and closes with questions for people who run operators and changes for people who write them.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Security, identity and policyKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you run cloud-resource operators in multi-tenant clusters and want to see how namespace users might reach cloud IAM through them.

    1. 2:45 Justin's first demo applies an IAMPolicyMember resource aimed at a whole GCP organization, and he says Config Connector made a namespace developer organization owner in under a minute.
    2. 10:31 Justin names the pattern a confused deputy, citing Norm Hardy's 1988 description, and lists three ingredients: a field the user fills in, a powerful credential in the operator, and no check on who is asking.
    3. 15:02 Justin demos what he calls trust policy injection with AWS Controllers for Kubernetes, putting an outside AWS account on a role's trust policy, and says the cloud logs attribute the change to the operator.
    4. 23:17 Justin goes through common mitigations such as policy engines, narrower operator permissions, cloud guardrails, logs and OIDC, and argues none of them makes the operator check whether the requester is allowed.
    5. 26:15 Justin's closing advice for operator authors is to verify the caller with a SubjectAccessReview at admission, authorize references instead of trusting raw strings, and fail closed.
