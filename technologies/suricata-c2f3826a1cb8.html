    Securing Rural Colorado with Open Source: Running a Student-Led SOC on Kubernetes

    Maciej Wal

    Maciej Wal describes how the Cybersecurity Center at Metropolitan State University of Denver replaced a vendor's security monitoring service, first with four virtual machines and then with a Kubernetes platform that he says he runs as the one full-time staff member. He covers the outages and adoption problems along the way, and how students learn log analysis and GitOps on the platform that monitors public sector partners.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Security, identity and policyObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you are a very small team weighing Kubernetes for a security monitoring service, or want ideas for teaching students with real infrastructure.

    1. 3:19 The speaker says that after the vendor ended the service he built a replacement in about 66 days on four virtual machines, with WireGuard, single-node OpenSearch and a new ticketing tool.
    2. 6:45 The speaker explains picking the Kubernetes stack from the CNCF landscape, including Keycloak, Argo CD, OpenTofu and Fluent Bit, and says it cost around $1,000 a month.
    3. 8:33 The speaker recounts a flood of Suricata logs taking down the OpenSearch cluster, which he says had too little memory and no autoscaling, and learning of breakage days late.
    4. 12:01 The speaker says one of the hardest problems he found was the gap between offering self-service and people using it, after students did not adopt browser certificate access.
    5. 13:48 The speaker describes a student workshop on Argo CD, Envoy Gateway, cert-manager and GitHub Actions that ended with deploying a Minecraft server through GitOps.
