    Kubernetes Anonymous: Confessions of Kubernetes Cargo Cultists

    Taylor Thomas, Brooks Townsend

    The speakers, who say they have worked both on Kubernetes-heavy platforms and on a wasmCloud platform that avoided Kubernetes, argue that both extremes are traps. They list red flags for using Kubernetes where it fits poorly, describe what each extreme cost them, and suggest that CNCF projects be compatible with Kubernetes without depending on it.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Platform engineering and developer experienceKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You are deciding how much of your platform or project should be built on Kubernetes APIs and want the tradeoffs from people who tried both extremes.

    1. 1:43 The speakers poll the room on red and yellow flags, such as using Kubernetes as the database for config objects, making every service a CRD and mapping an API permission model onto Kubernetes RBAC.
    2. 10:58 Taylor Thomas says avoiding Kubernetes on principle for their wasmCloud platform was their own yellow flag, and that it cost them adoption and integrations with the rest of the ecosystem.
    3. 15:27 Describing Kargo, Thomas says Kubernetes became its database, API and auth model, and that most of its high and critical CVEs this year came from contorting the access model to fit Kubernetes RBAC.
    4. 17:29 He says Kargo 2.0 moves promotion and artifact tracking to a real database while Kubernetes stays as the execution substrate that runs pods.
    5. 20:21 Thomas asks people to stop putting everything in a CRD and offers test questions, including whether the work depends on a Kubernetes feature and whether it would be valuable outside Kubernetes.

    We Have GitOps at Home: How Meta Went from Terraform on Cron to GitOps for AI Infra

    Phong Nguyen, Shaun Hopper

    Phong Nguyen and Shaun Hopper explain how Meta's AI infrastructure team overloaded Terraform layers to deploy Kubernetes apps, so a one-line Helm change could take hours or get stuck. They describe moving app delivery to Kargo and Argo CD with a config sync tool, a pipeline.yaml order form and a managed Argo CD service, and say deploy times dropped from hours to minutes.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Accelerators and workload schedulingDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: your Terraform pipeline also deploys your Kubernetes apps and you want a worked path to Argo CD and Kargo.

    1. 0:04 Phong Nguyen says this is Meta's first time on the KubeCon keynote stage and that Meta's Kubernetes footprint grew fast over the past year.
    2. 0:51 Shaun Hopper says each research and inference cluster is split into about 12 Terraform layers that also deploy most of the cluster's apps, which he strongly suggests never doing, since a stuck DaemonSet or cloud SDK error can block a one-line Helm change.
    3. 2:40 Phong describes the new split: Terraform builds cloud infra, Kargo promotes apps and Argo CD deploys them, plus Ketch, which syncs Meta's internal configs into Git and merges layered Helm values.
    4. 3:31 He shows pipeline.yaml, an app order form where developers answer what to deploy, where to run it and how it rolls out, with no Kargo or Argo CD expertise needed.
    5. 3:57 He credits a managed, multi-tenant Argo CD service built inside Meta, and says early results cut deployment time from more than four hours to minutes.

    You Don’t Have to Choose: Argo and Flux Are Friends!

    Emily Chen, Leigh Capili

    Emily Chen of Akuity and Flux maintainer Leigh Capili compare the design priorities of Argo CD and Flux and describe ways teams run both, such as Flux for cluster add-ons across a fleet and Argo CD for developer applications. In live demos, Flux bootstraps Argo CD, a Flux signature failure shows up as a degraded Argo CD application, and Kargo promotes a Flux Helm release of cert-manager across three virtual clusters.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Delivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: your organization runs both Argo CD and Flux, or is arguing over which one to pick, and you want to see them working in one cluster.

    1. 8:43 Chen says Argo CD has shipped Flux health checks since version 2.13, so Flux Helm releases and kustomizations show their health in an Argo CD application.
    2. 10:12 The speakers describe a common split where Flux handles fleet management and cluster add-ons while Argo CD serves application developers.
    3. 16:34 In the demo, Capili pushes an unsigned chart and the signature verification failure from Flux appears in the Argo CD interface.
    4. 20:19 Chen's Kargo promotion of cert-manager commits directly to Git for Flux to pick up, without using Argo CD.
    5. 22:16 Capili says Kargo has only one health checker, for Argo CD applications, and proposes one that reads kstatus so Kargo could understand Flux and other tools.
