    We Have GitOps at Home: How Meta Went from Terraform on Cron to GitOps for AI Infra

    Phong Nguyen, Shaun Hopper

    Phong Nguyen and Shaun Hopper explain how Meta's AI infrastructure team overloaded Terraform layers to deploy Kubernetes apps, so a one-line Helm change could take hours or get stuck. They describe moving app delivery to Kargo and Argo CD with a config sync tool, a pipeline.yaml order form and a managed Argo CD service, and say deploy times dropped from hours to minutes.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Accelerators and workload schedulingDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: your Terraform pipeline also deploys your Kubernetes apps and you want a worked path to Argo CD and Kargo.

    1. 0:04 Phong Nguyen says this is Meta's first time on the KubeCon keynote stage and that Meta's Kubernetes footprint grew fast over the past year.
    2. 0:51 Shaun Hopper says each research and inference cluster is split into about 12 Terraform layers that also deploy most of the cluster's apps, which he strongly suggests never doing, since a stuck DaemonSet or cloud SDK error can block a one-line Helm change.
    3. 2:40 Phong describes the new split: Terraform builds cloud infra, Kargo promotes apps and Argo CD deploys them, plus Ketch, which syncs Meta's internal configs into Git and merges layered Helm values.
    4. 3:31 He shows pipeline.yaml, an app order form where developers answer what to deploy, where to run it and how it rolls out, with no Kargo or Argo CD expertise needed.
    5. 3:57 He credits a managed, multi-tenant Argo CD service built inside Meta, and says early results cut deployment time from more than four hours to minutes.
