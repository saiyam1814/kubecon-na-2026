    AI Found a 0-Day in Metal3. What Happens Next?

    Tuomo Tanskanen

    Tuomo Tanskanen, who leads the Metal3 security committee, follows a vulnerability report from arrival through triage, fix, release and downstream upgrade, drawing on reports from his own AI discovery pipeline and showing how its findings broke down after triage. He also describes how fixes in OpenStack Ironic reach Metal3 and vendor distributions, and advises users to run supported releases, know their configuration and ask vendors how quickly they take in fixes.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policyDelivery, GitOps and release safetyAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you maintain a project that receives AI-generated vulnerability reports, or you run Metal3 and want to know how its security fixes reach you.

    1. 4:12 Tuomo Tanskanen says every vulnerability disclosure Metal3 received this year came from an AI discovery pipeline he built himself, which also scans OpenStack Ironic, and that its findings go through each project's normal security process.
    2. 8:27 He gives the pipeline's numbers: of about 220 findings, 80 were false or duplicates, 31 became hardening issues, 22 were plain bugs and 22 were confirmed, and he concludes that discovery is fast while triage, fixing and coordinated release are slow.
    3. 10:34 He says Metal3 fixes low and medium issues in the open, because GitHub private forks give almost no CI support and force manual testing, and reserves embargoed handling for high and critical ones.
    4. 14:41 He describes a lesson from combining four related cross-namespace issues into one advisory: no CVE could be issued for it, because a CVE covers a single issue.
    5. 20:20 Using two fixes as examples, he says each step down the dependency chain into vendor distributions took roughly a month, so fixes take a long time to reach production.

    Harbor Project – The Maintainers Session

    Orlin Vasilev, Vadim Bauer, Rani Chowdary Mandepudi

    Three Harbor maintainers and contributors cover what is in Harbor 2.16, including custom project roles, proxy cache filters and upstream Arm64 builds, and share community statistics. They then outline what they are working on for 2.17, such as syncing AI models from Hugging Face into Harbor as OCI artifacts, and ask for feedback on the open proposals.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policyDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: you run Harbor and want to know what 2.16 changed and what the maintainers plan for 2.17, or if you need Harbor on Arm.

    1. 0:40 Vadim Bauer lists the highlights he picks from Harbor 2.16: custom project roles, a configurable proxy cache with bandwidth limits and regex filters, Valkey replacing Redis, multi-architecture images and PostgreSQL 18.
    2. 7:36 Rani Chowdary Mandepudi explains how Arm64 support landed upstream in four parts: native CI, build tooling, online and offline installers, and multi-platform OCI manifests.
    3. 14:23 Orlin Vasilev shows that pull requests nearly doubled year over year, which he attributes to AI, and asks contributors to link an issue, keep PRs small and be patient.
    4. 18:05 Vadim Bauer describes the model sync proposal for 2.17: replicate models from Hugging Face and convert them on the fly into OCI artifacts in the model spec format.
    5. 21:13 Vadim Bauer describes a proposed scanner spec upgrade that he says would let Harbor run several scanners at once, including linters, license and secret scanners, and aggregate the results.

    Next Generation DevOps: Reshaping the Cloud Native DevOps Infinity Loop with AI

    Rey Lejano

    The speaker reviews how DevOps and cloud native grew up together and then proposes a framework that places an example AI agent in each phase of the DevOps infinity loop. He says the agents take the drafting and repetitive work while people keep the judgment and approval steps.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    AI agents and automationDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: You are new to cloud native delivery and want a phase-by-phase picture of where AI agents could fit in the DevOps loop.

    1. 3:28 The speaker introduces a framework for embedding AI in each phase of the DevOps infinity loop, with people remaining in the loop for accountability.
    2. 15:37 The speaker argues that AI is the next substrate, and says it does not change the stages of the loop but who or what executes each one.
    3. 17:31 For the plan phase, the speaker describes a spec agent that turns vague requests into user stories and draft architectural decision records, with the human deciding.
    4. 22:50 For the build phase, the speaker describes a pipeline agent that reviews container files, runs CVE scans, generates SBOMs and signs images, with policies enforced by the pipeline, in his case Tekton.
    5. 28:44 For deploy and operate, the speaker describes a rollout agent that runs canary steps with Argo Rollouts and watches metrics for rollback, and a runbook agent that does root cause analysis.

    OpenCost: What's New, What's Next, and How We're Scaling Kubernetes Cost Intelligence

    Rajith Attapattu, Warwick Peatey

    The OpenCost maintainers review what they say the project shipped over the past year, focusing on its MCP server, signed releases with SBOMs, and KubeModel, which they describe as a usage model kept separate from pricing. They show a pre-recorded MCP query from Claude Code, say the MCP endpoint is still unauthenticated, and spend the second half answering audience questions about discounts, right-sizing and AI-assisted contributions.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Observability and reliabilityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you use OpenCost and want the maintainers' account of the MCP server, the supply chain changes and how KubeModel separates usage from pricing.

    1. 4:44 Warwick Peatey describes the OpenCost MCP server as an agentic proxy for the existing REST API that is designed for agents to consume, and lists its tools for allocation, asset, cloud and efficiency costs.
    2. 7:20 He shows a pre-recorded demo in Claude Code asking what each namespace cost over the last seven days, and notes some queries are slower than the OpenCost UI on large data.
    3. 9:04 He says the MCP server is opt-in but still unauthenticated, so it is meant for use inside your own network.
    4. 11:59 Rajith Attapattu introduces KubeModel as a price-free record of what ran, where and for how long, so that pricing can be re-applied later once discounts and the final bill are known.
    5. 27:34 Asked about agent-written pull requests, Peatey says the project accepts contributions regardless of author as long as a person is responsible for them.

    Zero to Maintainer in 45 Days: An AI-Assisted Migration of a CNCF Project's Legacy Frontend

    Jeff Spahr

    The speaker, who says he is not a frontend developer, describes how he used AI coding agents to migrate the aging Kubeflow Pipelines frontend off deprecated dependencies, starting from a wish to remove vulnerabilities. He walks through the checks he used to earn maintainers' trust, from adding tests before each change to a pixel-by-pixel visual comparison, and how he became a frontend approver after 45 days and later a maintainer.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Community and open source adoptionAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: You have a daunting migration in an open source project's backlog and want to see how one contributor used AI agents on it while keeping maintainers' trust.

    1. 2:50 The speaker says the dependency problem had stayed unsolved because it meant replacing deprecated dependencies such as Create React App and the whole UI testing framework, not just bumping versions.
    2. 6:38 His approach for each change was to check test coverage with his AI agent, add the missing tests first and only then make the change; along the way he found the frontend end-to-end tests had been disabled for years.
    3. 8:50 The speaker shows a comparison tool he built with his AI agent that compares main and the pull request pixel by pixel and circles visual regressions, such as a contrast problem that passing tests had missed.
    4. 10:11 He opens the largest pull request, which adds about 124,000 lines and removes about 145,000, and says the maintainers merged it after the visual comparison showed no regressions.
    5. 21:25 The speaker's takeaway is to retry problems that looked too hard a year ago, and he says unexciting migration work on the foundations is a perfect use case for AI.
