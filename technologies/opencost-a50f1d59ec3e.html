    Beyond End User: CAP and the Evolution of Open Source at CVS Health

    Michael Amundson, Jim Beyers

    Jim Beyers and Michael Amundson of CVS Health present CAP, the internal application platform they say lets a new hire ship to production on day one by hiding Kubernetes, Istio, DNS and certificates behind one app model. They demo creating and deploying an app, explain how CAP spreads services across clusters, and announce their first open source projects, Flag Controller and the CAP Registry API.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you build an internal developer platform and want to see one that hides Kubernetes, Istio and DNS behind a single app model.

    1. 0:25 Jim Beyers says the CVS Health DevEx team built CAP, the CAP Application Platform, in about 10 months, and that it now serves hundreds of engineers and tens of thousands of deployments a month.
    2. 0:58 He says their feature flagging platform, built with OpenFeature, handles hundreds of millions of flag evaluations a day, and that CVS Health has joined CNCF as a platinum member.
    3. 1:45 Michael Amundson gives the question CAP is measured against, whether a new hire can ship to production on their first morning, and its tenets: no prerequisites, an abstract app model, and governance built into the platform.
    4. 3:08 Demo: a new app gets a Docusaurus quick start service, CAP creates the repo and CI pipeline, then deploys it to a cluster with a DNS name and TLS certificate in a couple of minutes.
    5. 5:30 CVS Health announces its first open source DevEx projects, the Flag Controller feature flagging product and the CAP Registry API, and says it plans to open source CAP fully next year.

    Clueless About The Cost of AI Model Usage? OpenCost+llm-d = first of a kind inference cost tracking

    Alexander Meijer

    Alexander Meijer, presenting alone because Sima Nadler could not attend, describes an integration where OpenCost reads llm-d and vLLM token metrics and combines them with GPU and provider pricing to report inference costs such as cost per token. He demos the APIs and dashboard live against an IBM Research cluster, comparing allocation and usage costs to find idle models, and outlines a roadmap for deeper GPU metrics.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and servingObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you self-host LLMs on Kubernetes and want to know what each model and token actually costs.

    1. 1:46 Alexander Meijer says self-hosted models are generally cheaper than hosted ones, but that this only delays the cost problem.
    2. 6:40 He draws the line of responsibility: llm-d handles token counts, routing and scheduling, while OpenCost handles hardware metrics, pricing and cloud provider connections.
    3. 12:27 He explains the two cost bases: allocation, the max of request and usage, which he calls the production metric, and usage, which he calls the R&D metric.
    4. 16:41 His worked example divides a $15 deployment cost by 150 processed tokens to get $0.10 per token.
    5. 20:54 In the live demo, comparing $246 allocation cost with $22 usage cost shows one model was idle about 90 percent of the time, which he flags as a right-sizing target.

    OpenCost: What's New, What's Next, and How We're Scaling Kubernetes Cost Intelligence

    Rajith Attapattu, Warwick Peatey

    The OpenCost maintainers review what they say the project shipped over the past year, focusing on its MCP server, signed releases with SBOMs, and KubeModel, which they describe as a usage model kept separate from pricing. They show a pre-recorded MCP query from Claude Code, say the MCP endpoint is still unauthenticated, and spend the second half answering audience questions about discounts, right-sizing and AI-assisted contributions.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Observability and reliabilityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: you use OpenCost and want the maintainers' account of the MCP server, the supply chain changes and how KubeModel separates usage from pricing.

    1. 4:44 Warwick Peatey describes the OpenCost MCP server as an agentic proxy for the existing REST API that is designed for agents to consume, and lists its tools for allocation, asset, cloud and efficiency costs.
    2. 7:20 He shows a pre-recorded demo in Claude Code asking what each namespace cost over the last seven days, and notes some queries are slower than the OpenCost UI on large data.
    3. 9:04 He says the MCP server is opt-in but still unauthenticated, so it is meant for use inside your own network.
    4. 11:59 Rajith Attapattu introduces KubeModel as a price-free record of what ran, where and for how long, so that pricing can be re-applied later once discounts and the final bill are known.
    5. 27:34 Asked about agent-written pull requests, Peatey says the project accepts contributions regardless of author as long as a person is responsible for them.

    Seeing Inside LLM Inference: Observability and Cost Visibility for llm-d on Kubernetes

    Nili Guy, Guangya Liu

    Guangya Liu walks through end-to-end tracing and metric, trace and log correlation in llm-d, using a slow time-to-first-token case as a worked example. Nili Guy then describes early work combining llm-d metrics with OpenCost to estimate the cost per token for self-hosted models by tenant and hardware.

    15 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    AI inference and servingObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: You operate llm-d or similar inference stacks and want ideas for tracing requests and tracking GPU cost per token.

    1. 1:06 Guangya Liu follows one request from Envoy through the llm-d router (EPP), the KV cache index and the prefill and decode pods to show why end-to-end tracing is needed.
    2. 3:50 He shows the correlation between metrics, traces and logs, with one click from a Grafana panel to the trace in the Jaeger UI.
    3. 4:43 He walks through debugging a P99 time-to-first-token alert, where the trace shows a request routed to a pod without the cached prefix.
    4. 6:57 He traces the root cause to a Helm release that changed a routing scorer weight from three to zero.
    5. 11:55 Nili Guy describes combining OpenCost with llm-d, vLLM and SGLang metrics in Prometheus to report cost per token at different granularities.
