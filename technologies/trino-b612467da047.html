    Feeding the GPU: Streaming Data into Training, Lakehouse, and Serving without the Serialization Tax

    Dheeraj Kapur, Deepak Choudhary

    The speakers describe a streaming design in which producers send Arrow IPC batches once to a gateway that writes them to GPU memory and to a lakehouse without converting rows to columns again. They share their own measurements for throughput, query engines and crash recovery, and say the work is not open source yet.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Data, storage and stateAccelerators and workload schedulingAI inference and serving

    Watch this if: You build streaming pipelines that feed both GPUs and lakehouse tables and want to see where serialization cost goes.

    1. 2:59 The speaker says separate pipelines for the GPU, the lakehouse and other destinations each convert row data into a columnar format again.
    2. 6:37 The speaker says the producer should send Arrow IPC so that preparation happens once, and that the format converts one to one into cuDF and Parquet.
    3. 10:51 The speaker explains that the gateway transfers batches into GPU memory, stores them in a durable log for the lakehouse, and sends the acknowledgment once the data is on disk.
    4. 17:00 The speaker reports a throughput of 3 million points per second with 20 pods, and says they want to scale the system to 17 million points per second.
    5. 21:33 The speaker says a fix took them from 25,000 duplicates to zero duplicates and no data loss over two weeks of chaos testing.

    Splitting the Monolith at Cloudera - Building Fault-Tolerant Workflows with Cadence

    Soma Koltai, Ender Demirkaya

    Soma Koltai of Cloudera describes moving the Cloudera Data Warehouse control service from a single Go binary to Cadence workflows, using a small bridging layer shaped like the Cadence client API and a lease service on PostgreSQL so the refactoring could start before Cadence was integrated. Ender Demirkaya of the Cadence project explains how workflows and activities recover state, and says the Cadence team built distributed semaphores after hearing about Cloudera's use.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Observability and reliabilityData, storage and state

    Watch this if: you are breaking up a stateful monolith with long-running operations and want a staged way to adopt a workflow engine.

    1. 1:47 Koltai says provisioning a cluster and restoring workloads can take a whole hour, and under the previous architecture a failure meant the whole process was retried.
    2. 5:36 Demirkaya explains that developers annotate one function as a workflow and its external calls as activities, and if everything crashes another worker continues where it left off.
    3. 9:48 Koltai describes the Async Executor, a bridging layer modeled on the Cadence client API that he says took about 600 lines and two weeks, and let them reuse every activity handler when they switched to Cadence.
    4. 12:32 For locking, Koltai says they chose leases, locks with a time to live, behind a three-method interface, implemented with essentially one insert call on the PostgreSQL database they already had.
    5. 18:54 Koltai tells of a memory leak that went unnoticed because Karpenter rescheduled the pod before it hit its memory limit, and says restarts after the migration no longer caused workflow failures or pages.

    Stop AI Agents from Guessing SQL with Semantic Operator on Kubernetes

    Manabu McCloskey

    The speaker introduces Semantic Operator, a new project that lets teams define business metrics in the Apache Ossie format as Kubernetes resources so agents request metrics and never write SQL. He says the operator runs the queries on StarRocks or Trino, has built-in governance, and is in its early days and looking for contributors.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    AI agents and automationData, storage and state

    Watch this if: You build agents or BI tools that answer questions from company databases and want them to stop producing wrong SQL.

    1. 0:32 The speaker says a benchmark of 90 prompts gave 28 wrong answers when an agent was asked about business metrics.
    2. 1:09 The speaker says the Apache Ossie spec is vendor neutral and is applied to the Kubernetes cluster, where the operator runs the query for the agent.
    3. 1:40 The speaker says the execution engine can be StarRocks or Trino right now and that adding new engines is easy enough.
    4. 2:43 The speaker says agents never write the SQL queries and only look at the metrics that are available.
    5. 3:00 The speaker describes a built-in governance model for columns, rows and metrics, with Open Policy Agent and Apache Ranger integrations.

    Why We Bet the Data Platform on Kubernetes - And How AI Is Changing How We Operate It

    Vara Bonthu, Damon Cortesi, Padma Aradhyula, Rainie Li, Ying Xiong

    Moderator Vara Bonthu asks panelists from Airbnb, Salesforce, Pinterest and Apple Maps why they moved Spark and other data workloads from Hadoop-style clusters to Kubernetes and what was hard about it. The panelists describe pod churn, scheduling choices among YuniKorn, Volcano and Kueue, and the AI agents and skills they say they now use for troubleshooting and upgrades.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Panel

    Data, storage and stateAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you are weighing a move of Spark or other data workloads from Hadoop to Kubernetes and want to hear what four large operators ran into.

    1. 5:30 Damon Cortesi says Airbnb reached 100% Spark on Kubernetes a couple of weeks earlier, shut down its last Hadoop cluster, and runs about 4 million Spark jobs a week.
    2. 9:46 Padma Aradhyula says Salesforce Data Cloud runs thousands of small near-real-time Spark jobs lasting two to five minutes, and that the resulting pod churn puts heavy pressure on the API server.
    3. 11:38 Rainie Li describes Pinterest's Moka platform with Apache YuniKorn and a remote shuffle service, and reports a 10% job performance improvement and over 10% annual infrastructure savings.
    4. 14:45 Ying Xiong says the hardest start at Apple Maps was convincing people to try Kubernetes without data locality, followed by scalability issues from huge jobs and from users submitting thousands of jobs an hour.
    5. 21:46 Rainie describes a multi-agent setup where sub-agents reach platform components through MCP servers and search past incidents, and says it now helps fix over 50% of troubleshooting issues.
