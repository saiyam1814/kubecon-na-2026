    Beyond End User: CAP and the Evolution of Open Source at CVS Health

    Michael Amundson, Jim Beyers

    Jim Beyers and Michael Amundson of CVS Health present CAP, the internal application platform they say lets a new hire ship to production on day one by hiding Kubernetes, Istio, DNS and certificates behind one app model. They demo creating and deploying an app, explain how CAP spreads services across clusters, and announce their first open source projects, Flag Controller and the CAP Registry API.

    5 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Community and open source adoption

    Watch this if: you build an internal developer platform and want to see one that hides Kubernetes, Istio and DNS behind a single app model.

    1. 0:25 Jim Beyers says the CVS Health DevEx team built CAP, the CAP Application Platform, in about 10 months, and that it now serves hundreds of engineers and tens of thousands of deployments a month.
    2. 0:58 He says their feature flagging platform, built with OpenFeature, handles hundreds of millions of flag evaluations a day, and that CVS Health has joined CNCF as a platinum member.
    3. 1:45 Michael Amundson gives the question CAP is measured against, whether a new hire can ship to production on their first morning, and its tenets: no prerequisites, an abstract app model, and governance built into the platform.
    4. 3:08 Demo: a new app gets a Docusaurus quick start service, CAP creates the repo and CI pipeline, then deploys it to a cluster with a DNS name and TLS certificate in a couple of minutes.
    5. 5:30 CVS Health announces its first open source DevEx projects, the Flag Controller feature flagging product and the CAP Registry API, and says it plans to open source CAP fully next year.

    How We Are Enabling Feature Flagging At Scale

    Juan Bernal

    Juan Bernal explains why CVS Health looked for an alternative to expensive vendor plans and limited homegrown feature flagging. He describes how OpenFeature and a self-hosted control plane let his team separate flag management from evaluation.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Delivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: You are weighing a self-hosted feature flag platform against vendor tools or ticket-driven workflows.

    1. 1:58 Bernal explains how feature flags separate deployment from release and let teams target a rollout to a specific segment.
    2. 4:53 Bernal explains why his team chose OpenFeature: it offers a common contract for evaluating flags across platforms and backends.
    3. 6:42 Bernal walks through the CVS architecture, where a core API feeds both the management interface and flagd, the service that evaluates the flags.
    4. 7:27 Bernal recalls how an OpenFeature maintainer proposed flagd FlagSets to support configuration at both the application and environment level.
    5. 11:09 In a recorded demo, Bernal shows the Flag Controller interface for organizing teams and applications, defining reusable targeting segments, and creating feature flags.
