    Following the Evidence with HolmesGPT: Troubleshooting Kubernetes Apps and Infrastructure

    Yash Sharma

    Yash Sharma, presenting alone after saying his co-speaker could not attend, describes his own experiment with HolmesGPT on a demo shop application running on a DigitalOcean Kubernetes cluster with three planted faults. He shows it investigating a full Postgres volume, a CPU-hungry CronJob and a cloud-side volume attachment limit, and then outlines how he would run it in production with read-only access and human approval for changes.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Observability and reliabilityAI agents and automation

    Watch this if: you are considering an AI troubleshooting assistant for Kubernetes and want to see it work through storage, CPU and cloud-provider faults before deciding how much access to give it.

    1. 4:41 Sharma describes HolmesGPT's loop: starting from an alert thread, it calls toolsets such as kubectl, PromQL, APIs and SQL, reruns the investigation if the results are not enough, and returns a root cause with evidence and a suggested fix.
    2. 12:21 In the live demo, after he fills the Postgres volume and checkout errors rise, the holmes ask command returns evidence of no space left on the device along with possible fixes.
    3. 17:26 He says HolmesGPT runs read-only in his cluster, so a kubectl exec it attempted was denied as needing approval, and it still traced the slow checkout API to the batch job he had added.
    4. 18:38 For the third case he explains a setup where a 16th pod cannot get a volume because of a per-node attachment limit on DigitalOcean Kubernetes, and shows holmes investigate alertmanager attributing the pending pod to the cloud provider side.
    5. 24:44 His security advice is to keep the tool read-only, require human approval for any write, issue least-privilege tokens per task, and check the logs rather than fully trusting the output, since logs can carry injected content.

    Next Generation DevOps: Reshaping the Cloud Native DevOps Infinity Loop with AI

    Rey Lejano

    The speaker reviews how DevOps and cloud native grew up together and then proposes a framework that places an example AI agent in each phase of the DevOps infinity loop. He says the agents take the drafting and repetitive work while people keep the judgment and approval steps.

    30 min slot · Beginner · Session

    AI agents and automationDelivery, GitOps and release safety

    Watch this if: You are new to cloud native delivery and want a phase-by-phase picture of where AI agents could fit in the DevOps loop.

    1. 3:28 The speaker introduces a framework for embedding AI in each phase of the DevOps infinity loop, with people remaining in the loop for accountability.
    2. 15:37 The speaker argues that AI is the next substrate, and says it does not change the stages of the loop but who or what executes each one.
    3. 17:31 For the plan phase, the speaker describes a spec agent that turns vague requests into user stories and draft architectural decision records, with the human deciding.
    4. 22:50 For the build phase, the speaker describes a pipeline agent that reviews container files, runs CVE scans, generates SBOMs and signs images, with policies enforced by the pipeline, in his case Tekton.
    5. 28:44 For deploy and operate, the speaker describes a rollout agent that runs canary steps with Argo Rollouts and watches metrics for rollback, and a runbook agent that does root cause analysis.

    The Missing Layer of Platform Maturity: Kubernetes Networking

    Faseela Kundattil, Christophe Sauthier, Chad M. Crowell, Marino Wijay, James Spurin

    The panel explains why Kubernetes networking remains a maturity gap despite higher-level abstractions, using incidents and observability workflows to show why engineers still need mental models of the underlying stack. The panelists describe the hands-on CKNE exam as a vendor-flexible way to validate networking skills while arguing that AI should assist rather than replace engineering judgment.

    35 min slot · Any Level · Panel

    Networking and connectivityPlatform engineering and developer experience

    Watch this if: your platform team owns Kubernetes networking troubleshooting or is evaluating the CKNE curriculum.

    1. 1:41 The panel argues that Kubernetes networking is a missing layer of platform maturity because usable abstractions still require underlying troubleshooting knowledge when failures occur.
    2. 10:08 Chad uses a 504 ingress incident to argue for peeling back the network path locally, from pod logs and events through service endpoints to ingress routing and host-header behavior.
    3. 15:21 James argues that engineers should learn enough iptables, nftables, conntrack, and eBPF to understand failures and judge AI-suggested fixes rather than treating AI as the authority.
    4. 17:35 Chad argues that knowing where in the networking stack a problem lives matters more than loyalty to a toolset, with observability used to narrow environment and namespace before deep packet inspection.
    5. 23:31 Christophe says SMEs from varied projects designed the hands-on CKNE exam so candidates can answer with different tools rather than being forced into one stack.
