    The Scheduling Stack: Real-World Lessons from Anthropic, Anyscale, Google, and CERN

    Wojciech Tyczyński, Ricardo Rocha, Kaslin Fields, Decheng Dai, Jui-An Huang

    The panelists compare what upstream Kubernetes scheduling can handle with where their teams still specialize. They describe how placement, queueing, cluster boundaries, and resource fragmentation affect distributed workloads.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Panel

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you are deciding which scheduling concerns belong in Kubernetes and which belong in a higher-level workload scheduler.

    1. 5:42 Fields introduces KubeRay as the controller for running Ray on Kubernetes.
    2. 10:30 A panelist distinguishes workload placement from deciding what should run now or wait in a queue.
    3. 13:46 Rocha recalls splitting CERN clusters because the scheduler could not launch the volume of workloads CERN wanted, rather than because of node count.
    4. 20:01 A panelist explains why they try to keep a tightly coupled workload within one cluster instead of exposing it to failures across several clusters.
    5. 25:14 A panelist describes a background job that scans for fragmentation and moves workloads when it finds a better placement.
