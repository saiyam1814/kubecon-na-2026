    Cloud Native: Built by Humans, Engineered to Evolve

    Jeffrey Sica

    Jeffrey Sica argues that cloud native can adapt to AI because the people who maintain its projects keep changing how they work without abandoning human review. He connects agent workloads to familiar distributed systems practices such as limiting blast radius, rolling back quickly, and sandboxing untrusted code.

    10 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    Community and open source adoptionKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: you want to connect AI agent workloads to the reliability practices and contribution norms cloud native teams already use.

    1. 2:55 Sica asks the audience to see the people who keep showing up behind each logo in the cloud native landscape.
    2. 4:25 Sica says maintainers responded to AI-generated contributions by writing policies for what a good contribution looks like and what they expect the submitting human to review.
    3. 5:37 Sica uses the term agent speed for the number of machine actions between human decisions and argues that the change matters for software architecture.
    4. 6:30 Sica points to small blast radii, fast iteration and rollback, sandboxing, and APIs as reasons cloud native systems can adapt to agent workloads.
    5. 7:59 Sica argues that Kubernetes fits training and inference because they are distributed systems problems, not because Kubernetes was originally built for AI.

    Many Clusters, One GPU Pool: Locality-Aware Scheduling and Data Delivery With Kueue and Dragonfly

    Yiqing Wang, Adam Buran

    Yiqing Wang and Adam Buran describe how Roblox shares GPU capacity across clusters using Kueue while leaving each cluster to check its own capacity. They explain how a locality-aware dispatcher chooses clusters and how Dragonfly caches training data and checkpoints near the jobs that use them.

    30 min slot · Advanced · Session

    Accelerators and workload schedulingData, storage and state

    Watch this if: You operate GPU clusters in more than one location and want to share capacity without repeatedly pulling the same datasets across regions.

    1. 6:55 The speakers describe two levels of MultiKueue admission: the hub checks team quota, then the candidate clusters check node capacity and the first to admit the job runs it with KubeRay.
    2. 8:15 The speakers explain how the dispatcher first filters for requirements such as GPU type and full-job fit, then prefers the data region for a limited time before considering other regions.
    3. 9:19 The speakers explain how Roblox separates pool-wide guaranteed quota from cluster-level borrowing and uses preemption to reclaim borrowed GPUs when a guaranteed job arrives.
    4. 14:51 The speakers describe Dragonfly looking for file pieces in the local cache, other peers, and seed peers before falling back to Amazon S3.
    5. 15:55 The speakers report that half of the data served came from node-local cache and another 43 percent from peers in the same cluster, leaving 7 percent served from Amazon S3.

    WG-Batch Updates: What’s New and What Is Next?

    Amy Chen, Yuki Iwai, Marcin Wielgus

    Marcin Wielgus, Amy Chen and Yuki Iwai give an update on the Kubernetes Batch Working Group and its projects. They cover new Kueue features such as concurrent admission, configurable preemption, topology-aware spreading, the dynamic quota orchestrator and partial scale-up, then Job, JobSet and workload-aware scheduling updates.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Accelerators and workload scheduling

    Watch this if: you run AI training or batch jobs on Kubernetes and want to know what changed in Kueue, Job and JobSet.

    1. 3:46 Wielgus says that since last November Kueue shipped 54 releases, while PRs, issues and contributors each more than doubled.
    2. 4:45 Wielgus explains concurrent admission, where virtual copies of a workload keep trying for cheaper reserved capacity and move the job there once it frees up.
    3. 12:36 Wielgus introduces topology-aware spreading for inference, which caps the share of serving capacity placed in one rack, for example at 45%.
    4. 14:27 Chen describes the dynamic quota orchestrator, which discovers real capacity from node pools or MultiKueue worker clusters and adjusts nominal quota automatically.
    5. 28:54 Iwai explains that Kueue is adopting a shared scheduler library from kube-scheduler so its node feasibility checks match what kube-scheduler will accept.

    30 min slot · Level not listed · Session
