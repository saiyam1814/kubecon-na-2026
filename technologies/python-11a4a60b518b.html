    Edging Closer to AI Assisted Troubleshooting - AI Assisted Dashboard Analysis

    Vijay Samuel

    Vijay Samuel credits Jing Hu for much of the work on eBay's dashboard analyzer and explains why his team moved away from feeding raw telemetry to a model. He describes how the analyzer computes statistics in code and links its hypotheses back to specific dashboard evidence.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI agents and automationObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: You want AI-assisted dashboard triage that keeps the math in code and gives on-call engineers evidence they can verify.

    1. 2:27 Samuel sketches a two-server MCP approach that sends dashboard metadata and raw results to a model, then explains why doing this for every panel can bury important facts in the context window.
    2. 6:08 Samuel describes the design rule he wants on-call engineers to take away: compute the telemetry statistics in code and let the model reason over the results rather than do the arithmetic.
    3. 6:38 Samuel explains how the analyzer links hypotheses to findings and then back to the exact dashboard panel and time range so an engineer can verify them.
    4. 10:02 Samuel walks through fetching dashboard metadata, making a plan, and handing different telemetry types to specialized subagents before combining their findings.
    5. 14:04 Samuel describes a metrics subagent that computes statistics in Go, detects anomalies in Python, and uses Largest Triangle Three Buckets to reduce the points it sends to the model.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    AI inference and servingKubernetes internals and runtimes

    Watch this if: You are comparing pre-warmed GPU pods with restoring initialized workloads when inference demand spikes.

    The Identity Crisis: Who is the AI Agent in Your Cluster?

    Abdel Sghiouar

    Abdel Sghiouar argues that agents sharing a Kubernetes service account are hard to distinguish when something goes wrong. He proposes short-lived SPIFFE identities, gateways for authorization, and sandboxing, with humans setting policy instead of approving every action.

    10 min slot · Level not listed · Keynote

    AI agents and automationSecurity, identity and policy

    Watch this if: you need to separate agent identity, authorization, and runtime isolation when designing a Kubernetes platform.

    1. 1:23 Sghiouar says it is hard to predict which tools or endpoints an agent will call, and argues that a shared Kubernetes service account makes it hard to trace an incident to a particular replica.
    2. 2:40 Sghiouar distinguishes agents acting for a human from agents triggered autonomously, and argues the latter need a unique, short-lived identity tied to their task.
    3. 3:29 Sghiouar proposes SPIFFE and SPIRE for giving agents short-lived identities at runtime, potentially scoped to a replica or session.
    4. 4:15 Sghiouar says identity does not answer authorization and discusses using a gateway to evaluate what an agent wants to do before it accesses an external resource.
    5. 8:09 Sghiouar urges platform engineers to move from human approval of each action to human-defined governance that sets boundaries for what agents can do.
