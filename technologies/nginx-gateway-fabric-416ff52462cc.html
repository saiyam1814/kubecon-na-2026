    Who's Governing the GPU Lane? Policy as Code for AI Inference Traffic in Kubernetes

    Ciara Stacke, Alessandro Fael Garcia

    The speakers argue that RBAC only decides who may create an object, not what is in it, and walk through demo scenarios in which a tenant team tries to reach models or GPUs it should not use. They show Kyverno validating policies in front of NGINX Gateway Fabric and InferencePool resources blocking each attempt, and Policy Reporter listing resources created before the policies existed.

    25 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Security, identity and policyAI inference and servingNetworking and connectivity

    Watch this if: You run shared GPU inference behind Gateway API and want admission policies for tenant isolation, model access and cost labels.

    1. 0:56 Alessandro Fael Garcia says RBAC decides who may create an object but not what is in it, and that AI inference traffic needs more granular checks.
    2. 9:04 Ciara Stacke shows a Kyverno validating policy under which only the platform team may share InferencePools through a ReferenceGrant, while tenant teams can still share services.
    3. 12:27 She says Kyverno can check each HTTPRoute against a per-team list of approved models, and then shows the route to the larger model being refused.
    4. 14:48 She requires cost center and tier labels on GPU workloads, and the demo denies a self-deployed copy of the large model.
    5. 17:10 She shows a policy that stops teams in regulated namespaces from switching their InferencePool from fail closed to fail open.

    Will Your Gateway Leave You High and Dry?

    John Howard, Mitch Connors

    John Howard and Mitch Connors walk through version 3 of Howard's Gateway API Bench, which tests agentgateway, Envoy Gateway, Istio, kgateway, NGINX Gateway Fabric and Traefik on route updates, a 5,000 route scale test, route propagation time and rolling restarts. Framed around a fictional cardboard box startup, they report which implementations returned errors or used more CPU and memory in their runs, and Howard states up front that both speakers work on several of the gateways tested.

    30 min slot · Any Level · Session

    Networking and connectivityObservability and reliability

    Watch this if: you are choosing a Gateway API implementation, perhaps while moving off ingress-nginx, and want to see how six of them behaved under route churn and scale.

    1. 4:20 Howard says the two speakers are biased because they work on many of the gateways tested, and that the report is reproducible with shared data.
    2. 8:22 Howard reports that four implementations returned all 200s while a route was updated, while Envoy Gateway and NGINX had brief periods of downtime in his test.
    3. 13:59 In the 5,000 route test, Howard says the NGINX data plane used about three cores of CPU while serving no traffic.
    4. 16:40 Howard attributes the resource gap between Envoy based gateways to metrics defaults, saying Envoy Gateway and kgateway exposed 350,000 metrics out of the box.
    5. 21:11 Howard says no implementation failed during continuous backend pod restarts, while NGINX and Traefik had errors when the gateway itself was rolled out.
