    (Maintainer) From FIFO to Fair Share: Extensible Queuing

    Kateryna Nezdolii, Tony Allen

    Two Envoy maintainers explain how the pending-request queue in Envoy's connection pools was factored out of core code into an extensible queue policy, and walk through FIFO, adaptive LIFO, CoDel and stochastic fair queuing. They show benchmark visualizations of each policy under a noisy-neighbor workload and say CoDel and stochastic fair queuing are still open pull requests.

    15 min slot · Level not listed · Session

    Networking and connectivity

    Watch this if: You operate Envoy for multiple tenants and want to understand what its new queue policies do during overload.

    1. 1:51 Kateryna Nezdolii says FIFO was part of Envoy's core code until recently and has been factored out as a dedicated extension so that more queueing policies can be supported.
    2. 6:17 She introduces adaptive LIFO, which she says is already merged: it serves requests FIFO in normal operation and switches to newest-first once the queue passes a high watermark, until depth falls below the low watermark.
    3. 8:49 She describes the CoDel proposal, an open pull request that measures how long the oldest request has waited against a target and interval, and may drop requests that wait more than twice the target.
    4. 11:19 Tony Allen compares benchmark runs and says adding CoDel to adaptive LIFO produced more errors but kept latencies under control after the noisy neighbor stopped.
    5. 13:46 He describes stochastic fair queuing, in review: a fixed number of queues chosen by a hash of the tenant, served round robin, with the hash perturbed about every second, which he says left well-behaved tenants unaffected in their test.
