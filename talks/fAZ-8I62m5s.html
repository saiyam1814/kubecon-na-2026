Talk summary

The presenters explain why multi-minute drug-discovery jobs led their team away from a general-purpose inference framework. They describe how stateless workers, durable queues, and native Kubernetes scheduling serve interactive and batch requests on the same fleet.

Watch this if:

Watch this if interactive inference and long-running batch jobs need to share the same pool of accelerators.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 6:01 The presenters explain why multi-minute accelerator jobs made durable requests and backlog-based scaling more useful to them than in-flight concurrency.
  2. 9:12 They describe how a stateless worker backed by an asynchronous queue became the core building block of the new platform.
  3. 16:29 They explain how KEDA wakes a deployment from zero replicas and passes queue signals to the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler for further scaling.
  4. 18:46 They walk through high-priority requests preempting batch workers and describe how queue leases let interrupted work become available again.
  5. 27:52 They describe dual-writing to Redis and Bigtable to verify parity before cutting over with a configuration flag they could flip back.
