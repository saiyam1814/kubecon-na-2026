Talk summary

Mirdul describes the contradiction between scarce GPU access and idle reserved capacity, framing hoarding as an organizational design problem rather than individual misconduct. He argues for fast self-service access, fair queueing and reclamation, cost visibility, and product-like metrics that expose wait time, idle time, and cost.

Watch this if:

your platform team is designing self-service access, fair sharing, and cost visibility for scarce GPU capacity.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:41 Mirdul says organizations simultaneously experience delayed AI projects because GPUs appear scarce and large amounts of reserved GPU capacity sitting idle.
  2. 1:52 Mirdul describes a scarcity and hoarding loop in which hard-to-get GPUs are retained even when idle, preventing other teams from using them.
  3. 5:07 Mirdul says fair sharing uses Kueue to prioritize GPU jobs and reclaim capacity from lower-priority or idle work so it returns to the pool.
  4. 5:38 Mirdul argues that making team-level GPU cost visible changes behavior because engineers can see when idle reservations are consuming meaningful budget.
  5. 6:31 Mirdul recommends keeping wait time, idle time, and cost visible to everyone as the operating metrics for treating GPU access as a product.
