Talk summary

Guangya Liu walks through end-to-end tracing and metric, trace and log correlation in llm-d, using a slow time-to-first-token case as a worked example. Nili Guy then describes early work combining llm-d metrics with OpenCost to estimate the cost per token for self-hosted models by tenant and hardware.

Watch this if:

You operate llm-d or similar inference stacks and want ideas for tracing requests and tracking GPU cost per token.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 1:06 Guangya Liu follows one request from Envoy through the llm-d router (EPP), the KV cache index and the prefill and decode pods to show why end-to-end tracing is needed.
  2. 3:50 He shows the correlation between metrics, traces and logs, with one click from a Grafana panel to the trace in the Jaeger UI.
  3. 4:43 He walks through debugging a P99 time-to-first-token alert, where the trace shows a request routed to a pod without the cached prefix.
  4. 6:57 He traces the root cause to a Helm release that changed a routing scorer weight from three to zero.
  5. 11:55 Nili Guy describes combining OpenCost with llm-d, vLLM and SGLang metrics in Prometheus to report cost per token at different granularities.
