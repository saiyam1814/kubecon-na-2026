Talk summary

Daniel Oh and fellow panelists introduce the CNCF Technical Advisory Group for Developer Experience, explaining its scope, its focus areas and initiatives such as a study of AI-assisted development in CNCF projects and the runtime conditions work. In an open discussion with the audience they talk about how to take part without being a chair or tech lead, how developers can find their way through the CNCF landscape, and where to find the meeting calendar and Slack channel.

Watch this if:

you care about developer experience in cloud native projects and want to know what the CNCF's developer experience group works on and how to join in.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 5:10 Daniel Oh says the group does not aim to create new CNCF projects but to work with existing ones on standards, guidelines and white papers, using the gap between platform engineers and developers around Backstage portals as an example.
  2. 9:02 Kevin Dubois describes the State of AI-Assisted Development initiative, which surveyed and interviewed CNCF project maintainers on how they use AI and produced a draft white paper in the TOC repository.
  3. 14:00 Dubois says the group also reviews projects moving to sandbox, incubating or graduated status for their developer experience angle, citing its review of Buildpacks for graduation.
  4. 15:10 Simon Forster describes joining as a platform architect through the runtime conditions initiative, which is about how applications state their intent to platforms, and says contributions of any size were welcomed.
  5. 23:18 Answering a Java developer overwhelmed by choices, Dubois says a use-case map of the landscape is hard to make without favoring projects, and the panel suggests asking maintainers to document how their projects fit together.
