Talk summary

Lorenzo Biava of LiveKit uses an earthquake scenario to show what real-time voice AI at scale demands and how LiveKit prepares its Kubernetes platform for data center and network failures. Jeff Hoffman of Oracle then describes how OKE and OCI cover reach, networking, compute and portable managed Kubernetes for workloads like LiveKit's.

Watch this if:

you run real-time voice or video AI on Kubernetes and want to hear how LiveKit plans for data center and network failures.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:30 Lorenzo Biava walks through an earthquake scenario where AI agents answer every emergency call while drones and robots search, which he puts at well over 100,000 real-time sessions on a half-second response budget.
  2. 2:25 He says over a million developers build on LiveKit's open source framework, and its hosted platform runs on 50,000 cores across 30+ Kubernetes clusters, mostly on Oracle, moving 120 petabytes of data.
  3. 3:27 He describes how they prepare: pre-warmed agents, share-nothing sites so losing one reroutes traffic, a real-time overlay that routes around bad links, and calls that move off a dying server, with live agent migration in progress.
  4. 4:24 Jeff Hoffman says OKE runs in 50+ OCI regions across 28 countries with one cluster spec, plus flat-priced networking with your own IP ranges and shapes up to bare metal and GPUs.
  5. 5:10 He says OKE is conformant and open to custom node images for tools like BuildKit and Kata, and that one in five CNCF projects, including Kubernetes, Cilium and OpenTelemetry, run tests, builds or live environments on OCI.
