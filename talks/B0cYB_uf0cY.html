Talk summary

Jorge Castro and Katherine Druckman give newcomers a guide to the CNCF landscape, covering what the CNCF is, the sandbox, incubating and graduated maturity levels, and a five-step way to evaluate a project. They show the landscape site and CLOMonitor live with Helm and Parsec, and say a low score or a red check can be read as a chance to contribute.

Watch this if:

you are new to KubeCon and want a way to find and evaluate CNCF projects without getting lost in the landscape.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 1:41 Jorge Castro says the CNCF built a landscape MCP server that people can run locally in a container so an AI agent can dig through projects and their contribution metrics.
  2. 4:19 Katherine Druckman explains the maturity levels, sandbox, incubating and graduated, and says some projects get archived as a normal part of project evolution.
  3. 7:51 She describes a basic method: define the problem, filter the landscape by category and maturity, look at project health and security audits, try the project in a development environment, then contribute back.
  4. 9:21 She introduces CLOMonitor as a report of checks on a project, and says a high score means something but should not be over-indexed on, and a low score is not necessarily disqualifying.
  5. 13:52 Jorge Castro shows a yellow Adopters check on Parsec and says adding your organization to a project's adopters file is an easy way to give back.
