Talk summary

Mitchell Davis of American Family Insurance describes how his team got application teams to adopt its Crossplane platform after building it. He says four things had to change: the API, the relationship with developers, the defaults, and how changes are shipped.

Watch this if:

you have built an internal platform on Crossplane and are now trying to get real teams to use it.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:26 Davis describes infrastructure at his company swinging from a central DevOps team with tickets, to forked Terraform modules owned by each team, and back to a central self-service platform.
  2. 1:20 He says the XRD is the contract developers see and the composition is what the platform team owns, and sums it up as the API being the product.
  3. 2:37 He gives Karpenter node disruption as an example: the default is nightly and teams can pick from presets, which he calls the illusion of choice.
  4. 3:03 He says the team swapped the ingress controller on about 30 EKS clusters with one composition change, and now rolls every change out by control plane tier with production last.
  5. 3:53 He reports 13 patterns across AWS and GCP, more than 160 Kubernetes clusters and more than 50 teams on the platform.
