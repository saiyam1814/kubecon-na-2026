Talk summary

Two engineers from Isomorphic Labs and a Kueue maintainer from Google recount a 10-day outage in which autoscaled inference deployments flooded a cluster with pending Kueue workloads. They walk through its three phases and causes, the upstream fixes in Kueue and Kubernetes, and the cluster, alerting and autoscaling changes they say they made afterward.

Watch this if:

You run autoscaled batch or inference workloads through Kueue and want to see how pending objects can take down scheduling.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 6:35 Jenny Vega says a flood of pending pods and workloads in February 2026 pushed a production cluster into a 10-day outage in three phases, ending with 20 hours of no scheduling.
  2. 8:14 Karol Szuster says the Kueue controller restarts came from one shared client-side rate-limit bucket that also covered leader election, so a storm of reconciles stopped the lease from being renewed.
  3. 9:52 Jenny Vega shows how unschedulable GPU replicas at the head of a shared queue kept admissible CPU work from being reached, and says the team split the queue while Kueue added scheduling equivalence hashing upstream.
  4. 14:38 The speakers explain that after a restart the API server had to call Kueue's conversion webhook for every workload to rebuild its watch cache, which could not finish within the five-minute compaction interval.
  5. 21:06 Cellan Hall says the team now feeds the admitted pod count back into the KEDA scaling trigger, and reports 80 percent fewer pending Kueue workloads and 40 percent fewer workload objects in etcd.
