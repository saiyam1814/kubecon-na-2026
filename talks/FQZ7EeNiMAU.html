Talk summary

John Kastner, a Cedar maintainer at Amazon Web Services, explains how partial evaluation lets Cedar authorize a request when some information is not yet known. He walks through an AI assistant that sends email, where he says the user is asked for approval only when the other policies cannot decide.

Watch this if:

you want an AI agent or service to ask for approval or fetch data only when the authorization decision needs it.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:48 John Kastner says Cedar is an authorization policy language designed around four principles: expressive, fast, safe and analyzable.
  2. 1:12 He sets up the use case: an AI assistant that sends email on your behalf, with something in the middle deciding when that is allowed and when a human needs to intervene.
  3. 1:41 He shows three policies: allow email to members of his team, never allow email with confidential information, and for any other case ask him for explicit approval.
  4. 2:50 He says partial evaluation lets the request leave the approval unknown, so an email to a teammate is allowed without asking the user, which he presents as a way to avoid decision fatigue.
  5. 4:44 He generalizes the pattern: give the partial authorizer what you know, and if it cannot decide, it returns residual policies that show which expensive information to fetch.
