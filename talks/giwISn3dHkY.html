Talk summary

The speakers, from LinkedIn's compute infrastructure team, describe how they define SLOs for their Kubernetes platform by starting from promises tied to each stage of a workload's life: rollout, scheduling, platform preparation, running and cleanup. They show how each promise becomes a scoped indicator and objective, with failures attributed to platform or application, and share incidents the SLOs surfaced and the questions they use to test an SLO.

Watch this if:

You run an internal Kubernetes platform and want a worked method for turning what users expect into SLOs that separate platform failures from application failures.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 5:57 Vasudev Bongale states the promises in plain terms, one per workload stage, such as not failing a rollout because of a platform issue and removing deleted pods in time.
  2. 9:52 Bongale explains the readiness contract: an application that fails on its own still meets it, an infrastructure failure breaches it, and an unknown reason also counts against the platform because missing diagnostics is the platform's to fix.
  3. 12:54 He says the rollout SLO showed healthy applications being marked failed by a static progress deadline, so the platform now gets its own preparation allowance and the deadline is calculated dynamically.
  4. 18:14 Swati Shridhar Nair describes a platform prep breach traced to an init container calling an external service with two slow hosts, and says a platform promise needs explicit contracts with the services it depends on.
  5. 21:25 Nair describes pods stuck terminating on healthy nodes, which they traced to a containerd bug that dropped container-stopped events under heavy load and fixed with a version bump.
