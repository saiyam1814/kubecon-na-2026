Talk summary

Erica Hughberg marks ten years of Envoy with a quick tour from its start at Lyft to what the project shipped in 2026. She covers HTTP/3 and QUIC, features for AI traffic, dynamic modules, Envoy Gateway and the renaming of Envoy AI Gateway to Agent Router.

Watch this if:

you use Envoy or a gateway built on it and want a five-minute catch-up on the 2026 changes.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:18 Erica Hughberg recalls that Envoy began at Lyft to connect a growing number of services, was open sourced in 2016 and graduated in 2018.
  2. 1:00 She says that in 2026 HTTP/3 and QUIC are stable in the core proxy, including upstream HTTP/3, along with post-quantum key exchange.
  3. 1:25 She says Envoy has added capabilities for what she calls the AI-native era, including MCP routing and LLM token usage.
  4. 1:49 She describes dynamic modules, which she says let you extend Envoy in Rust, Go or C++ without rebuilding all of Envoy or running code in an external process.
  5. 3:29 She says Envoy AI Gateway, announced in Salt Lake City two years ago, now goes by Agent Router and has moved to another foundation while staying part of the Envoy family.
