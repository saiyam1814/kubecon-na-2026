Talk summary

Phelipe Fabres, an AWS solutions architect presenting without the customer co-speaker he says could not attend, tells how a Brazilian legal-tech startup fine-tuned a vision language model for messy legal documents on 32 nodes with 256 GPUs on Amazon EKS. He organizes the story as four patterns he says they learned, starting every worker together, moving data fast between nodes, keeping every GPU fed and recovering from failures, with two escalations as examples.

Watch this if:

you plan multi-node GPU training on Kubernetes and want to hear what stalled one team's jobs and the patterns they adopted in response.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 2:04 Fabres opens with an escalation where instance checks on all 32 nodes passed, yet one overheating GPU stalled the whole distributed training job.
  2. 14:56 He describes gang admission, a pre-job that verifies every node is healthy before the job is submitted, because he says each hour of waiting costs GPU time across all 32 nodes.
  3. 17:46 He recounts a networking escalation in which performance dropped at batch transfers of 128 to 256 MB, traced to the NCCL, libfabric and Elastic Fabric Adapter layers inside the pod, and says tuning restored the same throughput at every size.
  4. 23:03 He explains the two data layers, object storage in Amazon S3 for prepared data and FSx for Lustre for checkpoints, copying only what each job needs to limit cost.
  5. 26:45 He outlines the recovery pattern: watch device health trends, block the node from new placements, wait for a compatible replacement and restart, and says the recovery job later grew into an agent.
