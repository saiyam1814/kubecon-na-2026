Talk summary

Akhil Mohan of VMware by Broadcom, a containerd maintainer, retraces how a CSI pod on an internal cluster sat in CreateContainerError for 19 hours after a hypervisor power failure while its containers were in fact running. He traces it to a VM that booted about eight minutes in the future, so tokens were issued in the future and kubelet's sorting of containers by creation time got stuck once chrony stepped the clock back, and he describes the fix of starting kubelet only after time sync.

Watch this if:

you operate Kubernetes nodes on virtual machines and want a worked example of debugging from a stuck pod down to the hardware clock.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:26 Mohan lays out the symptom: a CSI pod in CreateContainerError with over 5,000 retries failing on a container name reservation, while crictl on the node shows the same containers already running.
  2. 8:51 He finds kube-proxy rejected by the API server because its service account token is not valid yet, issued by a control plane VM whose clock was ahead of the other two.
  3. 10:08 Journal logs show the time jumping backwards 45 seconds after boot: he says the VM inherited a clock about eight minutes ahead from the ESXi host and chrony stepped it back in one go, after kubelet had already started.
  4. 18:55 He explains the loop: kubelet sorts container statuses by created-at wall clock time, so the attempt created on the future clock always looks newest and kubelet keeps trying to recreate an attempt that is already running.
  5. 21:36 The fix he describes is ordering the kubelet systemd unit after a chrony wait service with a 180 second timeout, chosen so a node boots slower instead of never booting when NTP is unavailable.
