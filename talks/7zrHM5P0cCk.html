Talk summary

Ronak Nathani describes how LinkedIn built a composable, multi-cluster workload API that treats the pod template, update strategy, persistence and placement as separate concerns instead of splitting workloads into stateless and stateful. He walks through the hub-and-spoke design, a demo of moving and coordinating workloads, and says the team plans to open source it.

Watch this if:

you design workload APIs for a platform that spans many clusters.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:25 Ronak says LinkedIn's fleet has half a million machines, more than 3,000 applications and about 5 million containers, with over 100,000 deployments a week.
  2. 3:41 He introduces three objects: an immutable revision, a workload with replica count, revision distribution and update strategy, and a workload placement that admins control.
  3. 5:30 He says LinkedIn runs one generic controller for all stateful workloads and asks each team to write a coordinator that must approve every change before the workload acts.
  4. 11:15 He says pod disruption budgets stop working across clusters, so they use workload disruption budgets with an eviction webhook in each spoke that checks the hub.
  5. 17:43 Asked about existing projects, he says they looked at Open Cluster Management and Karmada but wanted rollouts, disruptions and availability respected globally rather than per cluster.
