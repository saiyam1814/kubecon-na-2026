Talk summary

David Jumani, a kgateway maintainer, argues that the hard part of running a gateway in a large organization is letting many teams ship routes without a platform team reviewing everything or teams breaking each other. He presents kgateway's route delegation, inherited policies and strict validation, and cites user scale numbers and resource reductions he attributes to the 2.5 release.

Watch this if:

many teams share one gateway in your organization and you want them to own routes without breaking each other.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:48 Jumani says Gateway API reference grants define where a route can connect but cannot require a prefix or stop one route from overriding another.
  2. 1:08 Jumani explains route delegation in kgateway: a platform team creates a parent route with a prefix and policy and delegates the sub-routes under it to specific teams.
  3. 2:16 Jumani says strict validation rejects a route with bad configuration instead of letting Envoy NACK it, which he says could otherwise bring down the whole gateway on a restart.
  4. 3:00 Jumani says one user runs kgateway with 700,000 concurrent gRPC streams, 90,000 JWT validations a second, over 200 proxies and about 300,000 pods that churn 11 times a day.
  5. 3:26 Jumani says the 2.5 release cut CPU use by 70% at 5,000 routes, memory by 95% at 40,000 routes and proxy memory by 48%.
