Talk summary

Engin Diri of Pulumi recounts helping a European customer build a self-hosted inference platform, moving from whole GPUs and time slicing to NVIDIA MIG slices that pods request through Dynamic Resource Allocation. He shows a recorded demo of MIG profiles, resource slices and claims on an AWS node serving a model behind an inference gateway, and lists open questions such as quotas, models larger than a node and start-up ordering.

Watch this if:

you run inference on your own GPUs and want a practical walk through MIG slices requested with Dynamic Resource Allocation.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 8:32 Diri says time slicing gives no memory or fault isolation between workloads, so the choice was between wasting GPUs and risking a noisy neighbor.
  2. 11:24 Diri says Dynamic Resource Allocation, which he dates to Kubernetes 1.34, lets a request describe device attributes instead of an integer count.
  3. 22:16 In the recorded demo, Diri lists the 1G, 2G and 4G MIG profiles on the node and the resource slices the DRA driver published.
  4. 24:28 Diri lists open day-two questions he is evaluating with the customer, including Kueue for MIG quota and which workload runs first.
  5. 28:06 Diri advises starting the DRA driver only after MIG is configured, because he ran into race conditions between the two.
