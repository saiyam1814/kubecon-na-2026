Talk summary

Justin O'Leary says he took over cloud environments from ordinary namespace access by asking cloud operators to act with their own credentials, and demos what he describes as the same confused-deputy flaw in Config Connector, Azure Service Operator, AWS Controllers for Kubernetes and Crossplane. He recounts how each vendor or project responded to his reports and closes with questions for people who run operators and changes for people who write them.

Watch this if:

you run cloud-resource operators in multi-tenant clusters and want to see how namespace users might reach cloud IAM through them.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 2:45 Justin's first demo applies an IAMPolicyMember resource aimed at a whole GCP organization, and he says Config Connector made a namespace developer organization owner in under a minute.
  2. 10:31 Justin names the pattern a confused deputy, citing Norm Hardy's 1988 description, and lists three ingredients: a field the user fills in, a powerful credential in the operator, and no check on who is asking.
  3. 15:02 Justin demos what he calls trust policy injection with AWS Controllers for Kubernetes, putting an outside AWS account on a role's trust policy, and says the cloud logs attribute the change to the operator.
  4. 23:17 Justin goes through common mitigations such as policy engines, narrower operator permissions, cloud guardrails, logs and OIDC, and argues none of them makes the operator check whether the requester is allowed.
  5. 26:15 Justin's closing advice for operator authors is to verify the caller with a SubjectAccessReview at admission, authorize references instead of trusting raw strings, and fail closed.
