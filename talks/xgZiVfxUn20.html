Talk summary

The speaker, who says she was a social worker and forensic psychologist before moving into tech, argues that explanations of infrastructure fail when implementation details come before shared mental models and relevance. She introduces communication debt, the curse of knowledge and an abstraction ladder, then rewrites one Kubernetes incident update for a product manager, a non-Kubernetes engineer, a new engineer and a non-technical teammate.

Watch this if:

You explain Kubernetes incidents or platform work to product managers, executives or engineers from other areas and keep losing them.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 2:06 The speaker defines communication debt as the long-term cost of people leaving a conversation believing they share an understanding, and suggests it may cost more than technical debt because it is harder to see.
  2. 7:19 She shows pictures of grapes, stars, crab, servers and eyelashes to make the point that 'cluster' means different things to listeners who do not share an engineer's mental model.
  3. 8:07 The speaker explains the curse of knowledge, comparing expertise to a zip file: once something is learned, it is hard to remember what not knowing it was like.
  4. 11:35 She introduces the abstraction ladder, with engineers on the bottom rung talking mechanics, then cause for product managers, behavior for technical managers and impact for executives and customers.
  5. 14:17 The speaker reads a jargon-heavy update about a failed rollout and then restates it in plain words, before showing versions tailored to four different audiences.
