Talk summary

The SIG Docs co-chairs describe how Kubernetes documentation is localized into 17 languages and say raw AI-generated translation pull requests do not meet their quality bar. In a recorded segment, localization lead Seokho Son presents a tool that flags outdated pages by document structure and per-language AGENTS.md files that give AI a team's glossary and style rules, with human review still required.

Watch this if:

you contribute to Kubernetes docs or translations and want to know where the maintainers accept AI help and where they do not.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 10:24 Natali Vlatko says unreviewed AI-generated contributions are a challenge across the Kubernetes organization and that contributors are expected to understand and be accountable for what they submit.
  2. 15:24 Divya Mohan says that for 11 of the 16 active localizations under 20% of the docs are translated, and that over 2,000 localized documents are outdated.
  3. 17:37 She says the team received around 320 AI-generated pull requests in two weeks and that relying on manual effort alone has reached its limit.
  4. 19:31 In a recorded segment, Seokho Son introduces a structural signal triage tool that compares headings, code blocks, shortcodes and links instead of commit dates to rate how outdated a localized page is.
  5. 20:25 He proposes language-specific AGENTS.md files holding a team's glossary, style guide and policies as reusable AI context, with humans still verifying output and disclosing AI use in pull requests.
