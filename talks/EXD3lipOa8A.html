Talk summary

Arka Saha, an etcd-operator contributor, recaps the operator's releases and explains why version 0.3 replaced StatefulSets with a custom controller. He describes how the new design replaces a corrupted member, a new option to pause reconciliation, and what is planned next.

Watch this if:

you operate etcd clusters on Kubernetes and want to know how the official operator now handles failed members.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:20 Arka Saha recaps the releases: 0.1 could create and scale an etcd cluster, 0.2 added TLS support, and 0.3 replaced the StatefulSet with the project's own custom controller.
  2. 1:11 He explains the StatefulSet problem: replacing a corrupted second member of a three-member cluster would mean scaling down, which he says also removes a healthy member.
  3. 2:29 He describes the new design as two CRDs: the etcd cluster the user defines, which manages etcd member resources that in turn manage the pods and PVCs.
  4. 2:54 He walks through recovery of a corrupted member: remove the member, delete its pod and PVC, start a new pod with a fresh PVC, add it as a learner and wait for it to become a voter.
  5. 3:42 He says users can now pause reconciliation to fix a stuck upgrade by hand, and names alarm-driven reconciliation, repair, and backup and restore in 0.4 as upcoming work.
