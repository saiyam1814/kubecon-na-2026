Talk summary

Leslie Pascual of ActiveState presents survey results on how teams take in open source dependencies and how much rework she says AI-assisted development creates. She proposes five dependency metrics, a governed package intake workflow and a 30-day plan for starting to measure them.

Watch this if:

Watch this if you want concrete metrics and a business case for treating open source dependency intake as a platform capability.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 3:15 Pascual says her figures come from a survey report her company paid for and an outside research firm ran: 300 leaders at organizations with over 1,000 employees, plus 10 practitioner interviews.
  2. 4:49 She cites the report's lower bound of 139 hours per engineer per year spent fixing AI-assisted development work, which she puts at about seven full-time engineers per 100.
  3. 12:47 She proposes five metrics from data teams already have: dependency-blocked deploys, remediation lead time measured from the upstream fix, unplanned dependency work, ungoverned intake rate and dependency build breaks.
  4. 18:02 She walks through one package's life in a governed intake model: request, policy checks, approve into an internal source, CI pulls only from that source, remediate, and the app team promotes the new version.
  5. 22:17 She suggests a 30-day start: label dependency work in week one, let the counts build up in weeks two to four, then price it for leadership.
