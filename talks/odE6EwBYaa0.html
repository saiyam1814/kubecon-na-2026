Talk summary

The speaker gives an overview of how he sees retail moving from automated processes to agents that make pricing, supply chain and customer decisions with few human touch points. He says the main challenge is governance, and calls for traceability, bias monitoring, human-in-the-loop checkpoints and a kill switch, plus open standards so multi-vendor agents can work together.

Watch this if:

You want a high-level view of where a retail technology manager sees agentic AI going and which governance questions he thinks platform teams need to answer.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 9:15 The speaker describes four maturity levels, from rule-based automation through predictive and adaptive decisioning to autonomous decisioning, and says most retailers are at level three.
  2. 10:45 He gives examples of agentic AI in retail: autonomous pricing engines that watch competitor prices and margin targets, self-healing supply chain pipelines and conversational commerce agents.
  3. 16:51 He describes an architecture where point-of-sale, clickstream and external feeds go into a unified intelligence layer of LLMs and agents on Kubernetes clusters, backed by a unified data layer of governed data.
  4. 19:05 He says it is hard to justify a claim that an agent raised revenue by 5% without complete traceability of what the agents did.
  5. 23:22 He says a trusted governance framework should always include a kill switch, with override controls, regular governance reviews and an AI council before production deployment.
