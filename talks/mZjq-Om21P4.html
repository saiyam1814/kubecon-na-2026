Talk summary

Simon Forster and Danielle Cook give first-time attendees an overview of KubeCon: who attends, where learning happens, and what exists outside the schedule. They advise starting small with conversations, picking sessions by the problem you have, and using quiet rooms and other wellbeing spaces when the event gets overwhelming.

Watch this if:

You are new to KubeCon and want a quick orientation on how the event works beyond the session list.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:20 Simon Forster says making connections is one of the most important things to do at KubeCon, and suggests starting small and opening up groups so others can join.
  2. 1:56 He lists where learning happens, from keynotes and sessions to the learning lounge, capture the flag, the poster showcase and ContribFest, and suggests picking sessions based on a problem you are experiencing.
  3. 4:06 Danielle Cook describes the Project Pavilion as the place to meet maintainers, and explains the sandbox, incubating and graduated project levels.
  4. 5:04 She describes the hallway track and says that since sessions are recorded, missing one to make a connection is a good thing.
  5. 5:52 Simon Forster says KubeCon can be overwhelming and points to quiet rooms and other spaces to recharge, lanyard stickers that signal how open someone is to talking, and the code of conduct.
