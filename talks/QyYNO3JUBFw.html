Talk summary

Shivangi Motwani of Skydance shares two stories where user complaints and support tickets pointed to problems in how the platform presented itself. She closes with principles from user design, such as helping users recover from errors and giving them visibility after they act.

Watch this if:

Watch this if you want a 6-minute look at the user signals hidden in platform support tickets and how to design errors and feedback around them.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:32 Motwani says her team learned to treat piles of similar 'user error' tickets as a signal that the platform's interface might be wrong.
  2. 2:05 In her first story, users copied a high-resource example from the docs and asked for more resources than they needed, and she says docs that show what is possible rather than what is reasonable can be costly.
  3. 3:41 She says failing builds to block costly mistakes led to complaints, and things improved only when the team added defaults, hints and messages explaining why a build failed and how to recover.
  4. 4:12 Her second story is about cases where silence looks like success: users assume accepted intent is live while dependencies delay or fail provisioning, so she suggests telling them what happened and what to do next.
  5. 5:48 She closes with principles taken from user design: prevent costly mistakes, help users recognize and recover from errors, give visibility after actions, and keep docs, examples and hints consistent.
