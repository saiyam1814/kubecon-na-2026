Talk summary

Neil Cresswell presents KubeSchool, an online set of plain-English lessons he wrote to explain how Kubernetes works, which he says began as material for one of his own engineers. He argues that official training material and exam cramming leave people certified but missing fundamentals, and says the lessons are meant to fill those gaps.

Watch this if:

You are new to Kubernetes, or learned it by memorizing commands, and want to know whether a plain-English course is worth a day of your time.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 1:10 The speaker says people get lost because the official training material assumes knowledge of containers, Linux, networking, DNS and storage, and that even he finds it overwhelming.
  2. 3:08 He argues there is a big difference between passing an exam and understanding how to build and run a platform.
  3. 3:29 He describes KubeSchool as an explainer in plain English, not a certification, exam cram or hands-on lab, and contrasts his one-line description of a Deployment with the technical spec.
  4. 5:05 He says the tracks start with Kubernetes internals and add observability and GitOps, and that a final lesson covers architecting a platform around what its users need.
  5. 9:12 He says the material started when one of his engineers told him he was lost in Kubernetes training, and he decided to publish what he wrote for him.
