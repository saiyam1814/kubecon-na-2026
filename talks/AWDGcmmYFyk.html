Talk summary

Chris Plank and Colin Griffin introduce the Platform Engineering User Group's resources and invite attendees to try its maturity self-assessment. The discussion turns to what platform teams should own and what they can measure when AI makes activity counts harder to interpret.

Watch this if:

Watch this if you want questions to ask about what your internal platform owns and how well it serves its users.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 2:43 The speakers point to three resources from the user group: the platform engineering maturity model, the platforms white paper, and the self-assessment.
  2. 6:36 A speaker says a platform can cover only part of the stack and warns that a small team can become a bottleneck if it takes on every responsibility itself.
  3. 12:51 A speaker describes using the self-assessment's spider diagram to spot an area worth investigating instead of assuming that deployment is the bottleneck.
  4. 17:10 An attendee asks how teams measure platform success when AI can generate a large pull request in minutes and make its size less useful as a signal.
  5. 24:08 A speaker describes looking at platform usage, documentation activity, and how long it takes to deliver features requested in Jira rather than counting lines of code.
