Talk summary

William Morgan says running open-weight models on Kubernetes shifts inference costs from buying tokens to operating GPU capacity. He presents Kinference as a layer for defining tenant policy, tracking usage, and routing requests to local or external backends.

Watch this if:

You run a Kubernetes platform and need to share inference capacity across teams without giving up visibility or control.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 12:01 Morgan explains that choosing an open-weight model variant depends on available memory, context length, and expected concurrency, not just the model family.
  2. 14:39 Morgan frames shared GPU inference as a platform problem: teams want self-service and different levels of service while operators must keep finite capacity reliable.
  3. 18:12 Morgan describes putting a traffic enforcement gateway in front of model servers to handle multiple models and tenants with explicit policy.
  4. 21:01 Morgan says the gateway can export Prometheus-compatible metrics for existing dashboards and break out performance by model and tenant.
  5. 23:47 Morgan walks through policies that can choose a model, reject a request, or send it to an external provider using prompt, GPU, and tenant signals.
