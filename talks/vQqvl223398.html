Talk summary

Five panelists who created or teach around the Certified Kubernetes Network Engineer (CKNE) exam discuss why CNCF added a hands-on networking certification and what foundational networking knowledge they think Kubernetes engineers need. They argue that AI tools can speed up troubleshooting but that an engineer still has to judge the suggested fix, and they describe what the exam covers and how to prepare.

Watch this if:

you are deciding whether to study for the CKNE exam, or you come from traditional networking and want to know how that knowledge carries over to Kubernetes.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 4:10 Sauthier says CKNE is a separate hands-on exam, not an extension of CKA, because practice builds instincts for a 3 a.m. incident and he wants common ground between the older NetOps world and the cloud native one.
  2. 10:20 Spurin says network policy lets you ignore iptables, nftables and eBPF until something breaks, and argues that without that low-level knowledge a person can only approve whatever an AI tool concludes.
  3. 15:34 Kundattil gives the example of an AI tool suggesting to disable mTLS to restore pod-to-pod connectivity in a service mesh, which she calls not the right fix.
  4. 20:11 Sauthier says some exam questions name the project to use while others leave the choice open, for example Cilium or Istio, and Wijay adds that candidates need subnetting, routing and BGP fundamentals and must read a tcpdump.
  5. 28:36 Asked whether CKNE will count toward Kubestronaut, Sauthier says it becomes a requirement for both Kubestronaut and Golden Kubestronaut from February 15.
