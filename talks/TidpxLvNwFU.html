Talk summary

Andy Anderson of IBM Research presents Hive Commons, which he says is now a CNCF sandbox project that helps repositories build AI agent co-maintainers they control. He describes what Hive does, its three founding principles, and a contributor relay called Clanker that hands tasks to outside agents.

Watch this if:

You maintain an open source project and are curious how one maintainer delegates triage and fixes to AI agents.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 0:52 Andy Anderson describes Hive as your judgment multiplied by agents backed by proof, a fleet of co-maintainers that answer to him.
  2. 1:37 He says Hive finds and triages project work, coordinates AI agents on your own tools, accounts and entitlements, reviews changes, enforces project rules and retains project knowledge.
  3. 2:19 He describes Clanker as a contributor relay that lets contributors do work for a project without having its GitHub credentials or being a recognized contributor.
  4. 2:47 He names three founding principles: many short-lived agents, external project context given per task, and deterministic bounds in a hybrid of determinism and non-determinism.
  5. 3:57 He says work that fails CI or review goes back to the Clanker to redo, that Hive finishes the work if the Clanker goes offline, and that a Clanker that breaches a security measure can be quarantined and its work taken over.
