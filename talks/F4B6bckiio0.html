Talk summary

Matt Lord introduces Vitess as a way to spread a MySQL workload across many servers while applications still see a single MySQL server, and walks through its components. Florent Poinsard then explains the MoveTables and Reshard workflows and runs a live demo that splits one shard into two under load on Kubernetes.

Watch this if:

your MySQL database is outgrowing a single instance and you want to see what sharding it with Vitess on Kubernetes looks like.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 2:46 Matt Lord says Vitess lets you distribute the workload of one MySQL instance across hundreds or thousands of MySQL servers while users still see a single server.
  2. 6:52 Matt Lord walks through the architecture: stateless VTGates that speak the MySQL protocol, VTTablets in front of each mysqld, the topology server, vtctld and VTOrc.
  3. 16:09 Florent Poinsard explains the MoveTables workflow: copy the data, keep streaming changes, switch reads and writes, and revert through reverse replication if needed.
  4. 21:02 Florent Poinsard starts the demo: a Vitess cluster on Kubernetes with the open source Vitess Operator on his laptop, with load pushing the single shard's CPU to 100%.
  5. 25:12 After completing the Reshard workflow, Florent Poinsard reports the demo going from about 7 to 8K QPS to 10 to 11K, with latency moving from about three milliseconds to two.
