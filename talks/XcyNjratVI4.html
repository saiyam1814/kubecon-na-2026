Talk summary

Zixuan Feng presents a mixed-method study of what he calls AI-DDoS, where a flood of plausible AI-generated contributions overwhelms a community's capacity to review them. He reports repository-mining and survey results on how contribution patterns and maintainer responses changed, and argues that review and repository-management mechanisms need to change rather than only defend.

Watch this if:

you maintain an open source project and want data on how AI-generated pull requests are changing contribution patterns and maintainer responses.

Key points from the speaker

  1. 3:45 Zixuan defines AI-DDoS by analogy with the security term, as a denial-of-service effect where plausible AI-generated contributions overwhelm a community's review capacity.
  2. 5:15 Zixuan describes the method: mining 294 repositories with more than 5,000 stars each, interviewing 11 maintainers, and validating the findings with over 200 surveyed maintainers.
  3. 9:01 Zixuan reports that 2025 pull request volume was significantly higher than his causal impact model predicted while issue volume was lower, and that one-time contributors' merge rate was about 18% below prediction.
  4. 12:46 Zixuan says more than 70% of surveyed maintainers chose preservative strategies such as tighter quality gates, about half chose adaptive ones, and transformative ideas such as reviewing intent or specs got low agreement.
  5. 17:15 Zixuan says a preliminary model flagged spam contributors from their history with about 85% accuracy, but he stopped the project over the risk of wrongly accusing the rest.
